Biology affects almost every part of human life. In medicine, knowledge of cell signalling, immunity and genetics underlies the diagnosis of disease and the design of drugs and vaccines. Antibiotic treatment depends on understanding how bacteria grow and evolve resistance, and cancer treatment depends on understanding how cells lose control of division. Medical practice is therefore a continuous application of biological research.

Food production is another area where biology is central. Crop breeding, soil microbiology and pest management all draw on knowledge of plant physiology, genetics and ecology. Fermentation, which has been used for bread, cheese and beer for thousands of years, is now studied at the molecular level to improve yields and reduce waste. Alberts et al. (2022) describe how the same cellular mechanisms that govern growth in a single cell also govern the productivity of a crop or a microbial culture.

The environment is the third field of application. Conservation decisions depend on understanding how species interact, how populations respond to change, and how ecosystems recover from disturbance. Monitoring of water quality, air quality and biodiversity relies on biological indicators, and biologists advise on how land and resources should be managed.

Biology also influences decisions that are not obviously biological. Public health policy depends on understanding transmission and immunity. Debates about genetic testing, gene editing and the use of animals in research raise questions that require a working understanding of the science. Dobzhansky (1973) argued that the unifying framework of evolution makes biology coherent, and the same coherence helps non-specialists judge the claims they encounter.

The importance of biology depends on the reliability of its knowledge. Applications built on weak evidence can cause harm, and applications built on sound evidence can save lives and protect ecosystems. Learning biology is therefore partly learning how to tell which claims have been tested and which have not.
