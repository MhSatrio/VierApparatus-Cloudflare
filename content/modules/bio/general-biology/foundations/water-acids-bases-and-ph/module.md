# Water, Acids, Bases and pH

Water makes up roughly seventy per cent of the mass of most cells, and nearly every biochemical reaction takes place in it. Its molecular properties are not incidental. They determine which molecules dissolve, how proteins fold, how membranes form, and how the concentration of hydrogen ions is controlled. Acids and bases are defined by their effect on that concentration, and the pH scale is the convenient way of expressing it.

The module begins with the structure of the water molecule and the hydrogen bonds that link water molecules together. It then considers water as a solvent, the distinction between molecules that dissolve in water and those that do not, and the self-ionisation of water that produces the hydrogen ion concentration on which pH depends. The later chapters cover acids and bases, buffers that keep pH stable, and the way cells use water and pH control in practice.

The subject connects to later modules in the curriculum. Enzyme activity, membrane transport and the folding of proteins all depend on pH and on the way water interacts with the molecules involved. Readers who understand this module will find many later results easier to interpret.

The chapters use the logarithmic scale of pH and give the equations needed to calculate it. Where a number is given, it is explained in terms of what it means for a biological system.
