Evolution is supported by several independent lines of evidence. The fossil record shows that organisms have changed over geological time, with older rocks containing forms that differ from those in younger rocks. Transitional fossils, which show features intermediate between major groups, are found in several lineages, and they document the appearance of new features in sequence.

Comparative anatomy provides evidence through homology, which is similarity of structure inherited from a common ancestor. Dobzhansky (1973) argued that the structures of organisms make sense only in light of their history, and the homologous bones of vertebrate limbs are a classic example. Comparative embryology adds evidence, because the early development of related species often shares stages that are modified in adults.

Molecular data give some of the strongest evidence. The DNA and protein sequences of organisms can be compared, and the patterns of similarity match the patterns of relationship inferred from anatomy and fossils. Genes that encode the same function in different species often share sequence features that reveal their common origin. The consistency of these patterns across many independent genes is what makes the evidence persuasive.

Direct observation shows evolution happening in real time. Populations of bacteria and insects have been followed through many generations under controlled conditions, and changes in their traits and genomes have been recorded. Antibiotic resistance and pesticide resistance in pests are everyday examples of evolution that can be observed within a few years.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe how these lines of evidence converge. Each one was gathered with different methods and by different investigators, and the agreement among them is the main reason evolution is accepted as a scientific explanation.

The strength of the evidence lies in its independence. A single line of evidence could be mistaken, but a conclusion supported by fossils, anatomy, molecules and observation is much harder to overturn.
