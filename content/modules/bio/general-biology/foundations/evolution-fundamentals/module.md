# Evolution Fundamentals

Evolution is the change in heritable characteristics of populations over generations. It is the process that connects the cells, organisms and ecosystems described in the earlier modules, and it is the framework within which the rest of biology makes sense. Darwin (1859) gave the first comprehensive account of it, and Dobzhansky (1973) summarised why the framework matters to every branch of the subject.

This module covers the mechanisms that drive evolution. The first chapters describe the raw material, heritable variation, and the process of natural selection that sorts it. Later chapters describe genetic drift, mutation, gene flow and speciation, which is the origin of new species. The module ends with common descent and the evidence for it, and with an example of evolution in practice that many readers will recognise from medicine.

The module treats evolution as a set of processes that can be measured, not as a story to be accepted on authority. Each chapter gives the logic of the mechanism and the kind of data used to test it.

Readers should work through the chapters in order. The idea of allele frequency, introduced in the first chapter, is used throughout the module.
