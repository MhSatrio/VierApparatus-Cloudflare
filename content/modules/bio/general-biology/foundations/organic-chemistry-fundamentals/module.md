# Organic Chemistry Fundamentals

Organic chemistry is the chemistry of carbon compounds, and nearly every molecule in a living cell is organic. Its vocabulary is the vocabulary of biology: the functional groups that give molecules their reactivity, the isomers that share a formula but differ in arrangement, and the energy carriers that link metabolism to every other process. This module introduces that vocabulary in the order a student needs it to read a biochemical pathway.

The module starts with carbon and its skeletons, then describes the functional groups that appear most often in biology. It explains isomerism, which matters because the arrangement of atoms can change biological activity completely. It covers the hydrocarbon chains of fats, the alcohols, aldehydes and ketones of sugars, the carboxylic acids and amines of amino acids, and the phosphate groups that store energy in ATP. The last chapter asks how organic molecules could have arisen before life, which connects the chemistry to the origin of biology.

Naming conventions are kept to the minimum needed. The aim is to recognise structures and predict their reactions, and the chapters explain each name in terms of the group it describes.

The module assumes the chemistry of bonds and energy from Chemistry for Biology. It prepares the reader for the metabolic modules, in which these groups are transformed one reaction at a time.
