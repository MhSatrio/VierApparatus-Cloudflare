# Chemistry for Biology

Biology is chemistry organised by natural selection. Every living process, from the copying of DNA to the contraction of a muscle, is a set of chemical reactions that obey the same rules as reactions in a laboratory flask. This module gives the chemistry needed to follow the rest of the curriculum. It does not assume prior study of chemistry. It introduces each idea through the biological system where it matters most.

The module begins with atomic structure and the electron arrangement that determines bonding. It then describes the three kinds of bond that hold biological molecules together and apart, the energetics of reactions, the speed at which they proceed and the role of catalysts, and finally the concentration of solutions, which is how biologists express the amount of a substance in a cell or a sample.

The chapters are written to be read in order. Concepts introduced early, such as the electron shell and the covalent bond, return in later chapters, and the reference list points to the primary sources for the claims that the chapters make.

Readers should expect to meet some numbers. They are kept to the level needed to understand biological reasoning, and each number is given with its meaning. The aim is not to memorise values but to know roughly how large an effect is and why it matters.
