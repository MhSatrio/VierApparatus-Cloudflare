An ion is an atom or molecule that carries a net electrical charge because it has gained or lost electrons. Sodium loses one electron to become Na+, and chlorine gains one to become Cl-. The two oppositely charged ions attract each other by electrostatic force, and this attraction is an ionic bond. Table salt is a crystal of sodium and chloride ions held together in this way.

In a cell, ions are rarely held in a solid. They are dissolved in water, where each ion is surrounded by a shell of water molecules that orient their oxygen or hydrogen toward it. The hydration shell weakens the attraction between ions, so that ionic interactions in water are much weaker than in a dry crystal. Ball (2008) emphasised that the solvent is an active participant in the chemistry of cells, and hydration is one of the ways in which water changes the behaviour of ions.

The most important biological use of ions is in the electrical potential across the plasma membrane. Cells pump sodium ions out and potassium ions in, so the concentration of sodium is much higher outside the cell than inside, and that of potassium is higher inside. The membrane is far more permeable to potassium than to sodium at rest, so potassium leaks out and leaves the inside of the cell negatively charged. This potential difference is the basis of nerve impulses and of the contraction of muscle.

Ion gradients store energy, in the same way that a dammed river stores energy in the height of its water. When sodium flows back into the cell through a channel, the energy released can drive other processes, such as the transport of nutrients. Alberts et al. (2022) describe how these gradients connect transport, signalling and energy conversion in cells.

Ions are measured by electrodes that sense their concentration, by fluorescent indicators that change brightness when a specific ion binds, and by atomic absorption and mass spectrometry in extracts. Each method has a different sensitivity and a different spatial resolution, so the choice depends on whether the question concerns the whole cell or a small compartment within it.
