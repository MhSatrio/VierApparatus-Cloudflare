# Ecology Fundamentals

Ecology is the study of how organisms interact with one another and with their physical environment. It asks what determines where species live, how many of them there are, how they are affected by each other, and how the whole system responds to disturbance. Ecology connects the cell and the organism to the biosphere, and it is the branch of biology most directly concerned with the conditions on which human society depends.

The module begins with the flow of energy and the cycling of nutrients, which set the limits of what an ecosystem can support. It then covers population growth, the interactions between species, food webs and their stability, ecological succession, and the human impact on ecosystems. The module treats each topic through its mechanisms and the evidence used to study it.

Ecology is an observational and experimental science, and many of its questions cannot be settled in a laboratory. The chapters explain the methods used in the field, the statistical problems they raise and the conclusions that are robust across studies.

Readers should expect the concepts of this module to be used in the Ecology and Environmental Biology specialization and in the Conservation Biology module.
