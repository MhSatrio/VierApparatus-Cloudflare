# Introduction to Cells

The cell is the smallest unit of living matter that can carry out all the processes of life on its own. A single bacterium is one cell. A human body contains trillions of cells that cooperate. This module introduces the cell in general terms before the later modules examine its structure, chemistry and behaviour in detail.

The eight chapters cover what cells have in common: a boundary membrane, genetic material, a cytoplasm in which chemistry takes place, a supply of energy, a way of dividing to make new cells, and a size that is limited by how materials move inside them. The module then places cells within the tree of life, which contains the three domains of archaea, bacteria and eukaryotes.

Cells differ in many ways, but the shared features are more important for a beginner than the differences. Learning them first makes the later modules on prokaryotes, eukaryotes, microscopy and cell biology much easier to follow.

Each chapter ends with the sources that support its claims, and the chapters can be read in order or on their own.
