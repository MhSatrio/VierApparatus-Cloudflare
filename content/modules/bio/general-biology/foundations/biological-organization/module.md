# Biological Organization

Living things are arranged in levels. Atoms combine into molecules, molecules assemble into structures, structures are organised into cells, cells form tissues, and so on up to the populations, communities and ecosystems that make up the biosphere. Each level is built from the one below, but each also has properties that cannot be read directly from its parts. Understanding the levels is the basis for choosing the right method for a biological question.

This module goes through the levels in order. The first chapter covers atoms and molecules, which is the chemical foundation for everything else. The second covers the assemblies of macromolecules, and the third covers organelles inside cells. The fourth discusses cells and their types, the fifth covers tissues and organs, and the sixth covers organ systems and the whole organism. The last two chapters move beyond the individual to populations, communities, ecosystems and the biosphere.

A recurring theme is emergence. A single neuron, a single water molecule or a single cell does not show the behaviour of the system to which it belongs. The property of the system arises from the interaction of its parts, and studying the parts alone does not always reveal it. Hooke (1665) saw cells for the first time with a microscope, and a century of work was needed to understand how cells combine into the organisms that interest most biologists.

The module asks the reader to think about which level is most informative for a given question, and what can be lost by studying a single level in isolation.
