# Characteristics of Life

This module examines the properties that distinguish living systems from non-living ones. It is a topic in the Foundations level, so each chapter assumes only the content of the introductory module. The aim is to show that the properties are not a checklist to memorise but a set of measurable features, each with its own methods, its own exceptions and its own open questions.

The module moves through the properties in an order that follows a single living cell. It starts with what a definition of life must do, then considers organisation and order, the use of energy, growth and development, reproduction and heredity, response to the environment, the maintenance of internal conditions, and adaptation over generations. Each chapter names the evidence used to study its property and gives at least one case where the property is hard to apply.

Readers should keep one question in mind throughout: what observation would show that a system is alive, and what observation would show that it is not? Biologists have answered that question differently for viruses, for seeds that have stopped growing, and for engineered cells. The module treats these disputes as part of the subject rather than as errors to be corrected.

The chapters are short. Each one can be read in one sitting, and the reference list at the end of each gives the sources needed for the next level of detail.
