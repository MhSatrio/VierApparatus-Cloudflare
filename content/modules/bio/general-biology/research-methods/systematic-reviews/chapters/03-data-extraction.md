Data extraction records from each included study the information needed for the synthesis. The extraction form is designed in advance and lists the population, the intervention or exposure, the comparator, the outcomes, the measures of effect and their precision, and the sample sizes. Each variable should be defined so that two extractors would record it in the same way, and the form should be tested on a few studies before it is used.

Two reviewers should extract data independently, and their extractions should be compared. Errors in extraction are common, and independent extraction catches many of them. Discrepancies are resolved by checking the original publication, and the resolution should be recorded. Page et al. (2021) required the number of extractors and the method of resolution to be reported.

Some studies report insufficient data, such as a mean without a measure of spread or a result without its sample size. The reviewer may contact the authors or calculate the missing values from other reported statistics, and the calculation must be transparent. Results reported on different scales must be converted to a common metric, and the conversion must be described, since different conversions can give different answers.

The measures of precision, including the standard errors, the confidence intervals and the sample sizes, must be extracted with the effects, because the synthesis depends on them. Cumming (2014) argues that the interval is the essential information for combining studies, and Peng (2011) argued that the extracted data should be deposited so that the synthesis can be repeated. Ioannidis (2005) described how a single error in extraction can change a conclusion, so the values should be checked against the originals.
