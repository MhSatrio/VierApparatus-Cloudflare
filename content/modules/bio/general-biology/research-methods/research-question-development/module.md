Every research project begins with a question, and the quality of the question determines much of what the project can achieve. A broad interest such as the role of the microbiome in health cannot be investigated directly, and it must be narrowed to a question that states what is being compared, in which population and with what outcome. Munafò et al. (2017) argued in their manifesto for reproducible science that the question and its analysis should be fixed in advance, since a question that shifts to fit the data produces findings that do not hold.

The topic begins with the move from a topic to a question. The question must be precise enough to be answered, and it must be capable of a negative answer, since a question that can only be confirmed is not a test. The later chapters examine feasibility, hypotheses, variables, pre-registration, ethics, pilot work and the reporting of the question.

Ioannidis (2005) described how the flexibility of a question and its analysis allows the investigator to find a result that is not real, and the defence is to fix the question before the data are examined. Cumming (2014) argues that the question should be framed in terms of the size of the effect and its precision rather than only as a yes or no test.

Sandve et al. (2013) and Peng (2011) recommended recording the question, the plan and any changes made to them, so that the course of the research can be traced and its results judged.
