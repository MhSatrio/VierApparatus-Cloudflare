Ethics is practised in the daily work of a research group rather than only at the point of approval. A group that considers the ethical implications of a study from its first draft is far less likely to find, late in the project, that a procedure needs approval it does not have or that a consent form does not cover the planned use of the data. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that ethical and methodological planning belong together, and the same care that improves the design improves the ethics.

A register of approvals, consents and amendments gives the group a record that can be checked. The register states which approval covers which study, the date of each amendment and the consent under which each dataset was collected. Peng (2011) argued that the reproducibility of research depends on a complete record of what was done, and the register provides the same completeness for the ethical conduct of the work.

Every member of the team should be trained in the principles and the procedures, since an ethical lapse by one member affects the whole group. Training should cover consent, data protection, animal welfare where relevant and the reporting of adverse events. Members must also feel able to raise an ethical concern early, without fear of reprisal, and the group should treat such concerns as a contribution rather than a complaint.

Ethical dilemmas arise when principles conflict, such as the value of openness against the privacy of participants, or the benefit of a study against the risk it imposes. There is rarely a rule that resolves such conflicts, and the group should reason through them with the help of the review committee and with reference to the principles. Ioannidis (2005) described how the pressure to produce results can lead to compromises that would not be accepted if they were made openly, and checklists that prompt the consideration of each issue help to keep decisions visible. Cumming (2014) argues that a group should review its practice periodically, so that its standards are maintained as the research changes and new questions arise.
