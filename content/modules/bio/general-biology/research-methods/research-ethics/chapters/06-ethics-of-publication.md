Publication is an ethical act as well as a scientific one. Participants and animals take part in research on the understanding that the results will contribute to knowledge, and a study whose unfavourable results are withheld has failed those who took part. Ioannidis (2005) described how the selective publication of favourable results distorts the evidence on which later decisions are made, and the ethical duty to publish all results follows directly from the purpose for which participants accepted risk.

Selective publication for commercial or personal interest is a particular breach of this duty. A sponsor that publishes only the trials that favour its product, or an investigator who buries a finding that threatens a reputation, betrays both the participants and the readers who rely on the record. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the transparency of reporting is a basic condition of an ethical science, and the suppression of results is a failure of that condition.

Disclosure of funding and conflicts of interest allows readers to judge the work in context. A study funded by an interested party, or conducted by investigators with a financial stake in the outcome, should say so clearly, so that the reader can weigh the findings accordingly. Adverse events must be reported accurately, with their number and their assessment, since an understatement of harm misleads those who would take the intervention. Cumming (2014) argues that the risk of an adverse event should be reported with its interval, since a small study with no events does not establish safety.

The results should be communicated to the people who took part in the research, in a form they can understand. Participants gave their time and bore risk, and they are entitled to know what was found. Where a study was stopped or could not be completed, its partial results should still be reported, so that the evidence it produced is not lost. The obligation to correct the record when an error is found applies to publication as it applies to every other part of research.
