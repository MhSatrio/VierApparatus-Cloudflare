Informed consent is the process by which a person agrees to take part in research after understanding what it involves. The information given must cover the purpose of the study, the procedures, the risks and the benefits, the alternatives to participation and the handling of data. Disclosure of these items is necessary, but it is not sufficient. The information must be comprehensible to the participant, and a consent form that is technically complete but impenetrable does not meet the standard.

Participation must be voluntary, and the consent must be free from coercion and from undue inducement. A payment that is so large that it overrides the judgement of a participant, or a relationship in which a participant feels obliged to take part, undermines the voluntariness of the agreement. The participant must be told that they may withdraw at any time without penalty, and the investigators must honour that right. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that openness about the conditions of participation is part of the trust that research requires.

Some people cannot consent for themselves, such as young children or people with impaired capacity. In such cases a legal representative may consent on their behalf, and the participant may be asked to assent where they are able to express a view. The protocol must describe how this is done and how the participant's own wishes are respected. Ioannidis (2005) described the importance of well-designed research in justifying the exposure of those who cannot protect themselves, and the scientific case for the study must be strong.

Consent for the storage and reuse of data and samples is distinct from consent for the study itself. A participant who agreed to a particular study may not have agreed that their data be shared or used for unforeseen research, and the consent form must state these possibilities clearly. Peng (2011) argued that data sharing supports verification, but it is bounded by the consent under which the data were given. The documentation of consent, in a form that can be checked, completes the process.
