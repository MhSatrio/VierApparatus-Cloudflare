Research findings can be protected by patents and licensed to developers who turn them into products. This process can return value to the institutions and researchers who made the discovery, and it can bring useful products to the public. It also creates conflicts of interest, because the financial stakes of a researcher or a sponsor may influence the design, the analysis and the reporting of the work that is meant to evaluate the product.

A researcher who holds a financial stake in an outcome has a conflict that must be declared and managed. The declaration allows readers and reviewers to judge the work in context, and the management may require that the study be conducted or analysed independently of the interested party. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the independence of evaluation is a condition of its credibility, and a stake that is undisclosed undermines the trust that the research depends on.

Commercial sponsors have an interest in favourable results, and the pressure to report them can influence which studies are published. Ioannidis (2005) described how the selective publication of favourable findings inflates the apparent benefit of commercial products, and the remedy is the registration of all trials and the publication of results whatever they show. Independent verification by a team without the commercial interest is necessary before a sponsor's claim can be relied upon.

Open licensing can make a finding available for public benefit, particularly where the commercial market would not otherwise develop a product. The choice between exclusive and open licensing should consider the benefit to those who need the product and the incentive for its development. Cumming (2014) argues that the precision of any commercial claim should be reported, so that users can judge its value.

The commercialisation of research should serve the wider purpose of making research useful to society. The rewards it returns to researchers and institutions are legitimate, but they must not displace the obligation to report, verify and share the evidence on which a product rests.
