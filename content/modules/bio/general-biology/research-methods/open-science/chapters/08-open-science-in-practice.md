Open science is practised stage by stage, and the stages are connected. A question registered in advance, a data management plan written at the start and a scripted analysis that is deposited with the data together make a project transparent from beginning to end. Munafò et al. (2017) set out the practices, and this chapter shows how they are applied across a project so that transparency is built in rather than added at the end.

At the planning stage, the question and the analysis are registered before data are collected, and the data management plan is written and made available. Wilkinson et al. (2016) described the principles that the plan should follow, and the registration fixes the primary outcome so that later flexibility is visible. Cumming (2014) argues that the precision the study aims to achieve should be part of the registered plan.

During conduct and analysis, the laboratory notebook or project log records each decision with its date and reason, and the analysis is scripted and kept under version control. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended these practices as the basis of computational reproducibility, and the deposited pipeline allows the analysis to be run by others. Sensitivity analyses that test the robustness of the conclusion are reported alongside the main result.

At publication, the article is made open access where possible, and a data availability statement says where the data and code can be found and under what conditions. A preprint may be posted early so that the work is scrutinised before the formal review ends. Peng (2011) argued that the chain from claim to evidence must be complete, and the publication should name each deposited component.

Maintenance continues after publication, because data and code must remain available and must be updated when problems are found. A research group can audit its own practice against a checklist covering each stage, identifying the points at which transparency is weakest and planning to strengthen them. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that such self-assessment is a practical way to turn the principles of open science into the habits of a research group.
