Oxygen enters the blood by diffusion across the alveolar membrane, and carbon dioxide leaves by the same route. The rate of diffusion depends on the difference in partial pressure of each gas on the two sides of the membrane, on the surface area available and on the thickness of the barrier. The alveolar membrane is extremely thin and the alveolar surface is large, so exchange is rapid enough to keep the blood fully saturated with oxygen at rest.

Most oxygen is carried by haemoglobin in red blood cells, and only a small amount dissolves in plasma. Each haemoglobin molecule binds four oxygen molecules, and the binding is cooperative, so that the saturation curve is sigmoidal. This shape allows haemoglobin to load oxygen efficiently in the lungs and to release it in tissues where the partial pressure is lower.

Carbon dioxide is carried in three forms: as dissolved gas, bound to haemoglobin, and as bicarbonate ions. Most of it enters the red cell and is converted by carbonic anhydrase into carbonic acid, which dissociates into bicarbonate and hydrogen ions. Ball (2008) described the importance of water in such equilibria, and the conversion of carbon dioxide to bicarbonate depends on the water that is present in the blood. The bicarbonate is exported from the cell in exchange for chloride, and the hydrogen ions are buffered by haemoglobin.

Breathing is controlled automatically. Chemoreceptors in the brainstem and the carotid bodies detect changes in the carbon dioxide and hydrogen ion concentration of the blood, and they adjust the rate and depth of breathing. A rise in carbon dioxide stimulates breathing, which removes the excess and restores the balance. This control keeps the blood pH within a narrow range, as described in the chapter on buffers in the Water, Acids, Bases and pH module.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cellular systems that regulate pH and transport across membranes, which operate in the lungs and in the blood. Respiratory function is measured by spirometry, by blood gas analysis and by the measurement of oxygen and carbon dioxide in exhaled air.
