# Human Physiology

Physiology explains how the body's parts work together to keep a person alive. Anatomy describes the structures; physiology describes what they do and how their activity is controlled. The human body maintains a narrow range of temperature, pH, fluid volume and glucose, while its organs respond to exercise, food, stress and infection. The processes that do this are the subject of this module.

The first chapter introduces homeostasis, the principle that the body regulates itself through feedback. The next chapters cover the electrical activity of nerves, the mechanics and energy supply of muscle, the circulation, and the exchange of gases in the lungs. The later chapters examine the kidneys, which regulate water and salts, the endocrine glands, which control the body through hormones, and the regulation of temperature and metabolism.

Each chapter explains a system in terms of the mechanisms that drive it and the measurements used to study it. Physiological explanations are often quantitative, and the chapters give the relationships that allow a clinician or researcher to judge whether a system is working normally.

Readers should connect the physiology to the anatomy described in the previous module, since each function depends on the structure that performs it.
