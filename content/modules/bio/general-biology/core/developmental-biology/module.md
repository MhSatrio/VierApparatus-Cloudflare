# Developmental Biology

Developmental biology asks how a single cell produces an organism with many cell types arranged in a precise form. Each step of development depends on information in the genome, the position of each cell in the embryo and signals exchanged between cells. The field has shown that the same regulatory genes work across a wide range of animals, which gives a molecular account of the shared body plans described in the Zoology module.

The module follows development in sequence. It begins with fertilisation and cleavage, then gastrulation, which forms the germ layers. The following chapters describe how morphogen gradients give cells positional information, how a screen in fruit flies identified the genes that divide the embryo into segments, how limbs form, and how stem cells and organs develop and regenerate.

Development is studied in many organisms. The fruit fly, the frog, the zebrafish, the mouse and the sea urchin each offer advantages, and the chapters draw on several of them.

The module assumes the cell biology of the earlier modules, and it prepares the reader for the Evolutionary Developmental Biology material of the specialised levels.
