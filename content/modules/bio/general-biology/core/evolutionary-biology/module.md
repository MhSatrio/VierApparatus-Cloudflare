# Evolutionary Biology

Evolution is the central theory of biology, and the Foundations modules introduced its mechanisms. This Core module treats evolution at a higher level of detail. It asks what fitness means precisely, how selection acts on traits that are costly to their bearers, how species interact and diverge, and how large-scale patterns of diversity and extinction arise from processes that operate in individual populations.

The module begins with fitness and adaptation, then turns to the forms of selection that go beyond survival: sexual selection, which acts through mating success, and kin selection, which explains apparent altruism. It then considers coevolution, in which species evolve in response to one another, adaptive radiation, in which a lineage diversifies rapidly into new niches, and convergence, in which unrelated lineages reach similar solutions. The last two chapters discuss the comparative methods that test evolutionary hypotheses and the macroevolutionary patterns of extinction.

The module emphasises the testing of hypotheses. Evolutionary explanations are often plausible in more than one way, and the chapters point out the observations that distinguish between them.

Readers should be comfortable with the genetics of populations described in the Genetics module, since much of the reasoning depends on how allele frequencies change.
