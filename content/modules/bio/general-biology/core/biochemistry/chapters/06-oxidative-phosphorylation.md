Oxidative phosphorylation is the process by which the electrons carried by NADH and FADH2 are passed through a series of protein complexes to oxygen, and the energy released is used to make ATP. It takes place on the inner mitochondrial membrane in eukaryotes and on the plasma membrane in bacteria. It produces most of the ATP that an aerobic cell uses.

The electron transport chain consists of four large complexes and two mobile carriers, ubiquinone and cytochrome c. Electrons pass from one complex to the next, and at three of the complexes the energy released is used to pump protons out of the matrix. The pumping creates a difference in proton concentration and in electrical potential across the inner membrane. The final electron acceptor is oxygen, which is reduced to water. The chain is therefore the means by which the energy of food is transferred to oxygen in controlled steps.

Chemiosmosis explains how the proton gradient is used. Peter Mitchell proposed that the gradient stores energy in the same way that a charged battery does, and that the energy drives the synthesis of ATP. ATP synthase is a rotary machine in the membrane. Protons flow back through its channel, and the rotation of the enzyme drives the attachment of a phosphate to ADP, producing ATP.

Lane and Martin (2010) argued that the energy supply of a cell sets the limits of its complexity, and the efficiency of oxidative phosphorylation is one of the main determinants of the energy available per gene. Their argument links the chemistry of the membrane to the architecture of the genome.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe how the coupling of electron transport and ATP synthesis depends on the integrity of the inner membrane. Uncoupling agents that make the membrane leaky release the energy as heat rather than ATP, and this effect is used naturally by some animals to generate heat.

The process is studied by measuring oxygen consumption, by monitoring the proton gradient with fluorescent indicators and by reconstituting the complexes in artificial membranes.
