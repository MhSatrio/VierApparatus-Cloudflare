An enzyme converts substrate into product, and the rate of conversion depends on how much substrate is present. At low substrate concentration the rate rises nearly in proportion to the substrate. At high concentration the enzyme becomes saturated, and the rate approaches a maximum. The Michaelis-Menten model describes this behaviour with two parameters: the maximum velocity, Vmax, and the Michaelis constant, Km.

The model assumes that the enzyme first binds its substrate to form a complex, and that the complex then breaks down to release product and regenerate the free enzyme. The Michaelis constant is the substrate concentration at which the rate is half its maximum. It is related to the affinity of the enzyme for its substrate, although it is not simply a dissociation constant, because it also depends on the rate of catalysis. A low Km generally means that the enzyme works efficiently at low substrate concentrations.

Kendrew and colleagues (1960) showed that a protein's function depends on the shape of its binding site, and the enzyme-substrate complex is a case where the shape of the site determines both binding and rate. The principle connects the kinetic parameters to the structure of the active site.

Inhibitors change the apparent kinetic constants in characteristic ways. A competitive inhibitor binds to the active site and increases the apparent Km without changing Vmax, because high substrate concentrations can overcome it. A non-competitive inhibitor binds elsewhere and reduces Vmax without changing Km. Plots of the reciprocal rate against the reciprocal substrate concentration, known as double-reciprocal or Lineweaver-Burk plots, make these patterns easy to recognise.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe how enzyme kinetics is used to understand the regulation of metabolism and the action of drugs. Many drugs are designed as enzyme inhibitors, and their potency is measured by the same kinetic analysis.

Enzyme kinetics is measured by following the appearance of product or the disappearance of substrate over time, at a series of substrate concentrations, and by fitting the results to the model.
