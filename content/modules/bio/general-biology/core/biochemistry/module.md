# Biochemistry

Biochemistry is the chemistry of living organisms. It explains how the molecules described in the earlier modules are built, broken down and transformed, and how the energy released in those transformations is captured and used. The discipline connects the structure of molecules to the reactions they undergo and to the regulation of those reactions in the cell.

The module begins with enzyme kinetics, which describes how fast enzymes work and how their rate depends on substrate concentration. It then turns to the major metabolic pathways. Glycolysis and the citric acid cycle release energy from sugars, oxidative phosphorylation converts that energy into ATP, and the pathways for lipids and amino acids connect metabolism to the building of cell components. The final chapter covers the methods biochemists use to measure these processes.

Metabolism is organised so that the cell can respond to its needs. Each pathway is regulated, and the regulation of one pathway affects the others. The module emphasises these links, because a biochemical pathway is understood only as part of a network.

The chapters draw on the structure of proteins described by Kendrew and colleagues (1960) and Anfinsen (1973), and on the energetic argument of Lane and Martin (2010), which explains why metabolism is shaped by the economy of energy.
