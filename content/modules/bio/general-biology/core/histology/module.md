# Histology

Histology is the study of tissues, the level of organisation between the individual cell and the organ. It depends on microscopy and on the preparation of thin sections that show how cells are arranged in relation to one another. Histology began with the observations of Hooke (1665), who described the compartments of cork, and it became the basis of diagnostic medicine when pathologists learned to read the changes in tissue that disease produces.

The module begins with the four basic tissue types and the features that identify each in a section. It then covers the cells of the blood and the bone marrow that produces them, the preparation and staining of specimens, and the use of histology in diagnosing disease. Each chapter describes what is seen under the microscope and what the structure reveals about function.

Histology relies on careful reading of images. A section shows a two-dimensional slice of a three-dimensional tissue, and the appearance of a cell depends on the plane of cutting and on the stain used. The chapters explain these limits so that a reader can interpret what is visible without being misled by the preparation.

The module draws on the tissue types introduced in the Biological Organization module and on the anatomy of the human body described in the Human Anatomy module.
