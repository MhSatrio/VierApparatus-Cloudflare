Bacteria mainly reproduce asexually, so they do not inherit genes from two parents in the way that eukaryotes do. Instead, they acquire new genes from other bacteria and from the environment through horizontal gene transfer, the movement of DNA between cells that are not parent and offspring. Horizontal transfer is one of the most important sources of variation in bacteria, and it can move a useful trait across species in a single event.

Transformation is the uptake of free DNA from the environment. Some bacteria are naturally competent, meaning they can take up DNA through their membranes and incorporate it into their chromosome by recombination. The DNA is often released from dead cells, so transformation links the fate of one bacterium to the genetic material of others.

Transduction is the transfer of DNA by a bacteriophage. A phage that infects a bacterium can package a fragment of the host's DNA into a new virus particle by mistake, and that particle can carry the fragment to another bacterium. The process can move genes between related strains, and it has been used to map bacterial chromosomes.

Conjugation is the transfer of DNA through direct contact between cells. A donor cell forms a pilus that attaches to a recipient and transfers a copy of a plasmid, which may carry resistance genes or other useful traits. Some conjugative plasmids can also integrate into the chromosome and transfer the chromosomal genes.

Dobzhansky (1973) described how the variation on which selection acts arises from genetic change, and horizontal transfer provides bacteria with variation that does not depend on mutation alone. The rapid spread of traits through transfer can make bacterial populations adapt much faster than would be possible by mutation.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the molecular mechanisms of recombination and plasmid replication that underlie these processes. Gene transfer is studied by introducing marked DNA into recipients and selecting for the transferred markers, and by sequencing the genomes of related strains.
