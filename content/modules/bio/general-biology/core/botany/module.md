# Botany

Plants are the foundation of most terrestrial food webs, and their photosynthesis supplies the oxygen and the organic carbon on which almost all other life depends. Botany studies plants at every level, from the molecules of photosynthesis to the communities of forests and grasslands. Plant biology also includes the physiology that allows plants to live in their environments, which is different from that of animals in important ways.

The module begins with the plant cell, whose walls, plastids and large vacuole shape its biology. It then describes photosynthesis, the process that captures light energy and fixes carbon. The following chapters cover growth and meristems, plant hormones, the movement of water and nutrients, reproduction and the diversity of plants and their evolution.

A recurring theme is that plants cannot move, so they must sense and respond to their surroundings through their growth. Their biology reflects this: their cells are arranged in ways that allow them to grow toward light, to take up water from the soil and to reproduce without going elsewhere.

The module draws on the cell biology of earlier modules, and it prepares the reader for the Plant Physiology and Plant Pathology material of the specialized levels.
