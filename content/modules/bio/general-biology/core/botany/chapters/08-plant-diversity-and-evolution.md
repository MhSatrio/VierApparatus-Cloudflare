Plants descended from green algae that lived in water. The transition to land required new solutions to problems of water loss, support, reproduction and the absorption of light and nutrients. The land plants, or embryophytes, share several features that set them apart from their algal relatives, including the retention of the embryo on the parent and the cuticle that limits water loss. Darwin (1859) noted that the diversity of plants reflects their descent with modification, and the groups discussed here form a sequence of lineages that acquired these features at different times.

Mosses and liverworts are non-vascular plants. They lack the xylem and phloem that carry water and sugars through larger plants, and they remain small and close to the ground. Their gametophyte is the dominant, photosynthetic generation, and the sporophyte depends on it. Ferns and their relatives have vascular tissue, which allows them to grow taller, and they still reproduce by spores that disperse in the wind.

Gymnosperms, such as conifers, produce seeds that are not enclosed in a fruit. Their pollen is carried by the wind, and they dominate many cold and dry environments. Flowering plants, the angiosperms, enclose their seeds in fruits and rely on animal pollinators, as described in the previous chapter. They are the most diverse group of land plants.

Nelson and Ben-Shem (2004) described the photosynthetic apparatus that is shared by all these groups, although its components differ in detail. The shared architecture shows that the machinery of photosynthesis was inherited from a common ancestor and adapted in each lineage.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe how the developmental programmes that produce the body plans of plants are organised by transcription factors and hormones, and how changes in these programmes produce new forms. The diversity of plants can be traced to changes in such regulatory networks.

Plant diversity is studied by comparing morphology, by sequencing genes that define lineages and by fossils that reveal the timing of the transition to land.
