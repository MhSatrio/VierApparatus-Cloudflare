# Taxonomy and Systematics

Taxonomy names and organises the diversity of organisms. Systematics studies the evolutionary relationships among them and uses those relationships as the basis of classification. The two are often used interchangeably, but they answer different questions. Taxonomy asks how a group should be named and where it belongs. Systematics asks what the history of the group was, and whether a proposed grouping reflects that history.

The module begins by separating these two activities. It then describes how characters are chosen and how a tree is built from them, how confidence in the tree is measured, and how molecular and morphological evidence are used. The later chapters cover species concepts, the codes that govern names, and the revision of classifications in light of new data.

The module is meant to make the reader able to read a phylogenetic tree critically, to judge whether its groups are supported, and to recognise the choices that the authors made in building it.

Many of the methods in this module are computational, and the chapters explain the logic of each algorithm without requiring programming. Readers who want to apply the methods will find the references point to the primary literature.
