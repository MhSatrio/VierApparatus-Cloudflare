The species is the basic unit of classification, yet it has no single agreed definition. Darwin (1859) wrote about the origin of species without a precise definition, and the question of what counts as a species has been debated ever since. Different concepts give different answers for the same organisms, and the choice of concept affects how biodiversity is counted and how conservation priorities are set.

The biological species concept defines a species as a group of populations that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring, and that are reproductively isolated from other such groups. Dobzhansky (1973) emphasised the role of reproductive isolation in the origin of species, and the biological concept reflects this view. It works well for sexually reproducing animals and plants, but it cannot be applied to asexual organisms, which do not interbreed, or to fossils, whose mating cannot be observed.

The phylogenetic species concept defines a species as the smallest group that is diagnosably distinct and that shares a common ancestor. This concept fits the tree-based approach of systematics, and it can be applied to asexual and fossil organisms. Its weakness is that it can divide populations into many small species if the differences are minor.

The morphological species concept defines a species by its appearance. It is practical for identification, and it remains widely used for organisms that are hard to study otherwise. It can miss cryptic species, which look alike but are genetically distinct, and it can merge species that differ in form only slightly.

Bacteria and archaea present special problems. Woese and colleagues (1990) showed that prokaryotes are divided into groups by sequence rather than by the species concepts used for animals, and the boundaries of prokaryotic species are often defined by degrees of similarity in genome sequences.

Species are studied by comparing genomes, by tests of mating and hybrid fertility, by the distribution of populations in the field and by the morphological examination of specimens.
