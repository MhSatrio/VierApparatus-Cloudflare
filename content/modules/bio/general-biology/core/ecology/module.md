# Ecology

The Foundations module introduced ecology as a set of concepts: energy, nutrients, populations, interactions, succession and human impact. This Core module examines the theory behind those concepts. It asks how communities are structured, what controls the size of populations, how competition and predation shape the species that coexist, how ecosystems function, why species occur where they do, and how ecological knowledge is applied in monitoring and management.

The module treats ecology as a science of testable hypotheses. Many ecological questions have several plausible answers, and the chapters identify the observations and experiments that distinguish between them. Paine (1966) and Hairston and colleagues (1960) appear repeatedly because their studies set out questions that are still central.

The chapters are written for readers who have completed the Foundations level and who are ready to work with data, models and the literature of the field.

Dobzhansky (1973) argued that ecological interactions are part of the evolutionary history of organisms, and the theory of this module draws on that view.
