Molecules cross the plasma membrane by several mechanisms. Small uncharged molecules can dissolve in the lipid bilayer and diffuse across it, down their concentration gradient. Water crosses membranes in the same way, and it also moves through aquaporin channels that speed its passage. Ions and larger polar molecules cannot cross the hydrophobic core of the bilayer on their own, and they need proteins that provide a route through it.

Channels are proteins that form pores. Most are gated, which means they open only in response to a signal such as a change in voltage or the binding of a ligand. Once open, a channel allows ions of one kind to pass down their electrochemical gradient at a very high rate, often millions of ions per second. Channels are the basis of nerve impulses and of many responses to stimuli.

Carriers bind a specific substrate and change shape to move it across the membrane. Their rate is much slower than that of a channel, but they can move molecules against their gradient when they are coupled to an energy source. Ball (2008) pointed out that water in and around the protein influences how these conformational changes occur, which is one of the reasons the solvent matters to transport.

Active transport uses energy to move molecules against a gradient. Pumps such as the sodium-potassium ATPase use the energy of ATP hydrolysis to move sodium out of the cell and potassium in. The gradient they maintain stores energy that other transporters can use, so a single pump can drive the uptake of nutrients by coupled carriers.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe how cells regulate these mechanisms, for example by changing the number of channels in the membrane or by modifying the activity of carriers in response to signals. Transport is therefore a controlled process that adjusts the cell's composition to its needs.

Transport is measured by radioactive or fluorescent tracers, by electrophysiology, which records the currents carried by channels, and by reconstitution of purified proteins into artificial membranes.
