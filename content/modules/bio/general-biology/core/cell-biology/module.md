# Cell Biology

Cell biology is the study of cells: how they are built, how they work and how they respond to their surroundings. It is the central discipline behind most of biology, because every process in an organism happens in cells or between them. The Foundations level introduces the basic ideas, and this Core module treats the field in more depth.

The module covers eight topics. It starts with cell theory, which is the framework for everything that follows. It then describes the transport of molecules across membranes, the trafficking of proteins through the secretory and endocytic pathways, and the cytoskeleton that gives cells their shape and drives their movement. The cell cycle, programmed cell death, cell signalling and adhesion complete the picture of how cells live, divide, die and interact.

Each chapter combines the structure of a process with its regulation, since function in cells depends on both. The chapters also show how the same principles apply to single-celled organisms and to the cells of tissues.

Readers who have completed the earlier modules on chemistry, macromolecules and microscopy will find that most of the vocabulary is familiar. The new material concerns how cells use these building blocks.
