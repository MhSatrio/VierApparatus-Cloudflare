Transcription is the synthesis of RNA using a DNA template. The enzyme RNA polymerase binds to a promoter, a region of DNA that marks where transcription begins and which strand is read. The polymerase opens the double helix locally and synthesises an RNA molecule whose sequence is complementary to the template strand, with uracil taking the place of thymine. The RNA is synthesised in the five-prime to three-prime direction, as DNA is.

Initiation is the most regulated step. In bacteria a sigma factor helps the polymerase recognise the promoter. In eukaryotes, general transcription factors assemble at the promoter with the polymerase, forming a preinitiation complex. Additional activator and repressor proteins bind to enhancers and other regulatory sequences, which can be many kilobases from the start site. Jacob and Monod (1961) showed that a repressor protein can prevent transcription by binding near the promoter, which established the principle of negative control.

Elongation proceeds as the polymerase moves along the template and adds nucleotides to the growing RNA. The polymerase unwinds and rewinds the DNA as it moves, and it can pause at certain sequences or be stalled by damage. Other factors help the polymerase proceed through nucleosomes in eukaryotic chromatin.

Termination occurs when the polymerase reaches a signal in the DNA. In bacteria, some termination signals form a hairpin in the RNA that destabilises the complex, and others require a protein to release the RNA. In eukaryotes, termination is coupled to the cleavage of the RNA downstream of the coding region.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe how transcription is controlled to produce the right amount of each RNA in each cell type. The combination of promoters, enhancers and regulatory proteins determines the expression pattern of every gene.

Transcription is measured by reporter genes that produce a detectable signal, by sequencing of RNA populations, and by chromatin immunoprecipitation, which identifies the DNA regions bound by specific proteins.
