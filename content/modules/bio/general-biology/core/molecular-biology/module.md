# Molecular Biology

Molecular biology studies the molecules that carry and use genetic information, and the processes that copy, read and regulate it. The discipline grew from the discovery of the structure of DNA and has since explained how cells store their instructions, make the proteins those instructions specify, and adjust their activity to conditions. It is the core of most modern biology.

The module follows the flow of information. Its first chapter sets out the central dogma of molecular biology and the exceptions that qualify it. The next chapters describe replication, transcription, RNA processing and translation in turn, and the regulation of gene expression. The later chapters cover post-translational modification and the techniques through which these processes are measured.

Each chapter links a mechanism to the experiment that revealed it. Molecular biology is an experimental science, and knowing how a result was obtained is part of understanding what it means.

The module assumes the structure of nucleic acids and proteins from the Biological Macromolecules module. It prepares the reader for the Genetics and Biochemistry modules that follow.
