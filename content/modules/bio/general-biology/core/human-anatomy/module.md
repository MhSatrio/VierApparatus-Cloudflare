# Human Anatomy

Anatomy is the study of the structure of the body. It describes the position, shape and relations of organs and tissues, and it provides the vocabulary that physicians, physiologists and biologists use to communicate. Human anatomy is the basis of medicine and of every other discipline that studies the body, and it is also a window onto the evolutionary history of vertebrates, since the human body shares its plan with other mammals.

The module begins with the terms that define position and direction, which are used throughout the curriculum. It then covers the skeleton, the muscles, the heart and blood vessels, the lungs, the digestive tract and the nervous system. The final chapter considers homology, the shared structures that link humans to other animals and reveal their common ancestry.

Anatomy is best learned in three dimensions. Each chapter gives the arrangement of organs and their relations to neighbouring structures, and it notes how the structure relates to the function described in the Human Physiology module that follows.

The descriptions here are general. Individual bodies vary in detail, and the variations are a subject of study in their own right.
