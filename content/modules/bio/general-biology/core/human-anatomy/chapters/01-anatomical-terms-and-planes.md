Anatomists describe the body from a standard reference position called the anatomical position. The body stands upright with the feet flat and parallel, the arms at the sides and the palms facing forward. All directions are given relative to this position, so that a term always means the same thing whatever the posture of the person. Without a common reference, a description of a structure could be misread by a reader who imagines the body in a different orientation.

Directional terms describe the relation of one structure to another. Anterior means towards the front, posterior towards the back, superior towards the head and inferior towards the feet. Medial means towards the midline of the body and lateral away from it. Proximal and distal are used for the limbs, meaning closer to and further from the point of attachment to the trunk. Superficial and deep describe position relative to the surface. Each pair of terms is opposite, and together they locate any structure precisely.

Planes are imaginary flat surfaces that divide the body. The sagittal plane runs from front to back and divides the body into left and right portions; a midsagittal plane divides it into equal halves. The coronal, or frontal, plane runs from side to side and divides the body into front and back parts. The transverse plane runs horizontally and divides the body into upper and lower parts. Sections made along these planes are named after them, and a radiologist reads images in these same planes.

Darwin (1859) argued that the structures of different animals are modified versions of shared designs, and the same body planes apply across vertebrates. The directional terms describe a plan that humans share with many other animals, which is one reason the terminology is standard across biology.

Anatomical description is also a practical skill. Surgeons, radiologists and pathologists use these terms to record and communicate findings, and an accurate description allows another observer to find the same structure.

The chapters that follow use these terms throughout, so the reader should be comfortable with them before reading the systems in detail.
