The nervous system is divided into the central nervous system, which consists of the brain and spinal cord, and the peripheral nervous system, which consists of the nerves that connect the central system to the rest of the body. The peripheral system includes cranial nerves that arise from the brain, spinal nerves that arise from the spinal cord, and the autonomic nervous system that controls involuntary functions such as heart rate and digestion. The central system integrates information, and the peripheral system carries signals to and from it.

The brain has several main regions. The cerebrum is the largest part and is divided into two hemispheres, each with four lobes, which control voluntary movement, sensation, language and higher cognition. The cerebellum lies at the back and coordinates movement and balance. The brainstem connects the brain to the spinal cord and contains the centres that control breathing, heart rate and consciousness. The diencephalon, which includes the thalamus and hypothalamus, relays sensory information and regulates hormones and the internal environment.

The spinal cord runs within the vertebral column and carries signals between the brain and the body. It is organised into segments, each giving rise to a pair of spinal nerves. Sensory information enters through the dorsal root and motor commands leave through the ventral root. Reflexes can be produced by the spinal cord without involving the brain, which allows rapid responses to danger.

The brain is protected by the skull and by three membranes called the meninges, and it is bathed in cerebrospinal fluid. Ball (2008) pointed out that water and its interactions with dissolved ions are central to the function of cells, and the composition of the fluid that surrounds the brain is carefully regulated to protect the nervous tissue.

Darwin (1859) observed that the nervous systems of vertebrates share a common organisation, and the arrangement of the spinal cord and brain is similar across the group. The variations among species reflect differences in the relative size of the regions.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cells of the nervous system and the way they form circuits. Neuroanatomy is studied by dissection, by imaging with magnetic resonance and by the examination of stained sections under the microscope.
