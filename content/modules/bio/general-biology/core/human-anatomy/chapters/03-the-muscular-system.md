The human body contains three types of muscle. Skeletal muscle is attached to bones, and it is under voluntary control. Its fibres are long, multinucleate and striated, and they contract rapidly and powerfully. Cardiac muscle forms the wall of the heart. Its cells are striated but branched, joined by intercalated discs that allow electrical signals to pass from cell to cell, and it contracts rhythmically without voluntary control. Smooth muscle lines the walls of blood vessels, the gut and the airways. It is not striated and it contracts slowly, under involuntary control.

Skeletal muscle is organised into bundles, and each fibre is a long cell packed with myofibrils. The sliding filament mechanism described in the Zoology module operates in each sarcomere, the repeating unit of the myofibril. Muscle fibres are grouped into motor units, each controlled by one motor neuron, and the number of motor units recruited determines the force of contraction.

Muscles act across joints, and most work in antagonistic pairs. The biceps flexes the elbow and the triceps extends it. When one muscle contracts, the other relaxes, so that movement is smooth and controlled. The arrangement reflects the fact that a muscle can only pull, not push, so two muscles are required to move a joint in both directions.

The major skeletal muscles are named by their shape, size, location, action or the number of their origins. The gluteus maximus is the largest muscle in the body, and the sartorius is the longest. Naming follows a logic that can be learned from the rules rather than from memorising each name.

Darwin (1859) observed that the muscles of different animals are modified versions of shared arrangements, and the antagonistic pairing of muscles in the limbs of vertebrates reflects their common plan.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the molecular basis of contraction and the regulation of muscle gene expression. Muscle is studied by electromyography, which records the electrical activity of muscle, and by biopsy, which allows the fibre types and their proteins to be examined.
