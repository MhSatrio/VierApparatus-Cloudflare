The adult human skeleton has about two hundred and six bones, although the number varies between individuals because some bones fuse and some small bones are variable. It is divided into two parts. The axial skeleton includes the skull, the vertebral column, the ribs and the sternum, and it forms the central axis of the body. The appendicular skeleton includes the bones of the limbs and the girdles that attach them to the axial skeleton.

Bone is a living tissue, not a rigid inert material. It consists of a mineral phase, mainly calcium phosphate in the form of hydroxyapatite, which gives it hardness, and an organic matrix, mainly collagen, which gives it flexibility. The two phases together make bone both strong and tough. Bone is constantly remodelled, with osteoclasts removing old tissue and osteoblasts laying down new matrix, which allows the skeleton to adapt to loads and to repair damage.

Cartilage is a flexible connective tissue that covers the ends of bones at joints and forms structures such as the nose and the ear. It has no blood supply of its own, and it receives nutrients by diffusion, which is one reason injuries to cartilage heal slowly.

The skeleton has several functions beyond support. It protects delicate organs, such as the brain within the skull and the heart and lungs within the rib cage. It serves as the site of attachment for muscles, so that contraction of a muscle moves a lever. It also stores most of the body's calcium and phosphate, and bone marrow within many bones produces the blood cells described in the Histology module.

Darwin (1859) noted that the bones of the forelimb in humans, bats, whales and horses share a common arrangement despite their different functions, and this similarity is evidence of common ancestry. The skeleton is one of the clearest records of that history, and it is discussed further in the chapter on homology.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cells that build and remodel bone, and the signals that control their activity. The skeleton is studied by radiography, by measurement of bone density and by the examination of bone tissue under the microscope.
