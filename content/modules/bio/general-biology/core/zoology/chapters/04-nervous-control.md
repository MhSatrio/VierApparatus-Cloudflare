Nervous systems allow animals to sense their surroundings, process the information and act on it, often within milliseconds. The basic unit is the neuron, a cell with a body, branching dendrites that receive signals, and a long axon that carries electrical impulses to other cells. The neuron maintains a resting potential across its membrane, produced by the unequal distribution of sodium and potassium ions and by the greater permeability of the membrane to potassium.

An action potential is a rapid, all-or-nothing change in the membrane potential. When a stimulus depolarises the membrane past a threshold, voltage-gated sodium channels open, sodium rushes in and the potential rises. The channels then inactivate, and voltage-gated potassium channels open, restoring the resting potential. The impulse travels along the axon because each patch of membrane depolarises the next. Ball (2008) noted that the behaviour of ions in water underlies these electrical events, and the hydration of ions influences their movement through channels.

Synapses transmit signals between neurons. At a chemical synapse, the arrival of an impulse triggers the release of neurotransmitter from vesicles into a narrow gap. The transmitter binds receptors on the next cell, opening channels or activating signalling pathways. The response may excite or inhibit the target, and the total effect depends on the balance of inputs. Synapses are the points at which information is computed in the nervous system.

Nervous systems range from simple nets in cnidarians to complex brains in vertebrates. The organisation of the nervous system determines how information is integrated, and larger nervous systems allow more complex behaviour.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the molecular basis of channels, transmitters and receptors, and how these are studied by electrophysiology and by genetic tools. The study of the nervous system links the chemistry of ions and molecules to the behaviour of whole animals.

Neural activity is measured by recording electrical potentials from single cells and from populations, by fluorescent indicators of calcium and voltage, and by genetic manipulation of identified neurons.
