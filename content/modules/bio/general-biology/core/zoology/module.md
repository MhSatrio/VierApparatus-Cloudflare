# Zoology

Animals are multicellular organisms that obtain their food by eating other organisms, that move at some stage of life, and that develop from a fertilised egg through a defined sequence of stages. The animal kingdom contains an enormous range of forms, from sponges and jellyfish to insects, fishes, birds and mammals. Zoology studies this diversity, and animal biology studies the cellular and physiological processes that make animals what they are.

The module begins with the body plans of animals and the features that define them. It then covers development, which builds the body plan from a single cell, and the movement and sensing that let animals respond to their surroundings. The later chapters cover nervous control, reproduction, behaviour and the diversity of the group, and the last chapter considers the origin of animals and the early evidence from the fossil and geological record.

Zoology draws on comparative anatomy, development and genetics. The chapters explain how these approaches reveal both the shared origins of animals and the differences that separate their groups.

The module connects to the Human Anatomy and Physiology module that follows, and it introduces the physiology that the specialized levels examine in more detail.
