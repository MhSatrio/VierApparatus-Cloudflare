A mutation is a heritable change in the DNA sequence. Point mutations change one base. A substitution replaces one base with another, and it may be a transition, between purines or between pyrimidines, or a transversion, between the two classes. Insertions and deletions add or remove bases. If the number of bases changed is not a multiple of three, the reading frame of the gene is shifted, and the protein downstream is usually lost. Chromosomal rearrangements, such as deletions, duplications, inversions and translocations, change larger regions of the genome.

Watson and Crick (1953) showed that the sequence of bases carries the information of the gene, so any change in sequence is a potential change in the message. The effect of a mutation depends on where it falls. A change in a non-coding region may have no effect, a change in the third position of a codon may be silent because of the degeneracy of the genetic code, and a change in a critical part of a protein may abolish its function.

Mutations are rare per base, but they occur continually. Errors in replication that escape proofreading and repair, damage from radiation and chemicals, and the activity of mobile genetic elements all contribute. The rate differs among species and among regions of the genome.

Dobzhansky (1973) argued that mutation is the source of the variation on which evolution depends. Most mutations are harmful or neutral, and only a few are advantageous. The balance between the loss of harmful variants by selection and the continual supply of new variants by mutation shapes the genetic diversity of populations.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cellular systems that detect and repair damage, and the consequences when repair fails. Defects in repair pathways increase the rate of mutation and can cause cancer and other diseases.

Mutations are detected by sequencing, by genetic screens that select for a phenotype and by comparisons of the genomes of related individuals, and their effects are tested by introducing them into organisms and measuring the outcome.
