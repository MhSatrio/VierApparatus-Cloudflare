# Genetics

Genetics is the study of heredity and variation. It asks how traits pass from parents to offspring, why some traits follow simple patterns while others do not, and how variation in genes produces differences among individuals. The subject began with careful breeding experiments, and it now uses the sequences of entire genomes. The laws of inheritance and the molecular basis of the gene are now understood together.

The module begins with Mendelian inheritance, the rules of segregation and independent assortment that describe the simplest cases. It then turns to the chromosomal basis of heredity, in which genes lie on chromosomes and can be linked. Recombination during meiosis separates linked genes, and this process is the basis of gene mapping. The later chapters cover mutation, the population genetics that describes allele frequencies, and the inheritance of disease in humans.

Genetics is quantitative. Each chapter gives the rules that predict the outcome of a cross or the frequency of an allele, and it shows how those predictions are tested. The approach connects the classical methods of breeding to the molecular data of today.

The module draws on the molecular basis of the gene from the Molecular Biology module, and it prepares the reader for the Genomics material in the Genetics and Genomics specialization.
