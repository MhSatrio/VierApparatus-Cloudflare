A fermentation report must allow another group to reproduce the run and to compare its performance with others. The organism should be described with its origin and the history of its inoculum, since the state of the cells at the start affects the whole run. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a fermentation those means include the strain, the medium and the procedure.

The medium, the vessel and the operating conditions should be stated. The composition of the medium, the dimensions and working volume of the vessel, and the temperature, pH, aeration and agitation determine the environment of the cells. Garcia-Ochoa and Gomez (2009) showed that the oxygen transfer depends on the vessel and its operation, and these should be reported with the run. The mode of operation and the feed profile, where a feed is used, complete the description.

The time courses of biomass, substrate and product should be shown, along with the contamination events and how they were handled. The yield, the titre and the productivity should be given with their intervals across replicate runs, and the by-products should be quantified. Cumming (2014) argues that a single run is weak evidence, and the number of replicate runs and the variation between them should be stated.

The mass balance and the monitoring data should be reported, along with the calculations that produced each figure. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs of an analysis together, and the run data and the calculations should be deposited with identifiers so that the performance can be recalculated by others.
