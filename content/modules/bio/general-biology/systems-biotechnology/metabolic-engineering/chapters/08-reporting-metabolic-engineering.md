A metabolically engineered strain is defined by its genotype, and a report must state every modification made to the host. The host species and its strain, the deleted genes, the inserted heterologous genes and their sources, and the promoters that control them should all be listed. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a strain the means are the genotype and the procedure by which it was built.

The culture conditions must be stated in full, because the performance of a strain depends on them. The medium, the carbon source, the temperature, the pH, the oxygen supply and the scale of the culture should be given, since a strain that performs well in a flask may behave differently in a vessel. Keasling (2010) emphasised that the conditions of production determine its economics, and a report that omits them cannot be compared with the process it claims to represent.

The performance should be reported with yield, titre and productivity, each with its time points and its interval, together with the tolerance of the strain. Cumming (2014) argues that each metric should be given with its precision, and the number of independent cultures behind it should be stated. Ioannidis (2005) described how a single favourable culture can mislead, so the replicates and any selection among them should be disclosed.

The controls and the history of improvement should be recorded, along with the deposition of the strain, its sequences and the data. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs of an analysis together, so that the strain and its performance can be reproduced and compared with other designs.
