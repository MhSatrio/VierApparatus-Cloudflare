A new crop variety must pass a regulatory assessment before it can be grown or sold, and the assessment considers its safety to food, to the environment and to the farmers who grow it. The assessment asks whether the new crop differs from its conventional counterpart in ways that matter, and what evidence supports that answer. Godfray et al. (2010) noted that the benefits of new technologies depend on their acceptance, and the regulatory process is one of the means by which acceptance is established.

Regulation differs between jurisdictions and between methods. A crop with a transgene is often regulated more strictly than one edited without foreign DNA, although the distinction is drawn differently in different places and it is contested. The regulatory status of a product may depend on the method used to make it rather than on the product itself, and developers must consider this from the start.

Labelling and traceability allow consumers and buyers to know what they are purchasing, and they require records of the lines and their handling. The records must be reliable, since a labelling system that cannot trace a product to its origin will not give the assurance it claims. Ioannidis (2005) described how claims that cannot be checked are of little value, and traceability makes the claims checkable.

Public acceptance depends on perception and on trust, and it is shaped by values and by experience as well as by evidence. Independent evidence, published openly and reproduced by others, is one of the means by which trust is built. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended that the evidence be recorded so that it can be checked, and Cumming (2014) argues that the risks and benefits should be reported with their intervals rather than as certainties.

Scientific evidence alone cannot settle every question of acceptance, since some of the disagreements concern values, such as how risk should be weighed against benefit or who should bear it. Such questions require public deliberation, and the role of the evidence is to inform that deliberation rather than to replace it.
