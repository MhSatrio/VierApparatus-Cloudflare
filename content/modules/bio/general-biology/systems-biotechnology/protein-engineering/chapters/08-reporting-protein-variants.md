A report of protein variants must allow another group to make the same variants and measure them in the same way. The parent sequence and the mutations of each variant should be given, since the function of a variant is a property of its sequence. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a protein the means include the sequence, the expression system and the purification.

The library and the screen should be described, with the method of generation, the number of variants made and screened, and the throughput and confirmation rate of the screen. Packer and Liu (2015) described how the method shapes the variants that are found, and the report should make the size of the search visible. A reader who sees only the best variant cannot judge how unusual it was.

The characterisation should give the kinetic parameters, the stability and the specificity of each variant, each with its replicates, its interval and the conditions of measurement. Cumming (2014) argues that each estimate should be reported with its precision, and the conditions determine the values, so a parameter without its conditions cannot be compared with another.

The full set of variants tested should be reported, not only the best. Ioannidis (2005) described how selecting the best of many candidates inflates its apparent value, and a report that discloses all results allows the selection effect to be judged. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended depositing the sequences, the assays and the data together, so that each claim about a variant can be checked and built upon.
