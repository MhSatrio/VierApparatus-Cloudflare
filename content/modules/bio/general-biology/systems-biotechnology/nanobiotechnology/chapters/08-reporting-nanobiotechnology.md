A nanobiotechnology report must describe the particles well enough for another group to make and measure the same preparation. The synthesis method and the composition should be stated first, along with the coating and its density. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a nanoparticle the means include the synthesis, the purification and the coating procedure.

The size distribution, the shape and the surface charge should be reported as measured in the medium of use, and the method and the medium should be given with each value. Nel et al. (2009) showed that the biological response depends on the particle as it is in the medium, and a size measured in water alone may not describe the particle in the cell culture or the body. The protein corona should be reported where it was characterised, with the fluid from which it formed.

The dose must be given in mass, number and surface area, since the response may correlate with any of them. The biological model and the exposure conditions must be stated, including the cell type or animal, the duration and the route. Cumming (2014) argues that each measurement should be reported with its interval, and Ioannidis (2005) described how a single preparation may not represent the class of particle studied.

The particle data and the protocols should be deposited with identifiers, so that the preparation can be remade and the response compared with other studies. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs of an analysis together, and this applies to a particle preparation and its biological test as much as to a computational analysis.
