A bioreactor design is reproduced only from a full description of the vessel and its internal components. The design criteria, with the weights given to each, should be stated first, since they explain the choices that follow. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a vessel those means are its drawings, its dimensions and its specifications.

The geometry and the internal components should be given with their proportions and their sizes. The height to diameter ratio, the impeller type, diameter and speed, the number and width of the baffles, and the sparger design with its hole size all determine the performance, and each should be recorded. Garcia-Ochoa and Gomez (2009) showed that the oxygen transfer depends on these together, so they must be reported together.

The measured performance should be given with the criterion used for scale-up and with the interval of each measurement. The oxygen transfer coefficient, the mixing time and the power per unit volume should be reported as measured, not only as calculated, and each should be given with its variation across replicate measurements. Cumming (2014) argues that a single measurement is weak evidence, and the number of measurements and their spread should be stated.

The materials, the sterility features and the scale-up tests should be described, and the drawings, the measurements and the calculations should be deposited with identifiers. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs of an analysis together, so that the vessel can be rebuilt and its performance checked by others.
