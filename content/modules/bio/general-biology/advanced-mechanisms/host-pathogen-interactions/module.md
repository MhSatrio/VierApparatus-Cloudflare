# Host-Pathogen Interactions

An infection is a contest between two sets of molecular systems. The pathogen seeks to enter the host, to obtain nutrients, to avoid destruction and to reproduce. The host seeks to detect the pathogen, to restrict its growth and to repair the damage it causes. The outcome depends on the balance between these strategies, and it varies with the pathogen, the host and the conditions of infection. Finlay and Falkow (1997) described the recurring themes that pathogens use, and their analysis provides the framework for this module.

The module begins with the strategies of entry and survival within the host, then turns to the mechanisms of immune evasion and manipulation. The later chapters describe the host defences that recognise and restrict pathogens, the genetic basis of susceptibility, the arms race between pathogens and hosts and the approaches to treatment and prevention.

The study of host-pathogen interactions has produced important insights into normal cell biology, because pathogens often exploit cellular processes that the host would otherwise keep hidden. Readers should expect each chapter to connect a pathogen strategy to the cell biology and immunology described in earlier modules.
