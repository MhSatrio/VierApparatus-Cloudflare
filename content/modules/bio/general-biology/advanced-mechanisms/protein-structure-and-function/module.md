# Protein Structure and Function

Proteins are the working molecules of the cell, and their function depends on their three-dimensional structure. Anfinsen (1973) established that the native structure is determined by the sequence, and the module builds on that principle at an advanced level. It asks how a sequence finds its fold, why proteins are dynamic rather than rigid, how a binding event at one site changes activity at another, how enzymes achieve their catalysis and how the structure of a protein can be predicted.

The module begins with the energetics of folding, using the free energy landscape described by Dill and MacCallum (2012). It then covers the structural motifs that recur across proteins, the motion within folded proteins, allostery, the mechanisms of enzymes, interactions between proteins, the proteins that do not fold, and the prediction of structure from sequence.

The chapters use the structural methods introduced in the Core level and the kinetic analysis of the Biochemistry module. Readers should expect to think in terms of distributions of structures, not single static models.

The module ends the Advanced level's structural strand and prepares the reader for the Biophysics and Structural Biology modules that follow.
