A tumour that grows beyond a few millimetres depends on a blood supply. Hanahan and Weinberg (2000) identified the induction of angiogenesis as a hallmark, and tumours achieve it by secreting signals such as vascular endothelial growth factor, which stimulate the growth of new vessels from existing ones. The vessels that form are abnormal. They are leaky, irregular and poorly organised, and they do not supply the tumour evenly, which contributes to the heterogeneity of the tumour.

The tumour microenvironment includes the stromal cells, the immune cells, the blood vessels and the extracellular matrix in which the tumour cells live. Fibroblasts are recruited and activated to produce matrix and signals, and macrophages in the tumour are often pushed toward a form that suppresses immune attack and promotes tissue remodelling. Medzhitov and Janeway (2002) described how innate cells recognise danger and respond to it, and this function is redirected in tumours.

Hypoxia develops when the supply of oxygen cannot keep pace with the demand of the tumour cells. The hypoxic cells adapt through the hypoxia-inducible factor, which activates genes for angiogenesis, glycolysis and survival. The hypoxic regions are often resistant to radiation and to some drugs, because oxygen is needed for the damage radiation causes and because drugs may not penetrate poorly perfused tissue.

The microenvironment is therefore a source of selection. Cells that can survive hypoxia, that can recruit stromal support or that can evade immune attack are favoured, and the tumour evolves within its environment. Targeting the microenvironment has been an approach to treatment, with anti-angiogenic drugs that reduce the blood supply, although their benefits have often been modest and resistance develops.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the signals that control vessel growth and the cells that respond to them. The microenvironment is studied by imaging of vessels and hypoxia in tumours, by analysing the cells present and by experiments that modify the stroma.
