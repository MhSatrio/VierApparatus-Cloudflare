# Cancer Biology

Cancer is a group of diseases in which cells escape the controls that normally govern their growth and death, and then invade and spread. The Advanced Cell Signaling and DNA Replication and Repair modules described many of the controls that are lost, and this module asks how a tumour accumulates the changes that make it cancerous, how those changes are visible in the genome, and how tumour cells interact with the tissue around them.

The framework of the module is the set of hallmark capabilities that Hanahan and Weinberg proposed, first in 2000 and then in an expanded form in 2011. The chapters follow these capabilities: self-sufficiency in growth signals, insensitivity to growth inhibition, resistance to death, replicative immortality, angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis, and the reprogramming of metabolism and the evasion of immune destruction. The later chapters treat the genomic landscapes that Vogelstein and colleagues (2013) described and the logic of treatment.

Cancer is an evolutionary process within a tissue. Mutations arise continually, and those that increase the growth or survival of a cell are selected, so that a tumour is a population of cells that has been shaped by selection. The module returns to this idea throughout, because it explains why tumours are heterogeneous and why they can resist treatment.

Readers should treat the hallmarks as a framework for organising evidence, not as a complete list of mechanisms. The chapters indicate where the evidence is strongest and where it is still developing.
