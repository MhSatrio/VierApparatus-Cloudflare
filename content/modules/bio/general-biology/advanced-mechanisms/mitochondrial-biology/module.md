# Mitochondrial Biology

Mitochondria were once regarded as static power stations. Research over the last decades has shown that they are dynamic organelles that fuse and divide, import most of their proteins from the cytosol, carry their own genome, and are removed when they are damaged. They are also central to cell death and to a wide range of disease. Nunnari and Suomalainen (2012) reviewed this picture, and their synthesis organises much of the module.

The chapters begin with the mitochondrial genome and its inheritance, then turn to the dynamics of fusion and fission and to the import of proteins across the two membranes. The later chapters cover mitophagy, the selective removal of damaged mitochondria, the role of mitochondria in apoptosis, the diseases that result from their dysfunction, the links to ageing and the inheritance of mitochondrial DNA through the maternal line.

The module builds on the bioenergetics described in the previous module. Mitchell (1961) established that the membrane is the seat of energy conversion, and the properties of that membrane depend on the organelle's integrity, which is what the quality control described here protects.

Readers should keep in mind that the functions of many mitochondrial proteins are still being defined, and some of the links between mitochondria and disease are correlations that are being tested.
