Mitochondrial diseases are a diverse group of disorders caused by defects in the respiratory chain or in the processes that maintain it. Because the organelle is present in every cell, the effects are often seen in tissues that depend most on oxidative energy, such as the brain, the heart, the skeletal muscle and the retina. Nunnari and Suomalainen (2012) described the range of these conditions, which include disorders caused by mutations in mitochondrial DNA and disorders caused by mutations in nuclear genes for mitochondrial proteins.

Mitochondrial DNA mutations are often heteroplasmic, meaning that a cell contains both mutant and normal copies. The proportion of mutant copies determines the severity of the defect, because the cell can tolerate a certain fraction of defective copies before its energy supply falls below the level needed for normal function. This threshold effect means that the disease may appear only when the mutant fraction exceeds a critical value, and the fraction can change between tissues and over time.

Inheritance of mitochondrial DNA is maternal in most cases, because the sperm contributes little or no mitochondrial DNA to the zygote. A mother with a heteroplasmic mutation can pass different proportions of mutant copies to different children, which makes the risk of disease in offspring hard to predict.

Diagnosis is difficult. The symptoms are varied, the genetic basis is often unclear and the proportion of mutant copies must be measured in the affected tissue. Ioannidis (2005) described how the reliability of a diagnostic claim depends on validation, and the diagnosis of mitochondrial disease requires confirmation in independent samples and clinical follow-up.

Treatment is limited. The aim is usually to support the affected tissue and to avoid triggers, and experimental approaches seek to reduce the proportion of mutant copies or to replace the missing function. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the respiratory chain whose defects underlie these diseases. Mitochondrial disease is studied by sequencing of the mitochondrial genome, by biochemical assays of the respiratory complexes and by animal models.
