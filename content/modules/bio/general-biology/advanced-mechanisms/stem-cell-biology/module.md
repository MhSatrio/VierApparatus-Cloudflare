# Stem Cell Biology

Stem cells are the source of new tissue throughout life and during development. Their defining properties are self-renewal and the capacity to produce differentiated descendants. The Developmental Biology module introduced them, and this module examines their regulation at an advanced level: what makes a cell pluripotent, how that state can be induced in differentiated cells, how adult stem cell systems are maintained, what happens when they become cancerous and what can be claimed for therapies derived from them.

The chapters begin with pluripotency and then turn to the induction of pluripotent cells, which Takahashi and Yamanaka (2006) reported, followed by embryonic and adult stem cells, cancer stem cells, reprogramming and epigenetics, and finally therapy and its evaluation. The subject combines developmental biology, chromatin regulation and clinical evidence, and the module treats each of these with the standards of evidence used throughout the curriculum.

Readers should expect the field to be one in which early claims were often overstated, and the chapters identify where the evidence is strong and where it is not.
