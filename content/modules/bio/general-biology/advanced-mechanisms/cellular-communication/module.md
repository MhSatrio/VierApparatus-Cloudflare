# Cellular Communication

Cells do not act alone. Each tissue is a community in which cells send and receive signals that coordinate their growth, their differentiation and their response to injury. The Cell Biology module described the basic classes of signal, and the Advanced Cell Signaling module examined the pathways that receive them. This module examines the communication itself: how signals are sent, how they travel between cells and how the modes of communication combine to produce tissue-level behaviour.

The chapters survey the modes of communication, from direct contact through gap junctions to secreted signals that act locally or at a distance. They then cover the extracellular vesicles that carry signals between cells, the synapses through which neurons communicate, quorum sensing in bacteria and the integration of signals within tissues.

Koshland (2002) included responsiveness among the principles of living systems, and communication is the means by which a multicellular organism responds as a whole. Medzhitov and Janeway (2002) showed that innate cells communicate danger signals to their neighbours, which provides an example of communication in the immune system.

Readers should note that the same signal can act through different modes depending on the distance and the concentration, so the classification is a convenience rather than a strict division.
