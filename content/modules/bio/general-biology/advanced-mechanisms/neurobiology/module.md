# Neurobiology

The nervous system is the most complex biological system that is studied at the molecular level. Its cells are excitable, its connections are highly specific and its function depends on the storage and modification of information. Neurobiology asks how these properties arise from the molecules described in earlier modules and how they give rise to behaviour and cognition.

The module begins with neuronal excitability, building on Hodgkin and Huxley (1952), and then turns to synaptic transmission and plasticity. It continues with the organisation of neural circuits, the development of the nervous system, the molecular basis of learning and memory, and the mechanisms of neurodegeneration. The chapters emphasise the transition from molecules to circuits, since the explanation of behaviour requires both levels.

Neurobiology has produced some of the most influential experimental results in biology, and it has also been a field in which claims were sometimes overstated. The chapters indicate where the evidence is firm and where interpretation is still under debate.
