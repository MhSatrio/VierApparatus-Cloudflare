Behaviour is produced by the nervous system acting on the body, and the molecules of the nervous system influence it at many levels. A single gene may affect the development of a circuit, the properties of a receptor, or the release of a neurotransmitter, and these effects combine to alter behaviour. Dobzhansky (1973) emphasised the evolutionary origins of traits, and behaviour is no exception: genes that influence behaviour are subject to selection, and their variation contributes to differences among individuals and species.

Genetic manipulation provides the most direct link between molecules and behaviour. Mutating a gene in an animal and observing a change in behaviour shows that the gene contributes to the behaviour, though not how. Conditional manipulations, which affect the gene in a defined cell type or at a defined time, narrow the explanation to the circuit and the period involved. The method has identified genes that influence feeding, sleep, social interaction and learning.

Neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin and acetylcholine are associated with many behaviours, and drugs that alter their action change behaviour predictably. The associations are strong in some cases, but they do not imply that a single transmitter controls a behaviour. Each transmitter acts on many circuits, and its effect depends on the receptors and the context.

The reductive approach has limits. Complex behaviours involve many genes, many circuits and the experience of the individual, and the explanation of a behaviour at the molecular level may not capture its organisation at the level of the whole organism. Ioannidis (2005) described how the association of individual genes with complex traits has often failed to replicate, and this is especially true of behaviour, where effects are small and measurements are noisy.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the molecular components whose roles in behaviour are studied. The approach requires careful behavioural measurement, controls for the genetic background and replication across laboratories.
