A signal must end when its job is done. Termination is as much a part of the response as initiation, and failure to terminate a signal can be as damaging as failure to start one. Phosphorylation is reversed by phosphatases, enzymes that remove phosphate groups from proteins. The balance between kinases and phosphatases sets the steady level of phosphorylation, and changes in either activity alter the duration and amplitude of the response.

Phosphatases are often regulated themselves. Some are inhibited by oxidation, some are controlled by their location and some are targeted to specific substrates by scaffolds. The regulation of a phosphatase can therefore change the response of a pathway without any change in the kinase, which gives the cell an additional level of control. The phosphatases are numerous, and many act on a broad range of substrates, so their specificity depends on targeting.

Receptors are terminated by internalisation. After activation, many receptors are removed from the surface by endocytosis and either degraded in the lysosome or recycled to the membrane. Receptor degradation ends the response permanently, while recycling allows the cell to respond again. The choice between the two depends on ubiquitination of the receptor, which marks it for the degradative route.

Koshland (2002) described adaptability as a feature of living systems, and the termination of signals is part of adaptation: a cell that cannot reset its signals cannot respond to new information. Persistent signals drive uncontrolled proliferation, and many cancers involve mutations that disable the termination mechanisms.

Quantitative analysis of termination uses the same kinetic reasoning as enzyme kinetics. The rate at which a phosphorylated protein returns to its unmodified state is the rate of the phosphatase reaction, and the response of the system depends on the ratio of the two rates. The model predicts the time course of the response after the stimulus is removed.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the phosphatases and the endocytic pathways that terminate signalling. Termination is studied by pulse-chase experiments, in which the stimulus is applied and then removed, and by measuring the decay of phosphorylation and the fate of receptors.
