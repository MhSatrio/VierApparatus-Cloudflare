Epigenetic states are inherited through the divisions of a cell lineage, as described in the chapter on epigenetic memory. Inheritance across generations is a more difficult question. In mammals, the germline undergoes a large resetting of its epigenetic marks during the development of the primordial germ cells, in which most methylation is erased and then re-established according to sex. This resetting makes stable transmission through the germline unlikely for most marks.

Bird (2002) noted that the faithful copying of methylation patterns within a lineage does not imply transmission between generations, and the resetting of the germline is a barrier that most marks cannot cross. Some marks escape the resetting, most notably the imprinted regions, where a parental origin mark is maintained. Imprinting provides a clear case of parent-of-origin inheritance of epigenetic states, although it is limited to specific loci.

Transgenerational inheritance has been reported in plants and in some animals, where a change in a parent appears in offspring that were never exposed to the change. Some of these reports involve small RNAs carried in the egg or sperm, and others involve chromatin marks that escape resetting. The evidence is strongest in plants and in simple animals, and it is weaker in mammals, where the replacement of the germline's marks limits what can be transmitted.

The debate is serious because claims of transgenerational effects have been made in human health research, and some reports have not replicated. Ioannidis (2005) described how small, flexibly analysed studies can produce findings that fail to hold, and the study of transgenerational effects requires large, carefully controlled experiments with independent replication before strong conclusions are drawn.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the germline and the processes of gametogenesis in which marks are reset. Epigenetic inheritance is tested by tracing marks through generations, by mutations that prevent resetting and by controlled exposures in experimental animals.
