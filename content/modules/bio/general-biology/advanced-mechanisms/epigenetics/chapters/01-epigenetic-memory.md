Epigenetic memory is the ability of a cell to maintain a transcriptional state through many divisions without a continuing external signal. A gene that was switched off during development can remain off in all the descendants of a cell, even though the signal that silenced it has long since disappeared. The state is remembered because the cell carries a mark that is copied along with the DNA and that recruits machinery to reproduce the state in the daughter cells.

Bird (2002) reviewed the evidence that DNA methylation provides such memory. Methylation of cytosines, particularly in the context of CpG sequences, is copied after replication by a maintenance enzyme that recognises hemimethylated DNA and methylates the new strand. Because the enzyme copies the existing pattern, the methylation state of each daughter cell matches that of the parent. Bird distinguished this maintenance from de novo methylation, which establishes new patterns during development.

A self-reinforcing mark is required for memory to persist. A mark that is not copied will be diluted by each replication, and after several divisions it will be lost unless the cell reproduces it. Methylation and some histone marks meet this requirement, because the enzymes that write them can recognise the existing mark on the template and copy it. The feedback between the mark and the machinery that writes it creates a stable state.

Memory is distinct from simple persistence. A state that persists only as long as the signal is present is not epigenetic memory, while a state that survives the removal of the signal is. The distinction matters for interpreting experiments, because a change that disappears when a drug is removed may reflect a transient response rather than a stable mark.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the enzymes that maintain methylation and the nucleosome-level processes that reassemble chromatin after replication. Epigenetic memory is studied by tracing the states of individual cell lineages, by measuring the stability of a state after the signal is removed and by manipulating the enzymes that copy the marks.
