# Epigenetics

Epigenetics concerns heritable changes in gene activity that are not caused by changes in the DNA sequence. A cell that has switched a gene off can pass that state to its daughters, and the state can persist for many divisions. The molecular basis is the set of chemical marks on DNA and on histones, and the proteins that write, read and erase them. The field has become central to development, to cancer and to the understanding of how environment and experience affect gene activity.

The module begins with the concept of epigenetic memory and the marks that carry it. It then covers DNA methylation, histone modification and the histone code, chromatin remodelling, and Polycomb-mediated silencing. The later chapters address the inheritance of epigenetic states through divisions and, in some cases, between generations, and the role of epigenetic changes in disease.

The module assumes the chromatin structure described in the Advanced Gene Expression module and the replication mechanisms of Molecular Biology. Many questions in epigenetics concern how these states are copied when the DNA is replicated.

Readers should keep the evidence in view. Some epigenetic claims rest on strong experiments, while others are based on correlations that do not prove causation, and the chapters indicate which is which.
