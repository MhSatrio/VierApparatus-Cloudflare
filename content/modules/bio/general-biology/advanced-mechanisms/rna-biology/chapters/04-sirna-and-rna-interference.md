Small interfering RNAs are produced from long double-stranded RNA, which may come from viruses, from transposons or from transgenes. Dicer cleaves the long duplex into pieces about twenty-one to twenty-three nucleotides long, with two-nucleotide overhangs at each end. Carthew and Sontheimer (2009) described how one strand is loaded into an Argonaute protein and guides the cleavage of any messenger RNA with which it is perfectly complementary.

The mechanism differs from that of microRNAs in its consequences. Because the siRNA pairs perfectly with its target, the Argonaute protein cleaves the messenger at the position opposite the centre of the guide, and the cleaved RNA is degraded. The effect is a strong, specific silencing of a single target, rather than the modest regulation of many targets that microRNAs produce. The difference reflects the degree of complementarity.

RNA interference is an antiviral defence in many organisms. Viral replication produces double-stranded RNA, and the processing of this RNA into siRNAs targets the viral genome for destruction. Some viruses have evolved proteins that suppress the pathway, which is evidence of its importance.

The method has been adopted as a research tool. Introducing synthetic siRNAs into cells reduces the expression of a chosen gene, allowing its function to be tested. The approach has also been developed for therapeutic use, but its specificity is imperfect, because partial matches can produce off-target effects, and the results of screens must be checked with independent methods.

Ioannidis (2005) described how biological studies with many results and flexible analysis can produce false positives. RNA interference screens are vulnerable to this problem, because off-target effects can produce apparent hits, and they must be confirmed with multiple independent siRNAs and genetic controls.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the enzymes of the pathway and the Argonaute family. RNA interference is studied by introducing siRNAs and measuring the target messenger, by genetic knockout of the pathway components and by sequencing of small RNAs.
