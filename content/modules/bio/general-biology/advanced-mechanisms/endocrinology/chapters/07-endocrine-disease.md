Endocrine diseases fall into a small number of mechanistic classes. In gland failure, the gland does not produce enough hormone, as in the destruction of insulin-producing beta cells in type 1 diabetes or the loss of thyroid tissue in hypothyroidism. In hormone excess, the gland produces too much, as in some pituitary tumours or in thyrotoxicosis. In receptor defects, the hormone is present but the target does not respond, as in insulin resistance. In autoimmune attack, the immune system destroys the gland or blocks its receptor.

Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells. Immune cells recognise beta cell antigens and kill the cells, and the loss of insulin produces hyperglycaemia and ketosis without treatment. The immune mechanisms described in the Immunology module underlie the disease, and the cell death is an example of the apoptosis described in the Cell Biology module.

Type 2 diabetes involves insulin resistance together with a failure of beta cells to compensate. The resistance is partly a defect in the insulin receptor signalling described in the Advanced Cell Signaling module, and it is linked to obesity, inflammation and lipid accumulation in tissues. The metabolic consequences are discussed in the Metabolism module.

Thyroid disease illustrates the feedback loops of the endocrine axis. Hyperthyroidism often arises when antibodies stimulate the thyroid hormone receptor, bypassing the normal feedback, and the excess hormone suppresses the pituitary. The diagnosis depends on measuring the trophic hormone as well as the thyroid hormone.

Ioannidis (2005) described the difficulty of establishing causal links in epidemiology, and many associations between endocrine factors and disease have not been confirmed. Diagnosis and treatment decisions should rest on controlled evidence, and hormone measurements must be interpreted with the conditions of sampling in mind.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cellular mechanisms of hormone production and action that are disturbed in these diseases. Endocrine disease is studied by clinical measurement, by immunological testing and by animal models.
