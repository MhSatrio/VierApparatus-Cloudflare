# Endocrinology

Endocrinology studies the hormones that coordinate the body. The Human Physiology module described the endocrine system at the level of organs and feedback loops. At the advanced level, the questions are molecular: how hormones are recognised by their receptors, how signals are amplified and integrated inside target cells, how the axes of the endocrine system are built, and how their failure produces disease.

The module begins with the receptor families for steroid, peptide and amine hormones, then treats the signalling each family triggers. The later chapters cover the architecture of endocrine axes, the integration of hormones with metabolism, the regulation of growth and reproduction, and the endocrine basis of common disease.

Koshland (2002) listed the signalling and response of cells among the principles of living systems, and endocrine signalling is one of the most sophisticated examples of that principle. The module draws on the signalling chapters of the Advanced Cell Signaling module throughout.

Readers should be aware that some endocrine claims, especially those about hormone supplements, have been made without adequate evidence, and the chapters indicate the standard of evidence needed.
