# Cellular Bioenergetics

Bioenergetics is the study of how living systems capture, store and use energy. The Biochemistry module described oxidative phosphorylation as a pathway. At the advanced level the question is how the free energy released by electron transfer is converted into a physical form, the gradient of protons across a membrane, and then into the chemical bond of ATP. The answer depends on membranes that are almost impermeable to protons and on molecular machines that couple one process to another.

The module begins with the chemiosmotic principle proposed by Mitchell (1961), which explains how the gradient links electron transfer to ATP synthesis. It then turns to ATP synthase, the rotary enzyme that Boyer (1997) described, to the electron transport complexes that generate the gradient and to the energetics of redox reactions. The final chapters consider the energy budgets of cells, the bioenergetic measurements used to study them and the comparison of photosynthesis with respiration.

Lane and Martin (2010) argued that energy supply constrains cell complexity, and bioenergetics is where that constraint is most directly expressed. The module returns to their argument when considering the efficiency of energy conversion.

Readers should note that the mechanisms described here are established in outline, and that the details of several complexes remain under active study.
