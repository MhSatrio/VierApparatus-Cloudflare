Bioenergetics is measured through several quantities that are linked by the chemiosmotic system. Oxygen consumption reports the rate of electron transfer to oxygen. The membrane potential and the pH gradient report the stored energy. ATP production reports the output of the synthase. Each quantity is measured by a different method, and each can be perturbed by a specific inhibitor, which allows the contribution of each component to be separated.

Oxygen consumption is measured with oxygen electrodes or with fluorescent probes that are quenched by oxygen. The rate is then compared with the rates obtained in the presence of inhibitors of each complex and of the ATP synthase, which identifies the fraction of respiration that is coupled to ATP synthesis. Membrane potential is measured with voltage-sensitive dyes that partition across the membrane, and pH gradients are measured with pH-sensitive probes. Mitchell (1961) predicted that the gradient would be the link between these quantities, and the measurements test that prediction.

ATP production is measured by luciferase assays or by the isotopic labelling of phosphate. The rate of synthesis is then compared with the consumption of oxygen, which gives the ratio of ATP made per oxygen atom reduced, known as the P-to-O ratio. The ratio depends on the substrate and on the leakiness of the membrane.

Interpretation requires combining the measurements. A decrease in oxygen consumption accompanied by an increase in membrane potential suggests a block in the chain, while a decrease in both suggests uncoupling or a loss of substrate. The conclusions depend on the completeness of the inhibitor controls, and on the assumption that the inhibitors act specifically.

Ioannidis (2005) pointed out that results obtained with a single measurement can be misleading, and bioenergetic conclusions should rest on several independent measures that agree. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the complexes whose activity is measured. The methods are standard in mitochondrial research and are applied to isolated organelles and to intact cells.
