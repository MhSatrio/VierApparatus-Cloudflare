Every redox couple has a standard reduction potential, a measure of its tendency to accept electrons. A couple with a more negative potential is a stronger electron donor, and one with a more positive potential is a stronger acceptor. When electrons pass from a donor to an acceptor with a more positive potential, the free energy change is negative, and the transfer releases energy. The energy released is given by the product of the number of electrons, the Faraday constant and the difference in potential between the two couples.

The respiratory carriers form a descending series. NADH has a strongly negative potential, around minus 0.32 volts at pH seven. The flavins and iron-sulphur clusters of complex I have intermediate potentials, ubiquinone has a value near zero, and the cytochromes and the oxygen couple have progressively more positive values. Oxygen, with a potential of about plus 0.8 volts, is the strongest acceptor among the common ones, and the total potential difference from NADH to oxygen is about 1.1 volts. The energy available from the complete oxidation of NADH by oxygen is therefore large.

The series is arranged so that each transfer releases an amount of energy that can be captured. If the potential difference between two adjacent carriers were too small, the energy would be lost as heat. Lane and Martin (2010) argued that the efficient capture of energy constrains the complexity of cells, and the arrangement of the redox series is one of the structures that allows energy to be captured economically.

The potentials are measured by electrochemistry, by titration with reducing and oxidising agents and by spectroscopic monitoring of the redox state of each carrier. The values are sensitive to pH and to the binding of ligands, so they must be measured under conditions that reflect the membrane environment.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the carriers of respiration and the reactions in which they take part. The description of redox energetics is the basis for understanding how each step of the chain contributes to the proton gradient.
