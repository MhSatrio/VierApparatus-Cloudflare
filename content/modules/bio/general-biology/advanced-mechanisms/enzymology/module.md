# Enzymology

Enzymology is the study of catalysts in living systems. Its concerns extend beyond the rate equations introduced in the Biochemistry module to the mechanisms by which enzymes act, the structures that make catalysis possible, the variety of chemistry that enzymes perform and the way that new enzymes arise. The field connects the structure of proteins to the metabolism of the whole cell and provides the tools used in drug design and biotechnology.

The module begins with the classification of enzymes and the kinetics of reactions with more than one substrate. It then treats inhibition, which is central to pharmacology, and cofactors, which extend the chemistry beyond what amino acids can do alone. The later chapters consider the evolution of enzymes, the catalytic capacity of RNA, the principles of enzyme engineering and the methods that measure catalysis at the level of crystals and single molecules.

Readers should note that enzyme function is a property of structure, dynamics and chemistry together. The chapters point out where the evidence for each mechanism is strong and where it rests on indirect measurement.
