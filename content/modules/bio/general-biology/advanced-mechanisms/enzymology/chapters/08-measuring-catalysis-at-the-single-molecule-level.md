Bulk kinetics report the average behaviour of a very large number of enzyme molecules. They show the rate of product formation, but they cannot reveal the individual steps or the variation between molecules. Single-molecule methods observe one enzyme at a time, and they show that enzymes can switch between active and inactive conformations, and that the rate of catalysis can fluctuate. The average hides these features, and single-molecule measurements can reveal them directly.

Single-molecule fluorescence records the activity of individual enzymes that have been labelled, usually with a fluorophore that changes brightness when the substrate binds or the product is released. The traces show dwell times in each state, and the distribution of those times gives the rates of the individual steps. The results have shown that some enzymes are kinetically heterogeneous, with molecules that differ in their rates over time.

Dill and MacCallum (2012) described the conformational landscape of proteins, and single-molecule measurements can follow the transitions between conformations that the landscape predicts. The fluctuations reveal the ensemble of states that the enzyme samples, and the activity depends on which states are populated.

Time-resolved crystallography captures structures of an enzyme at successive moments after a trigger, for example after the light-induced release of a substrate in a crystal. The method produces a series of snapshots of intermediates that are too short-lived to be seen by conventional methods. The approach extends Kendrew and colleagues' (1960) demonstration that structure reveals function, and it adds time to the structure.

The interpretation of these experiments requires care. Labels can alter the behaviour of the enzyme, the conditions of a crystal may differ from those in solution, and the apparent heterogeneity can reflect measurement noise. Replication, controls with unlabelled enzyme and comparisons with bulk measurements are essential.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the stages of enzymatic reactions that these methods resolve. The results are used to test mechanisms proposed from bulk kinetics and structures, and they often refine them.
