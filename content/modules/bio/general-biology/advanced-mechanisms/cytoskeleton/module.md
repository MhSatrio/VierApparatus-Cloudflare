# Cytoskeleton

The Cell Biology module introduced the three filament systems of the cytoskeleton. This module examines how they work at an advanced level: how filaments are nucleated and assembled, how their dynamics are controlled, how motor proteins generate force along them, how cells use these systems to move and to sense mechanical forces, and what happens when the systems fail.

The chapters begin with the polymerisation of actin and the nucleators that initiate new filaments, then treat the dynamic instability of microtubules, intermediate filaments, motor proteins, cell migration, cellular mechanics and disease. Each chapter emphasises that the cytoskeleton is not a fixed scaffold but a set of dynamic polymers whose behaviour is regulated.

Betzig and colleagues (2006) showed that fluorescent proteins can be imaged at nanometre resolution, and the imaging of cytoskeletal structures has depended on such methods. The chapters refer to this kind of evidence when describing filament organisation.

Readers should also note that the physical properties of polymers, which Anfinsen (1973) connected to the folding of single proteins, apply to the assembly of filaments as well, since filament growth is the addition of subunits whose interactions determine the structure.
