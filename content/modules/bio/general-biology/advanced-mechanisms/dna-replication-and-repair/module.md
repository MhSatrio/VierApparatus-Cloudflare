# DNA Replication and Repair

The genome must be copied accurately once in each cell cycle, and it must be protected against the damage that threatens it every day. Replication and repair are the two processes that achieve this, and they are closely linked, because replication forks that meet damage must be restarted and damage must be removed before it becomes a permanent error. This module examines both processes at an advanced level, from the licensing of origins to the mechanisms that fix each type of damage.

The module begins with replication, covering the control that allows each origin to fire once per cycle, the assembly of the replisome and the handling of stalled forks. It then treats the repair pathways in turn: base excision, nucleotide excision, the repair of double-strand breaks and mismatch repair. The final chapters address how replication and repair fidelity are measured and what happens when they fail.

Lindahl (1993) showed that DNA is chemically unstable, and that spontaneous damage happens continually. Repair is therefore not an occasional process but a constant background activity, and the module frames each pathway in relation to the damage it addresses.

The chapters draw on the replication mechanism from the Molecular Biology module and on the cell cycle described in the Cell Biology module.
