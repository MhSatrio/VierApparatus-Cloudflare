# Gene Expression and Regulation

The Core module described gene regulation through the lac operon, a system with one repressor and a clear on and off switch. Most genes in a eukaryotic cell are controlled by many inputs, and their expression is continuous and variable rather than binary. This module examines regulation at that level of complexity. It asks how the architecture of a promoter and its distant enhancers determines the rate of transcription, why the same gene produces different amounts of RNA in genetically identical cells, and how networks of regulators maintain the states that distinguish cell types.

The chapters begin with the DNA sequences that control transcription and the factors that bind them. They then turn to chromatin, the dynamics of transcription, the role of noise in gene expression, and the networks that link many genes together. The last chapter describes the methods that measure expression in single cells and across populations.

The module draws on the mechanisms of Molecular Biology and on the cell-level signalling of the previous module, which provides many of the inputs to the regulatory network.

Readers should note that the field is changing quickly, and some of the quantitative claims depend on measurements made with methods that are still being refined.
