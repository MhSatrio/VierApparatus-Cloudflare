An enhancer is a regulatory sequence that increases transcription from a promoter, often from a distance of tens or hundreds of kilobases. Enhancers were first identified as elements that could boost expression from a promoter placed next to them in an experiment, regardless of their orientation, which distinguished them from the promoter-proximal elements. The distance is a challenge, because the enhancer must communicate with a promoter that lies far along the chromosome.

The leading explanation is looping. The DNA between the enhancer and the promoter folds, so that the two are brought into contact. Transcription factors bound to the enhancer recruit a coactivator complex, the Mediator, which contacts the general transcription machinery at the promoter. Watson and Crick (1953) described the double helix as a structure whose strands can bend and twist, and the looping of chromatin is a further level of such flexibility, requiring proteins that bridge distant sites.

Cohesin and CTCF are proteins that help define the loops. CTCF binds specific sequences and creates boundaries that limit which enhancers can contact which promoters. The domains defined by these boundaries are called topologically associating domains, and they tend to contain the genes that share regulation. Disruption of a boundary can allow an enhancer to activate a gene that it does not normally regulate, which can cause developmental abnormalities.

The choice of target is therefore constrained by the three-dimensional organisation of the genome. Enhancers do not act on the nearest gene by default. The architecture of the loops and boundaries determines which promoter an enhancer can reach, and the activity of the enhancer depends on the factors bound to it.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the chromatin organisation that underlies these interactions. Enhancers are identified by chromatin marks and by open chromatin assays, tested by deleting them in cells and animals and studied by chromosome conformation methods that detect the contacts between distant regions.
