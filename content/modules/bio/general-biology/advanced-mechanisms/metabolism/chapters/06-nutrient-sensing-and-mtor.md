Cells must match their growth to the supply of nutrients and energy. Two signalling systems do much of this work. The mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1, mTORC1, promotes growth when amino acids, glucose and growth factors are abundant, and it inhibits the degradation of proteins and stimulates their synthesis. AMP-activated protein kinase, AMPK, is activated when the energy state of the cell falls, and it switches on catabolic pathways that produce ATP while suppressing the synthesis of macromolecules.

mTORC1 senses amino acids through a set of lysosomal proteins that bring it to the surface of the lysosome, where it is activated by a small GTPase. Growth factor signals act through the PI3K and AKT pathway, which removes an inhibition on mTORC1. The integration of these signals means that mTORC1 is active only when both the nutrients and the growth signals are present, which prevents the cell from growing when conditions are poor.

AMPK responds to the ratio of AMP and ADP to ATP. When the energy charge falls, the enzyme is phosphorylated and activated, and it phosphorylates targets that increase glucose uptake, fatty acid oxidation and the production of ATP, while reducing processes that consume ATP. AMPK and mTORC1 act in opposition, and their balance sets the metabolic state of the cell.

Lane and Martin (2010) described the energetic constraints that shape cell growth, and these nutrient-sensing pathways are the means by which cells adjust their growth to the energy available. Growth that exceeds the energy supply cannot be sustained, and the sensors ensure that the biosynthetic demand is matched to supply.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the signalling kinases and the cellular processes they control. The pathways are studied by measuring the phosphorylation of their targets, by manipulating nutrient supply and by genetic deletion of the components.
