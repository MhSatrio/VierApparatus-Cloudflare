# Metabolism

The Biochemistry module described the pathways of metabolism one at a time. At the advanced level, the question is how the network behaves as a whole. A cell does not simply contain a set of independent pathways: the same intermediates feed several routes, enzymes are shared, and the flux through one step depends on the state of many others. Understanding metabolism requires methods that quantify how control is distributed across the network and that link metabolic state to signals of growth and nutrition.

The module starts with metabolic control analysis, which asks how much each enzyme controls a flux. It then covers flux balance analysis, which predicts flux distributions from the stoichiometry of a network, and metabolomics, which measures the concentrations of intermediates. The later chapters treat compartmentation, the sensing of nutrients by signalling pathways, the flexibility of metabolism in different conditions and the systems view that integrates these elements.

Lane and Martin (2010) argued that the energy available to a cell constrains its complexity, and metabolism is the process by which that energy is distributed across the network. The module returns to this constraint when considering how cells allocate their metabolic capacity.

Readers should note that flux and concentration are different quantities. A pathway can have a high flux with low concentrations of its intermediates, and the chapters explain how to tell them apart.
