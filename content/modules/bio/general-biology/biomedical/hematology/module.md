# Hematology

Blood is a tissue in which the cells are continually made, used and replaced, and it is the most frequently sampled tissue in medicine. The Immunology and Cell Biology modules described the cells that circulate, and this topic describes how they are produced, what they do and how the laboratory describes them. Haematology links the biology of the stem cell to the numbers and shapes of the cells in a sample of blood.

The chapters follow the lineages from their origin. Haematopoiesis comes first, based on the work of Till and McCulloch (1961), who identified the stem cells that give rise to the blood. Erythropoiesis and the haemoglobin that red cells carry follow, and the disorders of red cells are then described. The white cells and platelets are treated next, followed by haemostasis and the coagulation cascade. The final chapters cover the blood count, the film, transfusion and the laboratory practice on which these depend.

The laboratory describes the blood by counting and measuring cells, and each measure is a sample of a much larger population. Ioannidis (2005) described how a measure taken from a sample can be treated as a precise property of the patient when it carries substantial uncertainty, and Platt (1964) recommended that the measures be interpreted against the alternatives they distinguish. The chapters emphasise the reference values, the variation of the measures and the patterns across several measures.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cell biology of the lineages and their regulation, which underlies each of the measures discussed.
