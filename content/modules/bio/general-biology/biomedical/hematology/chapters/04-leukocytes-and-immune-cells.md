The white cells of the blood are the mobile arm of the immune system, and they are made in the marrow from the same progenitors as the other lineages. Neutrophils are the most numerous, and they are the first responders to bacterial infection, phagocytosing and killing microbes with enzymes and reactive oxygen species. Eosinophils and basophils are fewer and carry granules that act against parasites and drive allergic reactions. Monocytes mature into macrophages in the tissues, and lymphocytes carry the adaptive response, with T cells that act on infected cells and B cells that produce antibodies. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the functions of these cells, and each responds to signals that direct it to the site of need.

The numbers of white cells change with the condition. Neutrophilia, an increase in neutrophils, is typical of bacterial infection and inflammation, while lymphocytosis is typical of some viral infections and of chronic lymphocytic disorders. A fall in neutrophils increases the risk of serious infection, and a very low count is a medical emergency. The pattern of change, rather than the absolute value, often indicates the cause.

The differential count reports the proportion of each type in a sample of a hundred or more cells, and it is read from the blood film, where the cells can be recognised by their nuclear shape and granules. The result is an estimate, and its precision depends on the number of cells counted. Ioannidis (2005) described how a proportion estimated from a small number of cells carries wide uncertainty, and the number counted must be stated.

Platt (1964) recommended a test that could separate the explanations, and the pattern of the differential count is most informative when it distinguishes between infection, inflammation and a primary disorder of the marrow. A single count rarely settles the question, and the sequence of counts over time is more informative.

The white cells therefore give a picture of the immune response in progress, and the reading of that picture depends on the pattern and the context.
