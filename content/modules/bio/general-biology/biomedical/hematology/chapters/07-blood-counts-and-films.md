The automated blood count is the most frequently performed laboratory test. The analyser draws a small volume of blood and counts the cells as they pass through an aperture, measuring their electrical impedance, which reflects their volume, and their light scatter, which reflects their internal structure. The red cells, white cells and platelets are separated by their size and their signals, and the counts are reported with the haemoglobin, the haematocrit and the indices. Till and McCulloch (1961) established the progenitor populations that these cells come from, and the count is a snapshot of the output of the marrow.

The indices describe the cells. The mean corpuscular volume is derived from the cell volume measurement, and the red cell distribution width describes the spread of sizes. The haemoglobin and the haematocrit are measured directly or calculated from the other values. The indices depend on the instrument, and each analyser has its own calibration, so that the same blood may give slightly different results on different machines.

The automated count has limits. Cells that are abnormal in shape, such as fragmented red cells, nucleated red cells or immature white cells, may be counted incorrectly, and aggregated platelets may be counted as single cells or missed. Ioannidis (2005) described how a result that appears precise may hide an error that the instrument cannot detect, and the flags that the analyser raises for suspicious results must be taken seriously.

The blood film confirms the automated result. A drop of blood is spread on a glass slide, dried, stained and examined under the microscope. The film shows the morphology of the cells, and it is the means by which abnormal cells are identified and an automated flag is resolved. Examination of the film is performed by a trained observer, and its result depends on the number of cells examined.

Platt (1964) recommended a test that could show the result to be wrong, and the comparison of the automated count with the film is such a test, since disagreement reveals the error and its cause.
