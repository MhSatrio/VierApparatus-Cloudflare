Anaemia is a reduction in the amount of haemoglobin in the blood, and it has many causes. The classification follows the mechanism. Anaemia from reduced production occurs when the marrow does not make enough red cells, which may follow a deficiency of iron, vitamin B12 or folate, or a failure of the marrow itself. Anaemia from blood loss follows haemorrhage, which removes red cells faster than they can be replaced. Anaemia from destruction, or haemolysis, occurs when red cells are removed from the circulation prematurely. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the lifespan of the red cell and the processes that remove it, and each mechanism alters a different part of this cycle.

Inherited disorders of haemoglobin have been studied in detail. Some involve a change in the sequence of a globin chain that alters the fold of the haemoglobin molecule. Anfinsen (1973) showed that the folding of a protein determines its properties, and a substitution that destabilises the fold can cause the haemoglobin to precipitate within the cell, which damages the membrane and shortens the red cell's life. Others involve a reduced synthesis of one globin chain, which leaves an excess of the partner chain that cannot form stable tetramers.

The laboratory classifies anaemia by the red cell indices. The mean corpuscular volume describes the size of the cells, and the mean corpuscular haemoglobin describes their content. Small cells with low haemoglobin content suggest iron deficiency or some inherited disorders, while large cells suggest a defect in DNA synthesis. The indices narrow the differential diagnosis, but they overlap, and the classification is a probability rather than a determination.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a classification that fits a pattern can be applied to cases that do not fit it, and the criteria of the classification should be stated. Platt (1964) recommended comparing alternatives, and the differential diagnosis of an anaemia tests the alternative mechanisms against the indices and the further tests that distinguish them.
