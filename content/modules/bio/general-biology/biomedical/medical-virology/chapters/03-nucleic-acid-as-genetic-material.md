Before 1952 it was not certain whether the genetic material of a virus was its protein or its nucleic acid. Hershey and Chase (1952) addressed this by labelling the two components of bacteriophage with different radioactive isotopes. The protein coat was labelled with sulphur, which is present in some amino acids but not in DNA, and the DNA was labelled with phosphorus, which is present in DNA but not in most protein. The labelled phages were allowed to infect bacteria, and the bacteria were then separated from the empty coats by agitation.

The result was clear. The phosphorus label entered the bacteria and was found in the progeny phages, while most of the sulphur label remained outside the bacteria with the empty coats. The nucleic acid had entered the cell and directed the production of new particles, and the protein had not. Hershey and Chase (1952) therefore concluded that the nucleic acid carries the genetic information, and this interpretation was an inference from the distribution of the labels.

Watson and Crick (1953) described the structure of DNA in the same year, and the two results together established that the genetic material is a nucleic acid with a defined structure. The structure suggested how the molecule could be copied, which explained how the progeny inherit the information of the parent.

The experiment is a model of careful inference. A single labelling method can be misleading if the label is not specific or if the separation of the components is incomplete, and the authors designed their controls to address both problems. Ioannidis (2005) described how a conclusion can be stronger than the evidence when the controls are not reported, and the strength of this experiment lies in the separation of the two labels and their clear distribution.

Platt (1964) recommended an experiment whose outcome can discriminate between alternatives, and the experiment of Hershey and Chase did exactly this, since the two hypotheses predicted different locations for the labels.

The principle that nucleic acid is the genetic material is the foundation of the molecular diagnosis of viruses, which detects the molecule that carries the information.
