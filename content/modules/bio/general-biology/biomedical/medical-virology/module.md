# Medical Virology

Viruses are obligate parasites that replicate inside host cells, and their small genomes depend on the machinery of the cell. The Molecular Biology and Gene Expression topics described the replication of DNA and RNA, and viruses exploit these processes in ways that define the disease they cause. Medical virology applies this understanding to the infections of patients: how the virus enters the cell and produces its progeny, how the host responds and how the laboratory detects the virus and the antibodies against it.

The chapters begin with the structure and genomes of viruses, and the replication cycles that determine what damage follows infection. Hershey and Chase (1952) showed that the nucleic acid of a bacteriophage carries the genetic information for the production of new viruses, and this principle underlies the replication of all viruses. The diagnosis of viral infection by nucleic acid detection and by serology is then described, followed by antiviral drugs, emerging viruses, surveillance and the evaluation of vaccines.

Virology is a field in which the interpretation of a test depends on the timing of infection and on the immune response, and the same virus may be detected in people with and without disease. Ioannidis (2005) described how the detection of a pathogen can be taken as the cause of disease, and Platt (1964) recommended tests that could separate the alternatives. The chapters emphasise the careful linking of a result to its meaning.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the host cell processes that viruses use, and the interaction between virus and host determines the course of infection.
