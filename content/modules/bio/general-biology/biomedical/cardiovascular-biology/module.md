# Cardiovascular Biology

The heart and the blood vessels form a single system whose task is to deliver oxygen and nutrients to every tissue and to remove their wastes. The Cell Biology and Advanced Cell Signaling modules described the contraction of muscle and the signalling of cells, and this topic applies them to the heart and vessels. It also connects the cardiovascular system to the blood cells and inflammation described in the Hematology and Human Pathology modules, since the diseases of the vessels involve all of them.

The chapters move from the structure and contraction of the heart to the regulation of the vessels, the disease processes that damage them and the evidence used to judge risk. The cardiac muscle cell and its contraction come first, followed by the endothelium that controls the diameter of the vessels. Atherosclerosis, described by Ross (1999) as an inflammatory disease, is then treated in detail, followed by the electrical basis of arrhythmia, the remodelling of the heart in failure and the evidence on which cardiovascular risk is assessed.

The field has a long record of observational findings that did not survive controlled testing, and the chapters emphasise the difference between a risk factor that predicts disease and one that causes it. Ioannidis (2005) described how such associations can mislead, and Platt (1964) recommended tests that discriminate between the explanations that a finding could support.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cell biology of muscle and the endothelium on which the chapters build.
