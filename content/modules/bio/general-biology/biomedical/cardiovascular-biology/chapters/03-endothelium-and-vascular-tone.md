The endothelium is a single layer of cells lining every blood vessel, and it is an active regulator rather than a passive barrier. It senses the forces and the chemicals in the blood and responds by releasing mediators into the wall. Nitric oxide is the most important of these, and it diffuses into the smooth muscle beneath, where it activates an enzyme that lowers calcium and relaxes the muscle. The vessel dilates, and blood flow increases where it is needed. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the signalling by which a small molecule of this kind acts on its target, and the endothelium uses this route to match the supply of blood to the demand of the tissue.

Blood flow exerts a shear stress on the endothelium, the frictional force of the moving fluid along the surface. The endothelial cells sense this force through mechanosensitive proteins and change their gene expression and their release of mediators in response. Steady laminar flow promotes a protective pattern, with the release of nitric oxide and the expression of anti-inflammatory genes. Disturbed flow, found at branches and bends, promotes a pattern that is pro-inflammatory, and this difference explains why atherosclerosis develops in some regions of the arterial tree and not others.

Ross (1999) described atherosclerosis as an inflammatory disease that begins with the injury and activation of the endothelium. The loss of protective signalling lets leukocytes adhere to the wall and migrate into it, and lipids accumulate in the intima. The endothelium is therefore both the regulator of vascular tone and the first site of the disease, and its dysfunction is an early marker of the process.

Measures of endothelial function are used clinically, and the response to a stimulus such as an increase in flow is measured to test the capacity of the vessel to dilate. Ioannidis (2005) described how a measure that differs between groups may reflect several causes, and Platt (1964) recommended tests that could separate the explanations, such as the response to a specific inhibitor of nitric oxide synthesis.

The endothelium is the interface between the blood and the vessel, and the health of the vessel depends on the balance of its signals.
