A clinical result is only as good as the measurement that produced it, and laboratories control their measurements continuously. Internal quality control runs samples of known value with each batch, and the result is plotted against control limits. A result that falls outside the limits, or that shows a trend, signals a problem with the method, the reagent or the instrument, and the batch is investigated before results are released. Platt (1964) argued that a process is controlled when it is designed to reveal its own failures, and the control material is exactly such a design.

External quality assessment compares the laboratory with others that measure the same sample. It reveals bias, a systematic difference from the consensus, which internal control cannot detect when the control material shares the same bias as the patient sample. The comparison is made against a reference value, and the laboratory's bias is reported with its uncertainty. Ioannidis (2005) described how a method that agrees with itself can still be wrong, and the external comparison is the test of that agreement.

Imprecision and bias together define the uncertainty of a measurement. Imprecision is the scatter of repeated results, and it is estimated from the variation of the control material. Bias is the difference from the true value, and it is estimated from the comparison. The combined uncertainty is reported with the result, typically as an interval, and it determines whether a change between two results is real.

The uncertainty matters for interpretation. A change of a few per cent may be within the imprecision of the method and carry no clinical meaning, while a larger change may be real. Anfinsen (1973) showed that the molecules measured have properties that vary with their environment, and the variation of the sample itself adds to the uncertainty.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the biological variation of the analytes, which sets a limit on what any measurement can tell. The laboratory and the clinician should both state the uncertainty so that the result is judged for what it is.
