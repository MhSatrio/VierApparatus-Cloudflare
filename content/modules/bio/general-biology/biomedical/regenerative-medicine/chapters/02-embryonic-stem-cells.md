Thomson et al. (1998) reported the derivation of human embryonic stem cell lines from the inner cell mass of blastocysts, and the lines they established could be maintained in culture while remaining pluripotent. The inner cell mass is removed from the embryo and plated on a supportive layer of feeder cells or on a defined matrix, where the cells proliferate as colonies. The cells retain their capacity to form all three germ layers, which was shown by their differentiation in culture and in grafts. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the developmental signals that maintain the inner cell mass, and the culture conditions must reproduce those signals in vitro.

Maintenance requires careful control of the culture. The cells must be passaged before they become crowded, since dense colonies differentiate, and the medium must contain the factors that suppress spontaneous differentiation. Karyotypes must be monitored, because the culture can select for cells with chromosomal abnormalities that grow faster, and an abnormal karyotype may change the behaviour of the line. The characterisation of a line therefore includes its markers, its differentiation potential and its genome.

Establishing a line requires several independent demonstrations. The cells must express the markers of pluripotency, form teratomas or differentiate into the three germ layers, and maintain a normal karyotype over extended passage. Ioannidis (2005) described how a set of markers can be taken as proof of a property that only the functional tests establish, and the characterisation should include the functional tests as well as the markers.

The source of these cells raises ethical questions. The derivation destroys the embryo, and the consent of the donors, the status of the embryo and the uses of the lines are matters of public debate and of regulation that differ between countries. These questions are separate from the scientific question of whether the cells are useful, and both must be addressed.

Platt (1964) recommended a test that could show a claim to be wrong, and the functional test of differentiation is the means of showing that a line is pluripotent and not merely marked as such.

The derivation of embryonic stem cells established the material from which many later regenerative approaches were developed, and the standards it set for characterisation remain in use.
