Contopoulos-Ioannidis et al. (2003) examined highly cited basic science findings and traced how many of them were carried into clinical use. A large proportion of the influential findings had not been followed by clinical application. The gap between laboratory promise and clinical benefit is therefore not random: it reflects the quality of the evidence that the field relies on at each stage.

Small and early studies are a principal cause. A basic finding that is established in a few cells or a few animals may be strongly positive, but the effect is often overestimated, because the first studies to report a result are those in which the chance effect was largest. When larger and better controlled studies are performed, the effect shrinks or disappears. Ioannidis (2005) described this pattern, and it explains why a finding can look convincing at one stage and fail at the next.

Selective reporting contributes. Experiments that give a favourable result are more likely to be published and cited, and the unfavourable results remain unseen. The apparent strength of the evidence is therefore greater than the true strength, and translation proceeds on the basis of a biased picture of the literature.

Overconfidence in mechanism is a further cause. A finding that fits a plausible biological explanation is accepted more readily, even when the evidence for the explanation is incomplete. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the networks that make such explanations attractive, and the attraction is a reason for caution rather than a substitute for evidence. Platt (1964) recommended testing the alternative explanations of a result, and a mechanism that has not been tested against its alternatives remains a hypothesis.

The measures that narrow the gap are the ones that address these causes: larger studies, prespecified analyses, the reporting of negative results and the testing of mechanisms against alternatives. The gap is narrowed most by requiring the same standards of evidence at the basic stage as at the clinical stage.

The translational gap is a consequence of weak evidence passed forward, and closing it depends on strengthening the evidence at every stage.
