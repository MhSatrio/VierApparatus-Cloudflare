A genotype may predict an outcome without improving it. A variant that is associated with toxicity is a prediction, and the use of the genotype is justified only if a strategy guided by the genotype produces a better outcome than the usual strategy. The distinction is central, because many pharmacogenomic associations are real but do not change the decisions clinicians would make. Evans and Relling (1999) argued that the clinical value of a genetic test must be judged by its effect on treatment and on outcome, not only by its biological correlation.

The trial that tests a genotype-guided strategy compares patients who are genotyped and treated according to the result with patients who are treated by usual care. Randomisation allocates patients to the two strategies, so that the groups differ only in whether the genotype is used. The outcome must be a patient outcome, such as the occurrence of a serious adverse event or the achievement of a response, and the trial must be large enough to detect the difference that the strategy is expected to produce.

Pharmacogenomic claims have often been made from small studies with a single candidate gene, and their results have not replicated. Ioannidis (2005) described how the winner's curse, in which the effect in the first study is larger than the true effect, inflates the apparent association, and the replication in independent cohorts is the remedy. The evidence for a genotype should therefore include replication and a trial of its use.

Platt (1964) recommended a test that could separate the alternatives, and the trial of a genotype-guided strategy tests whether the genotype changes the decision in a way that matters, rather than whether it correlates with the outcome.

The reporting of such a trial must include the genotype method, the rules that translated it into action, the proportion of patients in each category, the adherence to the strategy and the outcomes in each group, so that the strategy can be repeated and judged.

Genotype is useful for prescribing only when the strategy it guides is shown to be better, and the evidence for that is a comparison of outcomes rather than a correlation.
