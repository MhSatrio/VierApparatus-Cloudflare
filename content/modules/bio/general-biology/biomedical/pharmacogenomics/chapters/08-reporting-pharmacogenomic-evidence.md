A pharmacogenomic report must allow another group to repeat the study and to judge whether the association or the recommendation holds. Ioannidis (2005) described how the failure to replicate many genetic associations arose from reports that omitted the details needed to test them, and the report is the means of avoiding that failure. The population studied must be described with its size, its ancestry, the criteria for inclusion and the number of patients who were excluded, because the ancestry determines both the allele frequencies and the risk of confounding.

The genotyping must be described in full: the variants tested, the method, the quality controls, the call rate and the concordance with a reference set. Without these, a reader cannot judge whether a missing association reflects the biology or the assay. The phenotype must be defined precisely, including the outcome, its time of measurement and how it was assessed, and the translation of genotype into metaboliser or response categories must be stated with its rules.

The statistical analysis must be specified, including the model, the adjustment for ancestry and other covariates, the correction for multiple testing and the effect size with its confidence interval. Platt (1964) recommended that a claim be stated so that it can be tested, and an effect size with its interval gives a reader the means to judge whether the result is consistent with the claimed benefit.

A clinical recommendation must state the level of evidence on which it rests. A recommendation supported by a trial of genotype-guided treatment is stronger than one supported only by an association, and the report must make this distinction explicit. Evans and Relling (1999) argued that the clinical use of a pharmacogenomic test should be justified by its effect on outcome, and the level of evidence is a measure of how well that justification is established.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the biology of the variants reported, and the mechanism of an effect strengthens its plausibility, although it does not replace the evidence from patients.

A pharmacogenomic report is a set of decisions made visible, so that another group can repeat them and judge what they show.
