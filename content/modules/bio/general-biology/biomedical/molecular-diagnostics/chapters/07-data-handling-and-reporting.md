Every molecular result must be traceable to the sample from which it came, the instrument and reagents used and the person who performed the test. Without this traceability, an error cannot be traced or corrected, and a result cannot be linked to its controls. The laboratory record therefore holds the sample identity, the extraction, the assay, the lot numbers, the controls and the raw data, and the record must be kept for the period the regulations require. Saiki et al. (1988) established the amplification on which such assays depend, and small changes in conditions can alter a result, so the record of those conditions is what allows a result to be explained.

The report states the result and its meaning in terms that the clinician can use. A positive result should state the target detected, the level if quantitative and the limits of the test. A negative result should state the limit of detection and the sample type, so that the reader can judge its completeness. Ioannidis (2005) described how a report that omits its limits is accepted more readily than it deserves, and the limits are part of the result.

Uncertain or unexpected findings require careful communication. A result that is near the threshold, that is from a sample of poor quality or that reveals a finding unrelated to the test request must be reported with its uncertainty, and the clinician should be told what action is needed. A finding that is not understood should not be presented as a diagnosis, and a recommendation for further testing is often the appropriate response.

Privacy is a legal and ethical obligation. Molecular data identify the patient and may reveal information about relatives, and their storage, access and disclosure must follow the rules that protect them. Access is limited to those with a clinical need, and results are transmitted by secure means.

Platt (1964) recommended a report that states a conclusion in a form that can be tested, and a molecular report that gives the result with its controls and its limits allows a clinician or an auditor to check whether the conclusion is justified.

The record and the report are the means by which a molecular result becomes a decision that can be trusted, and each must be complete enough to support that trust.
