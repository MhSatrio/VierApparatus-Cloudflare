A molecular result is produced at the end of a long chain of steps, and the steps before the analysis often determine its outcome more than the analysis itself. These preanalytical variables include the time between removal of the tissue and its fixation, the choice of fixative, the duration of fixation, the proportion of tumour in the section and the method of extraction. Each can change the amount and quality of the nucleic acid, and a molecular test applied to poorly handled material gives a result that is not comparable with one from well-handled material.

The time to fixation is a particularly important variable. After removal, the tissue continues to change, and the RNA in particular degrades rapidly. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the turnover of cellular molecules that continues in tissue after its blood supply is lost, and the delay before fixation allows this turnover to alter the molecules measured. The delay is recorded, and the acceptable limit is set for each assay.

The quality of the nucleic acid is measured before the test. The concentration, the purity and the integrity of the extract are checked, and a sample that falls below the threshold is reported as unsuitable rather than tested. Saiki et al. (1988) established the amplification on which these tests rely, and an inhibited reaction can fail, so a poor extract may give a false negative because the reaction fails rather than because the target is absent.

Quality is verified by controls and by proficiency testing. Positive and negative controls are run with each batch, and the laboratory participates in external schemes in which it measures samples of known composition and is compared with other laboratories. The results are recorded, and any failure is investigated before results are released. Ioannidis (2005) described how a laboratory that does not test its own accuracy against an external standard can drift without detection.

Platt (1964) recommended that a process be designed to reveal its own failures, and the controls and proficiency testing are the means by which the laboratory detects its errors before they reach a patient.
