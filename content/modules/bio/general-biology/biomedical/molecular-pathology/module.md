# Molecular Pathology

Histopathology reads disease from the appearance of tissue, and molecular pathology reads it from the molecules within the tissue. The two are complementary: a tumour that looks the same under the microscope may carry different mutations, and those mutations determine how it behaves and how it responds to treatment. The Human Pathology module described the morphology of disease, and this topic describes the molecular methods that refine the diagnosis and the quality controls that must surround them.

The chapters follow the path of a molecular diagnosis. The molecular basis of diagnosis comes first, with the double helix as the substrate of the test. Detection of mutations by PCR and sequencing, and of copy number and fusions, are then described, followed by expression profiling and protein markers. The heterogeneity of tumours and the use of blood as a source of tumour DNA are treated next, and the last chapters address preanalytical variables, quality and the reporting of a molecular result.

The molecular result is only as reliable as the sample and the method, and the number of steps between the patient and the report is large. Ioannidis (2005) described how a result that depends on many steps can be affected by any one of them, and each step is controlled. Platt (1964) recommended a test that could show the result to be wrong, and the validation and the controls are the means by which a molecular diagnosis is made testable.

Vogelstein et al. (2013) described the landscape of genetic changes in cancer, which is the context in which the molecular tests are interpreted.
