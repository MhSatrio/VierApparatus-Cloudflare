Biosafety is organised into four levels, which increase in the precautions required as the hazard of the agent increases. Biosafety level one covers agents that are not known to cause disease in healthy adults, and it requires good microbiological practice, such as handwashing, the restriction of eating and drinking in the laboratory and the decontamination of work surfaces. Biosafety level two covers agents that cause disease of moderate severity and whose transmission is limited, and it adds controlled access, biological safety cabinets for procedures that produce aerosols and the training of staff.

Biosafety level three is used for agents that can cause serious or lethal disease through inhalation, and it requires controlled access, a separate suite with directional airflow and the use of respirators and biological safety cabinets for all manipulations. Biosafety level four is reserved for agents that cause severe disease for which there is no treatment or vaccine, and it requires full-body protective clothing or a sealed suit, dedicated air supply and the highest level of physical containment.

The level is chosen by risk assessment. The assessment considers the pathogenicity of the agent, the route of infection, the availability of treatment, the infectious dose and the procedures to be carried out. Finlay and Falkow (1997) described how pathogens achieve infection by entry and survival in host cells, and these properties determine the risk to laboratory workers, since aerosols can carry infectious material to the respiratory tract.

Containment must match the risk. An agent handled at too high a level imposes unnecessary cost and may discourage the research, while an agent handled at too low a level endangers workers and the community. The assessment is reviewed when the agent or the procedure changes, for example when a gene is introduced that increases virulence.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the microbiological practices and cell culture procedures that the containment levels govern. The assessment, the training and the records of each laboratory are the means by which containment is maintained.
