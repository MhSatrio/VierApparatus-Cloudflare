# Biosafety and Research Ethics

Biological research carries obligations to the people who do it, to the public and to the organisms that are studied. Some agents can cause serious disease in the laboratory, as the pathogen strategies described in the Host-Pathogen Interactions module show, and the protections against them must be matched to the risk. Other obligations concern the welfare of human participants and research animals, and the integrity of the data on which all research depends.

The module covers biosafety first, including the containment levels and the assessment of risk. It then turns to research ethics, including consent and the welfare of animals, and to the integrity of data and the responsibilities of authorship and reporting. The principles are presented as the working standards that investigators are expected to follow, not as abstract rules.

Ioannidis (2005) showed that unreliable findings are costly, because they waste resources and can mislead the people who depend on the research. Integrity in the reporting of data is therefore an ethical obligation as well as a scientific one, and the module links the two.

The chapters are general. Local regulations, institutional committees and national law set the detailed requirements, which investigators must follow.
