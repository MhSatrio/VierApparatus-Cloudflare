Preparative chromatography isolates a compound in quantity, so that it can be studied or used. Its aims differ from those of analysis. The priority is the yield and the purity of the target, not the precision of a measurement, and the column is loaded with much more material than an analytical column would tolerate. Loading a large amount reduces resolution, because the peaks broaden and overlap, and the trade-off between the amount loaded and the purity achieved must be found for each separation.

Scale-up is done by increasing the column size in proportion to the amount of material, while keeping the flow rate per unit of cross-section and the gradient shape constant. A separation optimised on a small column can fail on a large one if the scaling is not done carefully, and a pilot run on a small column is usually made first. Anfinsen (1973) showed that the folded structure of a protein determines its behaviour, and a protein that aggregates at high concentration may fail to separate at the loads needed for preparation.

Fractions are collected across the peak, and the fractions that contain the target are pooled. The pooling criteria determine the balance between yield and purity, and they must be set in advance rather than chosen by the appearance of the chromatogram. The pooled material is then checked for identity and purity by an independent method.

Confirmation is essential. A preparative peak may contain a compound that co-elutes with the target, and the identity must be established by mass, by activity or by a specific assay. Purity is then assessed by a method that differs from the separation, such as electrophoresis for a protein purified by chromatography. The checks must be done on every preparation, not only on the first.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a single preparation can be unrepresentative, and the reproducibility of the isolation should be shown by repeating it on independent batches.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cellular sources of the compounds isolated. The protocol should record the load, the column, the gradient, the pooling criteria, the yield and the checks performed.
