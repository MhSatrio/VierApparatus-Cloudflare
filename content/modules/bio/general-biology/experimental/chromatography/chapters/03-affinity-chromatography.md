Affinity chromatography captures a protein by a specific interaction. A ligand that binds the target is immobilised on the resin, and the protein in a crude extract binds to it while most other proteins pass through. The bound protein is then released by a change in conditions that disrupts the interaction, such as a high concentration of a competing ligand, a change of pH or a denaturant. The method gives high purity in a single step, because the specificity of the ligand is high.

The ligand may be a natural substrate, an inhibitor, an antibody or a metal chelate. Tags are often used: a short peptide sequence added to the protein by genetic engineering, such as a run of histidine residues that binds nickel, allows purification of many proteins whose natural ligands are unknown. The tag must be placed where it does not interfere with the folding or activity of the protein, and it may need to be removed afterwards. Anfinsen (1973) showed that the folding of a protein is determined by its sequence, and a tag added to the sequence can alter the folding if placed carelessly.

Nonspecific binding reduces purity. Some contaminants bind the resin or the ligand through charge or hydrophobic interactions, and they co-elute with the target. Washing with increasing salt or mild detergent removes weakly bound contaminants, and the wash must be tested so that the target is not lost too.

Elution must release the protein without damaging it. Harsh conditions can denature the protein and lose its activity, and the recovery of activity must be measured after elution. Gentle elution with a competing ligand is preferred when the activity is to be studied.

Ioannidis (2005) described how claims based on tagged proteins can be affected by the tag, and the activity and structure of the tagged protein should be compared with the untagged one where possible.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the binding interactions that the method exploits. The protocol should record the ligand, the tag, the wash and elution conditions and the yield and purity at each step.
