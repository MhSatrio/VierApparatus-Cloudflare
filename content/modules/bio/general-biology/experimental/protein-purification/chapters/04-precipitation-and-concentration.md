Precipitation concentrates a protein and removes a proportion of the contaminants from a large volume of extract. Ammonium sulphate is the most used salt. At high concentration it draws water away from the protein surface, reducing the solubility of proteins and causing them to aggregate and fall out of solution. Different proteins precipitate at different salt concentrations, so a stepwise addition, taking the fraction that precipitates within a chosen range, separates the target from some of the contaminants. The fraction is chosen by assay, not by appearance, since the target may be in any fraction.

Anfinsen (1973) showed that the solubility and structure of a protein depend on its environment, and a salt that stabilises the native fold at one concentration can denature it at another. Precipitation is therefore followed by an assay of the redissolved fraction to confirm that the target retains activity. The precipitate is collected by centrifugation, redissolved in a small volume of buffer and the salt is removed by dialysis or by gel filtration.

Ultrafiltration offers a different route to concentration. A membrane with a defined molecular weight cut-off retains proteins above the cut-off while water and small molecules pass through. It concentrates the sample without adding a salt, which avoids the need to remove one, and it is useful for samples that are sensitive to precipitation. The membrane must not adsorb the protein, and the loss by adsorption must be measured.

The total protein is measured after each step, using the assay described in the previous chapter. The protein concentration after precipitation is compared with the starting extract, and the loss is recorded. A precipitation that recovers the target with a large proportion of the contaminants removed has done its work.

Ioannidis (2005) warned that a step that is judged only by the appearance of a pellet can lose the target without notice, and the activity should be measured in every fraction. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the solubility of proteins in different salts.
