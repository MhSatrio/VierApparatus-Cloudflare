# Spectrophotometry

The Laboratory Instrumentation module described the spectrophotometer and the Beer-Lambert law. This topic is about the assays built on those principles: the measurements of nucleic acids, proteins and enzyme activities that are performed in almost every biology laboratory. Each assay has a range, an interference profile and a set of controls, and the reliability of a result depends on knowing all three.

The chapters cover the absorbance assays for nucleic acids, the protein assays, including the dye-binding method introduced by Bradford (1976), the continuous enzyme assays that follow a change in absorbance, the fluorescence assays, the spectroscopic study of structure and the common pitfalls that affect every assay. Bradford's method, for example, depends on the composition of the protein, and its response varies between proteins, which must be remembered when a standard is chosen.

Ioannidis (2005) described how measurements that are not validated can mislead, and a spectrophotometric assay is only as reliable as its standard, its blank and its linear range. Each chapter gives the controls that the assay requires.

Readers should expect the assays to differ in sensitivity and cost, and the choice should match the question and the sample.
