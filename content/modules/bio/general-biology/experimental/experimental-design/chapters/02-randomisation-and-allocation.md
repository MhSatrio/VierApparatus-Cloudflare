A comparison between treatment groups is informative only if the groups differ in the treatment and in nothing else. Systematic differences can arise when the investigator chooses which samples receive which treatment, because the choice may be influenced by the appearance of the sample, by the order of processing or by expectations about the outcome. Random allocation breaks the link between these influences and the treatment, so that any remaining difference between groups is due to chance, which can be quantified.

Randomisation must be carried out with a documented procedure. A random number generator or a table of random numbers is used to produce the sequence of allocations before the experiment begins, and the sequence is concealed from those who assign samples. Allocation by alternation, by the order of arrival or by convenience is not randomisation, and it can introduce bias even when the investigator intends to be fair.

Blocking groups samples into sets that share a source of variation, such as the day of the experiment or the batch of reagent, and randomises the treatments within each block. The method removes the variation between blocks from the comparison, which increases power. Stratification applies the same idea to characteristics of the sample, such as sex or age in animal studies, and it ensures that the groups are balanced on those characteristics.

Randomisation is not always possible. Some treatments cannot be assigned, such as exposures that individuals choose, and some comparisons are made between naturally occurring groups. In those cases the investigator must measure and adjust for the differences, and the conclusions are weaker because the confounding factors may be unmeasured.

Ioannidis (2005) listed flexibility in the allocation and analysis of samples among the sources of bias that produce unreliable findings, and random allocation is one of the simplest protections against it. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the biological variability that blocking and stratification can control. The choice of allocation scheme should be recorded in the protocol before the study begins.
