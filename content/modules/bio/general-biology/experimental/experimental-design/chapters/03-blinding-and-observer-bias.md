Randomisation protects against bias in the allocation of samples, but bias can enter again in the measurement and interpretation of outcomes. An investigator who knows which samples received the active treatment may score an ambiguous result in its favour, may take more care in measuring one group, or may treat a borderline sample differently. Such effects can be substantial, and they are especially likely when the outcome requires judgement, as in histology, behavioural scoring or the reading of images.

Blinding conceals the treatment from those who would otherwise be influenced. Single-blind designs hide the allocation from the measurer, and double-blind designs hide it from both the measurer and the investigator who interacts with the subjects. Triple-blind designs add a blind analyst. The more people who are blinded, the less scope there is for bias, though blinding is not always complete, because the treatment may have visible effects that reveal the allocation.

Practical methods include coding samples with neutral labels, using a third party to prepare and label treatments, and recording the code key separately until all measurements are complete. The key should be unsealed only after the data are locked. A method that is convenient but reveals the allocation provides little protection.

Blinding is not always possible. A surgical procedure or a behavioural intervention cannot be hidden from the person who performs it, and in such cases the outcome measures should be as objective as possible and should be made by someone who does not know the group.

Platt (1964) recommended that investigators design experiments to exclude the influence of expectation, and blinding is one of the means of doing so. Ioannidis (2005) listed the flexibility of measurement and interpretation among the causes of unreliable findings, and blinding addresses this directly. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the methods of measurement whose objectivity is at issue. The protocol should state the blinding procedure and report whether it was achieved.
