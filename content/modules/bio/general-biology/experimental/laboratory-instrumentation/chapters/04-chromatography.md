Chromatography separates the components of a mixture by their differing affinity for a stationary phase, through which a mobile phase flows. A component that binds the stationary phase strongly moves slowly, while one that prefers the mobile phase moves quickly. The time a component takes to emerge from the column, its retention time, is characteristic of the compound under the conditions used, and the separation of two components depends on the difference in their retention and on the efficiency of the column.

Liquid chromatography is used for most biological molecules. Reversed-phase columns, with a nonpolar stationary phase, separate molecules by their hydrophobicity, which is the same property discussed in the Water module. Ion-exchange columns separate by charge, and size-exclusion columns separate by molecular size, as the protein purification described in the Biochemistry module uses them. The choice of phase depends on the property that distinguishes the components.

Resolution measures how well two peaks are separated. It depends on the difference in retention, the width of the peaks and the efficiency of the column. Peaks that overlap cannot be quantified accurately, and the conditions must be adjusted to separate them. Identification by retention time alone is ambiguous, because different compounds can co-elute, so the identity is usually confirmed with another method, such as mass spectrometry.

Quantification requires calibration. The peak area is proportional to the amount of the component within the linear range of the detector, and a calibration curve made from standards converts the area into a concentration. Internal standards correct for variation in injection and detection. Without calibration, the apparent amounts are relative and cannot be compared between samples.

Ioannidis (2005) described how results depend on the choices made in data analysis, and the integration of chromatographic peaks, the choice of baseline and the rejection of outliers should be specified in advance.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the biomolecules whose separation is needed in cell biology. Chromatography is practised with standards, controls and validated methods, and the conditions are recorded for each analysis.
