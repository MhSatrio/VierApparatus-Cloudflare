# Laboratory Instrumentation

Biological research depends on instruments that convert a sample into a number. A balance measures mass, a spectrophotometer measures how much light a solution absorbs, a centrifuge separates particles by their size and density, and a mass spectrometer measures the mass of molecules. Each instrument carries assumptions and limits, and a result is only as trustworthy as the instrument that produced it and the care with which it was used.

The module treats the main classes of instrument in turn. For each one it explains the principle, the calibration and the common errors. The later chapters cover chromatography, mass spectrometry and electrophoresis, the imaging instruments that Hell and Wichmann and Betzig and colleagues developed, and the practice of validation and quality control that makes instrumental data reliable.

Instruments often seem more objective than they are. A reading is a physical signal interpreted through a calibration, and the chain from signal to conclusion includes several steps that can introduce error. The module emphasises the checks at each step.

Readers should consult the manufacturer's documentation and the laboratory's standard operating procedures for each instrument, since the details vary between models.
