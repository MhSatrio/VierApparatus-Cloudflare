A cell culture result cannot be interpreted without the conditions that produced it, and many published findings in culture have failed to replicate because these conditions were not reported. Ioannidis (2005) described how unreported variables make results impossible to test, and the cell culture report should state every variable that could plausibly change the outcome.

The cell line is identified by its name, its source, its catalogue or repository number and the date it was obtained. The authentication profile and the mycoplasma test are reported with their dates. The passage number at the time of the experiment is stated, since the cells change with passage, as Hayflick and Moorhead (1961) showed for normal cells, and the experiment should specify the passage range used.

The medium is specified in full, including the basal formulation, the serum or defined supplements, their source and lot, the antibiotics if any and the glutamine or other additives. The seeding density, the vessel and the time from seeding to treatment are recorded, because each affects the state of the cells. The atmosphere, the temperature and the incubator conditions are stated.

Replicates require the same care as in other methods. Experiments performed on the same passage, in the same plate, are technical replicates, and experiments from independent thaws or independent cultures are biological replicates. Platt (1964) argued that a conclusion is strengthened when it has been tested in a way that could have failed, and independent biological replicates provide that test.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cellular behaviour that the culture is meant to reproduce, and the report should state where the culture might depart from the cell in the body. Each claim should be limited to the system that produced it.
