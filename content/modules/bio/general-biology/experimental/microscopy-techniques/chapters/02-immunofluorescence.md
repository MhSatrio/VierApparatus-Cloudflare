Immunofluorescence uses antibodies to locate a protein in fixed cells. A primary antibody binds the target, and a fluorescently labelled secondary antibody binds the primary, which amplifies the signal and allows the same secondary antibody to be used with many primaries. In direct immunofluorescence the primary carries the fluorophore itself, which is simpler but less sensitive. The indirect method is standard because each secondary can be chosen for its host species and its fluorophore, and the signal of a weak target can be enhanced.

Specificity is the central problem. An antibody that binds its target in a blot may bind other proteins in a cell, and the cross-reactivity may be visible only in the context of the cell. Controls establish specificity. A negative control lacks the primary antibody and shows the background of the secondary. A genetic control, in which the target is deleted or knocked down, shows whether the signal disappears with the protein. A competition control, in which the antibody is pre-absorbed with its antigen, shows whether binding is saturable. Betzig and colleagues (2006) emphasised the value of labelling a defined molecule, and the controls ensure that the label is the molecule intended.

The pattern of staining must be interpreted with care. Fluorescence that appears in a compartment may reflect the true location of the protein, or it may reflect aggregation or the fixation artefacts described in the previous chapter. Co-labelling with a marker of the compartment, and comparison with a second antibody against a different epitope of the same protein, give stronger evidence.

Ioannidis (2005) described how the claims that depend on a single reagent are fragile. Antibody validation, including the controls above and the use of more than one antibody, is an essential part of the method.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the subcellular structures commonly labelled. The protocol should record the antibody source, its dilution, the controls and the imaging settings, which are needed for anyone who wants to repeat the experiment.
