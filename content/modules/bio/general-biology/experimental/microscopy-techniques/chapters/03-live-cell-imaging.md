Live-cell imaging records processes as they happen, which fixed cells cannot show. The cells must be kept at physiological temperature, in a medium that buffers the pH and supplies nutrients, and in an atmosphere with the right carbon dioxide and humidity. The microscope stage is often enclosed for this purpose. Without these conditions, the cells change behaviour within minutes, and the dynamics recorded are those of stressed cells rather than of healthy ones.

The labels must be introduced without disturbing the process. Fluorescent proteins are genetically encoded, so every cell that expresses the construct carries the label, but overexpression can change the behaviour of the protein. Expression levels should be kept low, ideally near the endogenous level, and the construct should be checked to show it does not disrupt the function of the protein. Betzig and colleagues (2006) imaged fluorescent proteins in living cells at high resolution, and the value of the method depends on this kind of validation.

Light damages living cells. Phototoxicity is the damage caused by the excitation light, often through the generation of reactive oxygen species, and it can slow division, alter organelles and kill cells. Photobleaching is the loss of fluorescence as the label is destroyed. Both are reduced by lowering the intensity and the frequency of illumination, by using more sensitive detectors and by choosing fluorophores that are stable under the light used. The experimenter must show that the imaging schedule does not change the outcome, for example by comparing imaged and unimaged cells.

Time-lapse data must be checked for drift, for changes in focus and for changes in the cells that have nothing to do with the process. Automated analysis is useful, but it must be validated against manual measurement on a subset of the data.

Ioannidis (2005) described how selecting the cells that behave as expected can bias the result, and live imaging invites this when the cells that move or divide are chosen for display. The selection criteria must be stated and applied to all cells that meet them.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the dynamic processes that live imaging reveals. The imaging protocol should record the medium, the temperature, the light dose and the frame interval, all of which are needed to interpret the data.
