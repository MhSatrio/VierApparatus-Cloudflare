Super-resolution microscopy reaches scales below the diffraction limit, as Hell and Wichmann (1994) and Betzig and colleagues (2006) demonstrated. Its application to a biological question demands more than the instrument. The resolution a method achieves depends on the density of labels and on the precision with which each label can be localised. A structure is seen clearly only if enough labels are present, and a structure labelled sparsely can look less dense than it is.

The labelling requirements are strict. The label must be bright and stable enough to be detected many times, and it must be attached to the target without changing its distribution. Fluorescent proteins are often used because they are genetically encoded, but their photophysics differs from that of organic dyes, and the choice of label should be matched to the method. Antibodies used in immunolabelling add a linkage that can displace the label by a distance of several nanometres, which limits the precision of the measurement.

Localisation precision is the accuracy with which the position of a single label is determined. It depends on the number of photons collected from each label. The final resolution of an image combines the precision with the density of labels, and a published resolution figure should be checked against the density of the labelled structure.

Quantitative localisation is possible. The positions of labels can be used to count molecules, to measure the size of clusters and to compare the organisation of proteins between conditions. Such analyses require careful control of the labelling efficiency, which determines how many of the molecules are seen.

Ioannidis (2005) described how claims about structure can exceed the evidence when the labelling efficiency and the statistics are not reported. The number of cells, the number of molecules localised and the labelling efficiency must accompany any claim about nanoscale organisation.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the structures that super-resolution methods are used to study. The protocol should record the labelling method, the localisation precision and the reconstruction parameters.
