Ribonucleases are enzymes that cleave RNA, and they are extremely stable, remain active after boiling and are present on skin, dust and most laboratory surfaces. The control of these enzymes is therefore a standing discipline rather than a single step. Chomczynski and Sacchi (1987) built their method around the inactivation of ribonucleases by guanidinium salts, but the protection must continue after the extraction, since a purified RNA is still vulnerable to any contamination introduced during handling.

The practices that protect RNA are simple but must be applied consistently. Gloves are changed frequently, and surfaces and pipettes are cleaned with a reagent that destroys ribonucleases. Water and buffers are prepared with diethyl pyrocarbonate treatment or purchased as nuclease-free, and plastics are chosen that have been tested for the absence of nucleases. A dedicated area and equipment for RNA work reduces the risk of carry-over from other samples.

The hydroxyl group at the 2' position of the RNA sugar, absent from the deoxyribose of DNA, makes RNA susceptible to alkaline and enzymatic cleavage. This difference explains why RNA requires more protection than DNA, which is stable in buffers that would degrade RNA rapidly.

Storage conditions determine how long a preparation remains usable. DNA can be kept for years at minus twenty degrees Celsius in a buffer at a slightly alkaline pH, which protects it from acid depurination. RNA is kept at minus eighty degrees Celsius, preferably as aliquots that avoid repeated freezing and thawing, which fragments it. The buffer should be free of nucleases and of chelators that would inhibit later reactions.

Ioannidis (2005) emphasised that the stability of stored material is an assumption that should be tested, and the integrity of a stored sample should be checked before use in an important experiment. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the chemistry of nucleic acid degradation that these practices guard against. The laboratory record should state the storage temperature, the buffer, the aliquot and the date of each preparation.
