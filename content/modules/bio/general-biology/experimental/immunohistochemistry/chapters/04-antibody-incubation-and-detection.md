Before the primary antibody is applied, the section is treated to reduce background. Tissues contain endogenous enzymes, such as peroxidase in red blood cells and phosphatase in some cells, which will convert the substrate and produce a signal unrelated to the antibody. These enzymes are inactivated by a short treatment with hydrogen peroxide or a specific inhibitor. The tissue also contains sites that bind antibodies nonspecifically, and a protein block covers them. Coons et al. (1941) showed that labelled antibodies reveal their specific binding, and the block protects that specificity by removing competing signals.

The primary antibody is applied at a dilution set by titration, and the incubation is usually overnight at a low temperature, which allows slow and specific binding. Too high a concentration increases nonspecific staining, and too short an incubation gives a weak signal. The conditions must be the same in every section that is to be compared, and the section must not dry during incubation, since drying produces artefactual staining at the edges.

The secondary antibody is applied next. It recognises the primary and carries the enzyme or the fluorophore. The detection system is chosen for the signal required, and the amplifying systems that use many labels per antibody increase sensitivity but also increase the risk of background. Ioannidis (2005) described how a more sensitive system can reveal signal that is not specific, and the sensitivity should be matched to the question.

Each step is followed by a wash, and the washes are timed and performed in the same buffer. A section that is under-washed retains reagents that bind later, and the background appears in the places where the reagents accumulated.

Platt (1964) recommended a control that could reveal an error, and the omission of the primary antibody, with all other steps retained, shows whether the detection system alone produces staining. The results of this control are reported with the stain.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the endogenous enzymes and structures of tissue that cause background.
