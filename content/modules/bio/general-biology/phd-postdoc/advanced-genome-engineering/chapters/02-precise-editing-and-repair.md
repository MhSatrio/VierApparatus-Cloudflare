A precise edit requires that the cell repair a break using a template that carries the desired change. Homology-directed repair uses a donor molecule with sequences matching the regions on either side of the break, and the donor must be designed so that the desired change is copied into the genome. Cong et al. (2013) described the use of repair templates in the early multiplex work, and the efficiency of this route is generally low in comparison with the error-prone repair pathways that compete with it.

The homology arms must be long enough to support recombination and short enough to be synthesised and delivered efficiently. A silent mutation placed in the guide-matching sequence of the template prevents the nuclease from cutting the edited allele again, which would otherwise undo the work or produce a second, unwanted change. The design should be checked for each of these features before the experiment begins.

Base editors convert one base to another without a double-strand break, which avoids many of the unintended outcomes of repair. They are restricted to particular conversions and carry their own off-target risks at the level of individual bases. Mali et al. (2013) showed the general principle of guided editing, and the choice between a template-based approach and a base editor depends on the change required and on the tolerance for unintended outcomes.

Selection strategies enrich for edited cells, since the frequency of a precise edit is often low. Selection may use a marker, a drug or a fluorescent reporter, and the marker must be removed or shown not to affect the phenotype under study. Ioannidis (2005) described how a selected population can differ from the population as a whole, and the verification must be performed on individual clones rather than on the pool.

The edit must be verified as precise, with no unintended insertions, deletions or rearrangements at the target. Cumming (2014) argues that the frequency of precise editing should be reported with an interval, since it depends on the number of clones examined and is uncertain when that number is small.
