Genome engineering at the advanced level means making precise, verified changes to the genome and measuring what those changes do. The field began with the discovery that a programmable nuclease can be directed to a chosen sequence, and Jinek et al. (2012) described the RNA-guided enzyme that made this possible. Cong et al. (2013) and Mali et al. (2013) showed that the approach works in complex cells and organisms, and the methods have since diversified into several families of editors, each with its own strengths and risks.

The specificity and efficiency of an edit depend on the guide and on the repair template. A guide that matches several sites in the genome may cut where it was not intended, and a repair template that is poorly designed may yield an imprecise change. The design should be checked against the whole genome for likely off-target sites, and the candidate guides should be ranked by predicted specificity. Ioannidis (2005) described how the reliability of a method depends on its validation, and off-target analysis is part of that validation.

Edits that do not require a double-strand break, such as base editing and prime-based approaches, reduce some risks of the nuclease, but they introduce their own off-target possibilities and their own constraints on which changes they can make. The choice of method should be justified by the change required and by the risks it carries. Multiplex editing of several loci at once is efficient but increases the number of possible unintended changes, and each must be checked.

Verification of an edited clone is at the sequence level, and it should cover the intended edit and the predicted off-target sites. Clones should be analysed independently, and the editing efficiency should be estimated with its interval. Cumming (2014) argues that the efficiency of an edit is a quantity with uncertainty, and reporting it with an interval shows how well the method performs. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the sequence data and the methods should be deposited, so that other groups can check the claim of a precise edit.
