A structure is only as useful as the report that accompanies it. A reader must be able to judge its quality from the statistics given, to see which regions are well supported and to obtain the data from which the model was made. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the reporting of statistics and data is the basis of trust in a structure, and a structure reported without them asks the reader to accept it on authority.

The data collection and processing statistics should be given for each method, including the number of particles or the number of reflections, the completeness of the data and the signal-to-noise measures. The resolution should be reported with the criterion used to define it, since different criteria give different values for the same map. Cumming (2014) argues that the uncertainty of each statistic should be considered, and a resolution stated without its criterion is not interpretable.

The refinement and validation statistics must include the geometry, the fit to the data and the tests for overfitting. Regions that are poorly supported should be identified, and the ensemble statistics should be given for methods that produce ensembles. Ioannidis (2005) described how a result that is reported selectively, with its favourable statistics only, misleads its readers, and the complete set of statistics is the safeguard.

The coordinates, the experimental data and the maps should be deposited with identifiers, so that the structure can be checked and reused. The sample, the construct and the conditions of the experiment should be described, since a structure of a modified protein or under unusual conditions may not represent the native molecule. Peng (2011) argued that the availability of the data is what allows a result to be verified, and the deposition is the means of verification for a structure.

The report should close with an honest statement of what the structure establishes about function and what remains to be shown. A structure is a basis for hypotheses about function, and the report should not claim more than the evidence supports. The structure's relation to the biology it describes is established only by the functional tests that accompany it.
