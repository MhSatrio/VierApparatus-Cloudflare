A structure is a model of a molecule fitted to experimental data, and its value depends on how well the model is supported by those data and how well it has been validated. The determination of structure at the advanced level requires understanding of the physics of each method, the statistics of the data and the criteria by which a model is judged. Jumper et al. (2021) showed that computational prediction can now produce accurate models of many proteins, and the experimental determination of structure remains necessary to test those models and to resolve what prediction cannot.

Each experimental method has its own limits. X-ray crystallography requires crystals and solves a phase problem that must be addressed by additional information. Cryo-electron microscopy reconstructs a density from many images of individual particles, and its resolution depends on the number of particles and the quality of the alignment. Nuclear magnetic resonance is suited to smaller and flexible proteins, and it reports on the ensemble of conformations rather than a single structure. Ioannidis (2005) described how a method's limits must be understood before its results are interpreted, and each method's limits should be stated.

The quality of a model is assessed by the agreement between the model and the data, and by validation statistics that test its geometry and its fit. A model with excellent geometry but poor agreement with the data is not a reliable description of the molecule. Cumming (2014) argues that the uncertainty of each parameter should be reported with its interval, since a structure that is precise in one region may be uncertain in another.

Integration combines structural data from several sources, such as crosslinking, mass spectrometry and computational prediction, to build models of large or flexible assemblies. The weight given to each source determines the result, and the agreement among sources must be assessed rather than assumed. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the deposition of the structure factors, the maps and the models allows the claim to be checked, and that is the basis of a trustworthy structure.
