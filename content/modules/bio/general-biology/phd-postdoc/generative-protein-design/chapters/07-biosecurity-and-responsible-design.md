A method that designs useful proteins can, in principle, design harmful ones, and the responsibility of the designers extends to this risk. The same capability that produces an enzyme for industry could be directed toward a toxin or a pathogenic factor, and the investigators should assess this dual-use risk before they publish or release a method. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that openness is a core value of science, and this value must be weighed against the risk with explicit reasoning rather than being abandoned or applied without thought.

Screening is one safeguard. Design requests and outputs can be checked against lists of hazardous sequences and functions, and orders for synthetic DNA can be screened by the providers that synthesise it. Such screens are imperfect, since they recognise only what is on the list, and their limits must be stated. Ioannidis (2005) described how a safeguard that appears complete may leave a large gap, and the coverage of a screen should be reported.

Access controls and staged release reduce the risk of misuse while allowing legitimate use. A model may be released to qualified users under a licence that restricts the use, or its most capable components may be withheld while the method is described. These decisions should be explained, and the criteria for access should be stated. Peng (2011) argued that the availability of the means to check a result is important, and restrictions should be as narrow as the risk requires.

The decision about publication should involve biosecurity and security expertise, and it should consider the information that a publication would release. The benefits of openness, including the ability of others to defend against a hazard, must be weighed against the risk. Cumming (2014) argues that the uncertainty of such judgements should be stated, since a decision made under uncertainty should be transparent about what is not known.

The report should state the safeguards in place, the screening applied, the access conditions and the reasons for each decision. A transparent statement of safeguards allows the community to judge the responsibility of the work and to propose improvements. Responsible design is a shared obligation, and the record of how it was discharged is part of the science.
