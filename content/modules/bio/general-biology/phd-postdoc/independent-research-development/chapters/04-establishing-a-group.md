An independent group needs space, equipment, a governance structure and a relationship with the institution that hosts it. The group's status must be agreed in writing, including its support, its access to shared facilities and the terms on which its funding is managed. Ioannidis (2005) described how the institutional environment shapes the reliability of research, and the agreement with the host is the foundation of the group's independence.

Governance defines how decisions are made, who decides what, and how disagreements are resolved. A clear structure prevents the decisions of the group from being made informally or inconsistently, and it allows members to know where their responsibilities lie. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that a transparent governance structure is part of the integrity of a research group, and it should be written down and shared.

Data systems must be established from the start, with storage that is secure, backed up and organised, and with standards for analysis code and version control. The systems should make the group's records reproducible and should support the data sharing that funders increasingly require. Peng (2011) argued that the means to reproduce a result must be preserved, and the group's systems are the place where that preservation happens.

The group carries health, safety and ethics responsibilities, including the approval of procedures that involve people or animals and the safe handling of hazardous materials. These responsibilities cannot be delegated away from the leader, and the group's compliance should be reviewed regularly. Cumming (2014) argues that the sample sizes and the uncertainty of the group's estimates should be reviewed alongside its safety, since both determine whether the work is justified.

The group's financial management should be transparent, and its performance should be reviewed periodically against its aims. Periodic review allows the leader to adjust the programme and the structure as it grows, and it provides the evidence that funders and institutions use to judge the group.
