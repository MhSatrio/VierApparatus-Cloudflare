The first independent grant is the most important funding event in an early career, because it establishes the researcher as a leader rather than an executor. Early-career schemes are designed for this step, and their eligibility and criteria should be read closely before an application is planned. The application must show that the researcher can conceive and direct a programme, and that the team and the resources are adequate to deliver it. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that reviewers assess the rigour of the proposed methods above the prestige of the applicant, and the application should make its rigour visible.

A track record of publications, presentations and preliminary data establishes the researcher's capability. The record should be presented accurately, without exaggeration of the researcher's contribution to joint work, and the preliminary data should be reported with their sample sizes and their uncertainty. Cumming (2014) argues that preliminary estimates from small studies are uncertain, and the application should say so, since a reviewer will otherwise assume a precision the data cannot support.

The researcher's role in the programme must be stated as the independent leader, with the responsibilities for design, analysis and reporting clearly assigned. A reviewer must be able to see what the researcher will do rather than what the group will do collectively. Ioannidis (2005) described how the failure to attribute responsibility weakens the accountability of research, and the statement of the researcher's role provides it.

The budget should reflect a realistic team and its costs, since a budget that is too small will not deliver the programme and one that is too large will not be funded. The costs should be derived from the design, as described in other chapters. Peng (2011) argued that the analysis plan and the data management should be described so that the programme can be reproduced, and these descriptions strengthen the application.

Responses to reviewers' comments in a resubmission should be structured and honest, as described elsewhere. A portfolio of funding from several sources secures the continuity of the group, and the researcher should plan the sequence of applications so that the group is not dependent on a single award.
