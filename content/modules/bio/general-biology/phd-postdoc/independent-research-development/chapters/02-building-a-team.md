A research team is a community whose practices determine the reliability of its output, and an independent investigator sets those practices from the start. Recruitment should select members for the skills the programme requires, and it should be fair and open, so that the group does not reproduce the narrow composition of its founder. Ioannidis (2005) described how the environment in which research is done shapes its reliability, and the team's culture begins with its members.

Record-keeping and data practices must be established early. Each member should keep complete records of procedures, observations and decisions, and the group should adopt common standards for data storage, analysis scripts and version control. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that such practices are the foundation of reproducible science, and their adoption by a new group is easier than their later imposition.

Students supervised within the group produce work that becomes their own record, and the supervisor is responsible for the standards applied to it. The supervisor should teach the student to analyse data reproducibly, to report null results and to state the uncertainty of estimates. Cumming (2014) argues that a student who learns to read an interval will judge evidence more soundly throughout their career.

Authorship and credit within the group should be agreed in advance, and disagreements about them should be resolved by a stated procedure. Errors discovered by team members should be welcomed and corrected, not hidden, since a group in which errors are reported is more reliable than one in which they are feared. Peng (2011) argued that a group's records allow the reconstruction of its work, and an open culture makes those records useful.

Members who raise concerns about practices or results must be protected from reprisal, and the group should make clear that such concerns are a contribution. The supervisor should also develop each member's independence, encouraging them to formulate their own questions and to build their own careers, since the purpose of a research group is to produce researchers as well as results.
