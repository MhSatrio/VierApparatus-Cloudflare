The transition from postdoctoral researcher to independent investigator is the moment at which a scientist must define a programme that is their own. The question must be distinct from the one pursued in the mentor's laboratory, yet connected to the expertise that the researcher has built, and it must be defensible on its own evidence. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that an independent programme is most credible when it is built on rigorous and transparent methods from the outset, and the early choices of method establish the reputation that the researcher will carry.

Establishing an independent group requires a team, and the recruitment and supervision of students and staff is a responsibility that the researcher must learn. The team's expectations, its training and its conduct shape the reliability of its output, and the researcher is accountable for the practices of those they supervise. Ioannidis (2005) described how laboratory culture influences the reliability of research, and the independent investigator sets that culture.

Funding is acquired through the writing of independent applications, and the first grant is often the decisive step. The application must demonstrate that the researcher can lead a programme, not only execute one, and it must present a plan with clear aims, a feasible design and an analysis specified in advance. Cumming (2014) argues that the precision expected from the programme should be stated, since a plan that promises certainty will not survive review.

The establishment of a group includes its resources, its governance and its relations with the institution, and each must be managed with attention to the risks of independence. Isolation from a mentor and the pressure to publish quickly can lead to shortcuts, and the researcher should build structures that protect the quality of the work. Peng (2011) argued that the records and practices of a group should be designed from the start so that its results can be reproduced.

The chapters that follow examine the research question, the team, the funding, the group, the collaborations and the record of independent contributions. Each step is a means of making the transition to independence visible and credible to funders, institutions and the field.
