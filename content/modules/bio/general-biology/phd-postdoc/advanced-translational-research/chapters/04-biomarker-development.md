A biomarker links the mechanism of a treatment to something that can be measured, and the development of such a marker is a translational task in its own right. Candidates are discovered in preclinical and early clinical samples, and the most promising are then validated. Ioannidis (2005) described how the screening of many candidates produces a few that appear strong by chance, and the discovery stage must be followed by independent validation before a marker is trusted.

The assay that measures the biomarker must be analytically validated, with its accuracy, precision and reproducibility established as described in other chapters. A marker that cannot be measured consistently cannot be used to make decisions, however strongly it tracks the mechanism. Cumming (2014) argues that the precision of the assay sets the lower limit of the uncertainty in every use of the marker.

The biomarker must be shown to change with the intended mechanism in people. A pharmacodynamic marker demonstrates that the drug engages its target, and this is evidence of biological activity rather than of benefit. A surrogate endpoint is a marker that is claimed to predict clinical benefit, and it requires stronger evidence, since the relation between the surrogate and the outcome must be shown across treatments. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the claim made for a marker must match the evidence, and a marker of engagement should not be presented as a surrogate.

A biomarker is qualified for a stated context of use, such as enrichment of a trial or early assessment of response, and its qualification applies only to that context. The context must be stated, since a marker that serves one purpose may fail another. Peng (2011) argued that the assays, the procedures and the data behind the qualification must be documented so that the marker can be judged.

The multiplicity arising from screening many candidates must be controlled by a validation in an independent set, and the number of candidates screened should be reported. The report should give the candidates, the validation, the context of use and the uncertainty of each performance measure, so that the reader can judge how far the marker can be trusted.
