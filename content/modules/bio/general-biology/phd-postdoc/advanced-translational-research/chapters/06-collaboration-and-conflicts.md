Translational research depends on collaboration among partners with different interests: academic investigators who seek knowledge, clinicians who seek better care, and industrial partners who seek a product. The collaboration brings resources and expertise that no one partner could supply, and it creates conflicts that must be managed in the open. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the independence of evaluation is the basis of trust in translational claims, and the structure of a collaboration must protect it.

The agreements that define the ownership of data and results must be settled before the work begins. They should protect the right of academic investigators to publish their findings, whatever they show, since a sponsor's ability to suppress unfavourable results is one of the most serious threats to the evidence. Ioannidis (2005) described how selective publication inflates the apparent value of treatments, and the publication rights are the defence against it.

The financial interests of industrial partners in the outcome must be declared, and the safeguards that keep the design and analysis independent must be put in place. Independent statisticians who hold the data and analyse them under a pre-specified plan provide such a safeguard, as do independent data monitoring committees. Cumming (2014) argues that the independence of the analysis is necessary for the interval to mean what it says, since an analysis shaped by the sponsor's interest will narrow or shift the interval in its favour.

Conflicts must be declared in publications and to regulators, so that readers and decision makers can weigh the evidence in context. The declaration should state the nature and the extent of each interest and the measures taken to manage it. Peng (2011) argued that the data and analysis should be available to qualified parties so that the claims can be checked independently of the sponsor.

The sharing of resources such as biobanks and models among partners must respect the terms under which they were built and the consent under which the data were collected. The report of the collaboration should give its structure, its agreements and its safeguards, so that a reader can judge whether the evidence was produced under conditions that protect its integrity.
