A mechanism is established when others can reproduce it. The discovering laboratory may have preferred reagents, cell lines or techniques that are not available elsewhere, and an account that depends on such particulars is weaker than one that survives their replacement. Replication by independent groups is therefore the decisive test of a mechanism, and the discovering group has a responsibility to make it possible. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the sharing of materials is a condition of the reproducibility on which molecular biology depends.

Independent laboratories need the reagents, cell lines, strains and protocols to repeat the key experiments. A reagent deposited in a public collection, with its identity and lot described, can be obtained by any group. A protocol that is described in enough detail for an independent group to follow exactly is far more useful than a summary in a paper. Peng (2011) argued that the means of verification must be made available to those who wish to check a result, and the protocols and materials are part of that means.

The experiments that a replication would test should be specified, ideally in advance. A replication that repeats a different experiment cannot confirm the original claim, and a pre-specified replication protects against the selection of favourable outcomes. The comparison of the original and replication estimates should be made with their intervals, so that the agreement or disagreement can be judged against the uncertainty of both. Cumming (2014) argues that the question of replication is one of estimation, and the intervals show how well the two studies agree.

A failure to replicate should be investigated before the mechanism is rejected. Differences in reagents, cell types, instruments and conditions may explain the failure, and each should be examined. Ioannidis (2005) described how many failures to replicate reflect such differences rather than a false original finding, and the investigation of them is part of the discovery of the mechanism. Conceptual replications with different methods test whether the mechanism is general, and the results of all replications should be incorporated into a revised account of the mechanism.
