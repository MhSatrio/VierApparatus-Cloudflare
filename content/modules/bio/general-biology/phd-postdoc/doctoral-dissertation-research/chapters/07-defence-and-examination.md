The defence is the examination of the candidate's understanding of the work, and it is the occasion on which the examiners test whether each claim is supported. The candidate should anticipate the questions on the design, the analysis, the interpretation and the limits of each study. A candidate who can state the uncertainty of each claim, and the evidence that would change it, demonstrates the judgement that the doctorate is meant to confer. Ioannidis (2005) described how the overstatement of findings is a common failing, and the defence tests whether the candidate has avoided it.

The candidate should be able to state the uncertainty of each claim in terms of its interval, and to say what additional evidence would narrow it or overturn it. Cumming (2014) argues that a researcher who can state the precision of each finding is able to judge it honestly, and this ability is the centre of the examination.

The candidate must defend their own contribution against the work of collaborators, stating clearly what they did and what they did not do. An overclaim of contribution is damaging, and a candidate who understands the whole project, including the parts they did not perform, shows a deeper grasp of it. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the attribution of work is part of the integrity of the record.

Criticism should be met without defensiveness, and a candidate who concedes a point the evidence does not support shows the scientific maturity the examination seeks. The explanation of the choices made in the methods, and of the alternatives that were rejected, shows that the candidate understood the trade-offs. Peng (2011) argued that the reasoning behind each analytic choice is part of the reproducibility of the work, and the candidate should be able to explain it.

The defence should end with a reflection on the questions the work leaves open and the directions the candidate would pursue next. The reflection shows whether the candidate can see the work in the context of the field, and it prepares the candidate for the independent research that follows the doctorate.
