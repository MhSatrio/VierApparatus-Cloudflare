A dissertation question begins as a broad interest, such as the regulation of a process or the response of a population to a stress, and it must be narrowed until it has a defined population, a defined system and a defined outcome. The narrowing is the most important intellectual step of the doctorate, and it determines whether the work can be completed and whether it will matter. Ioannidis (2005) described how an ill-defined question produces results that cannot be interpreted, and a sharp question is the remedy.

The survey of the field identifies an important gap, a conflict among results or a population that has not been studied. The candidate should be able to state the evidence that establishes the gap and the reason it matters. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that a question justified by its evidence is more credible than one justified by its novelty alone, and the survey should make the justification explicit.

The feasibility of the question must be tested against the time and resources available. A question that requires a decade of work cannot be the subject of a doctorate, and one that can be answered in a few months may not be substantial enough. The candidate should estimate the duration and cost of each study that the question would require, and should adjust the question until the estimates are realistic.

The question should be stated as an aim with an expected effect and the precision the work will achieve. Cumming (2014) argues that an aim framed as an estimate with a stated precision is more informative than a question that asks only whether an effect exists, and it allows the examiners to judge the value of the answer.

The studies that together would answer the question should be identified, and each should be planned with its analysis specified in advance. The supervisor and the committee should agree the question before the main work begins, since a question changed late in the doctorate may leave the candidate with an incomplete body of work. Peng (2011) argued that the plan should be recorded so that the progress of the work can be checked against it.
