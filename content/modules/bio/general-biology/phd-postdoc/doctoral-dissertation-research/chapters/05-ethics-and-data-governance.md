A doctoral candidate has the same ethical obligations as any researcher, and the obligations begin before the first measurement is taken. Ethical approval must be obtained for each protocol that involves people, animals or biological material, and the candidate must not collect data under an approval that does not cover the work. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the integrity of research depends on its procedures being visible, and the record of approvals is the evidence that the procedures were followed.

Consent for participants must describe the purposes for which the data will be used, including the dissertation and any later publication or sharing. A consent that covers only the dissertation does not permit later reuse, and the candidate must obtain a fresh consent or confine the use to the original purpose. Ioannidis (2005) described how the misuse of data beyond consent damages the trust on which research depends, and the scope of consent should be stated clearly in the record.

Where animals are used, the candidate must observe the principles of replacement, reduction and refinement, and must report the number of animals and the welfare measures applied. The candidate's experiments are subject to the same review as any others, and the candidate is responsible for the welfare of the animals in their care. Cumming (2014) argues that the sample size should be justified, since an underpowered study uses animals without a reasonable prospect of an informative result.

The candidate must manage the data from collection through storage, analysis and sharing to retention. The data should be stored securely, analysed reproducibly, and retained for the period that the institution requires. Peng (2011) argued that the reproducibility of a dissertation depends on the availability of its data and code to the examiners, and the candidate should prepare these from the outset.

Authorship and attribution within collaborations must be stated clearly, so that the examiners can see which parts of the work are the candidate's own. The candidate is responsible for reporting errors that they discover in their work and for correcting the record when an error is found. The record of approvals and consents, and of the data governance that applied, should be kept for examination.
