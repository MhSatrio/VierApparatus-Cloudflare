A doctoral thesis is a complete record of the work, and it is often much longer than the articles that report it. The translation into publication requires the selection of the studies that justify a stand-alone article, the restructuring of each for a journal, and the preservation of the full record in the thesis. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the publication of research should not be fragmented in ways that hide its complete results, and the candidate should make sure that the articles together present the whole of the findings honestly.

Each study must be restructured for the journal and its reporting requirements, following the reporting standard for its design. The article should state its design, its analysis and its results with their intervals, and it should follow the journal's policies on data sharing and registration. Cumming (2014) argues that each article should report the precision of its estimates, since a reader of a single article cannot see the thesis's wider context.

The supervisor and co-authors must be included in the publication plan, with the authorship agreed in advance as described elsewhere. The plan should set out which studies will be published, in which order and with which authors. Ioannidis (2005) described how the splitting of a body of work into many small articles can inflate the apparent output, and the plan should avoid this.

Where a journal requires registration or prior posting, the candidate must comply, and the posting should be consistent with the thesis. Negative and exploratory results contained in the thesis must be reported honestly in the articles, and the exploratory ones must be labelled as such. Peng (2011) argued that the data and code from the thesis should be deposited with identifiers, so that the articles can be checked.

The response to the journal's reviewers should be structured and transparent, as described elsewhere. The candidate's first publication is a demonstration of the independent scientific judgement the doctorate has built, and it should display the reporting standards on which the candidate's reputation will rest.
