A systems medicine prediction is clinically useful only when it performs in patients who were not used to derive it, and this performance must be shown in an independent cohort. External validation tests whether the signature transports to other sites and populations, since a signature derived in one hospital may depend on its practices. Ioannidis (2005) described how signatures that performed well in their derivation cohort have often failed in validation, and the independent test is the decisive step.

Calibration is the agreement between predicted risk and observed event rates. A signature may rank patients well while predicting risks that are systematically too high or too low, and such a signature would mislead a clinician who uses the absolute values. The calibration should be reported with its interval, and it should be checked across the range of predicted risks. Cumming (2014) argues that each observed event rate should be given with the interval that its sample size supports.

The prospective design applies the signature to patients before outcomes are known, and this is the strongest form of clinical validation. The prediction is registered, the outcomes are recorded, and the agreement is assessed without the possibility of retrospective adjustment. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that prospective registration protects the validation from the flexibility that can make retrospective success appear, and it should be used where feasible.

A signature must add information beyond established clinical factors, since a molecular test that reproduces what the clinician already knows adds cost without value. The incremental value is measured by comparing models with and without the signature, and the improvement should be reported with its interval. Peng (2011) argued that the models and the data must be available so that the comparison can be repeated.

The final test is whether the signature would change a clinical decision for the better. A decision-analytic evaluation weighs the benefits and harms of acting on the signature against those of the standard approach, and it shows whether the signature is worth using. The report should state the decision the signature informs, the cohort, the outcome and the calibration, together with their intervals.
