A companion diagnostic is an assay that measures a biomarker to decide whether a therapy should be given, and its validity must be established in three distinct senses. Analytical validity concerns whether the assay measures the biomarker accurately, precisely and reproducibly. Clinical validity concerns whether the result is associated with the outcome in the population. Clinical utility concerns whether using the result improves decisions. Ioannidis (2005) described how the confusion of these forms of validity leads to tests that are accurate but useless, and each must be demonstrated.

Analytical validity is assessed for accuracy against reference materials, for precision within and between runs, for the limit of detection and for reproducibility across laboratories. The assay must perform in the sample types that will be tested in practice, and its performance should be reported with its interval. Cumming (2014) argues that the precision of the assay sets the uncertainty of every decision based on it, and the precision should be stated.

Concordance between the assay used in the pivotal trial and the assay used in routine practice must be assessed, because a difference between them can misclassify patients. The agreement is estimated on paired samples, and the discordance rate and the classification agreement should be reported with their intervals. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the assay and its version should be specified, since a test that changes between the trial and practice may not perform as the trial suggested.

Samples of poor quality or small volume can yield indeterminate results, and the handling of these must be stated. An indeterminate result is neither positive nor negative, and the rate of indeterminate results affects how many patients can be assigned a therapy by the test. The rate and the handling of indeterminates should be reported. Peng (2011) argued that the procedures for handling each class of result must be documented so that the test can be reproduced.

Regulatory expectations require evidence of all three forms of validity before a companion diagnostic is approved alongside a therapy. The report should present each form with its evidence and its interval, and it should state the limits of the evidence for each.
