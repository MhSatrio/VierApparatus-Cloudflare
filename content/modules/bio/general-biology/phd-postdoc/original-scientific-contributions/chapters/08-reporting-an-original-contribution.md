A report of an original contribution must allow the field to evaluate it and to test it. The report begins with the statement of the contribution and the question it answers, and it gives the primary estimate with its interval and the replication on which the claim rests. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the strength of a claim should be visible in its report, and the contribution's estimate and interval are the first thing a reader needs.

The sensitivity analyses and the controls that exclude artefacts should be reported, together with the alternative explanations and the evidence that distinguishes them from the contribution. A report that presents only the favoured explanation invites the sceptic to find the others, and it is better for the report to address them first. Cumming (2014) argues that the comparison between the contribution and its alternatives should be made on the evidence and its uncertainty.

The contribution must be placed against the prior literature, with each important relation to earlier results stated explicitly. The placing should acknowledge the work that anticipated the contribution and should not claim priority that the literature does not support. Ioannidis (2005) described how an honest account of prior work protects a field from the inflation of its own history.

The data, the code and the materials should be deposited with identifiers, and the conditions under which the result was obtained should be stated. Peng (2011) argued that the reproducibility of a result is the measure of its value to the field, and the deposition of these materials makes the contribution testable by others.

The report should state the impact of the contribution and its dual-use assessment with the safeguards applied, and it should close with the uncertainty that remains and the experiment that would resolve it. A contribution reported in this way is one that the field can test, use and build upon, and it is the mark of an investigator whose work the field can trust.
