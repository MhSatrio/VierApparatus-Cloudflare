The evidence for an original contribution is the basis of its credibility, and it must be presented with the uncertainty that the evidence carries. The primary estimate should be reported with its interval, and the interval must be narrow enough that the contribution is useful. A contribution whose interval includes effects too small to matter has not yet established its importance, however striking its point estimate. Cumming (2014) argues that the interval is the honest statement of what the evidence shows, and the contribution should be built on it.

Replication by the same group in independent samples and conditions is a stronger test than any single study. An effect that appears in a second cohort, under different conditions, is much more likely to be real. Ioannidis (2005) described how the failure of early findings to replicate has undermined many fields, and the researcher's own replication is the first defence against being one of those failures.

Sensitivity analyses show that the result holds across reasonable analytic choices, such as different models, exclusion rules and transformations. A result that appears only under one specific analysis is fragile, and the researcher should demonstrate its robustness across the plausible alternatives. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the sensitivity of a result to the analysis should be reported openly, rather than hidden behind the analysis that worked.

Controls must exclude the artefacts that would produce the same result, including batch effects, contamination, off-target effects and confounders. The control that excludes the most likely artefact is the most convincing, and the researcher should identify that artefact and test it directly. Peng (2011) argued that the controls and their results must be documented so that others can judge whether the artefacts were excluded.

The strongest alternative explanation must be stated and compared with the claimed contribution, and the evidence that would distinguish the two should be identified. The report should state the uncertainty that remains and the evidence that would reduce it, so that the field knows what the next test is.
