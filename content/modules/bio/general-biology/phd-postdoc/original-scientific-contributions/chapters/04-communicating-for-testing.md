A contribution becomes useful to the field only when others can test it, and the communication must be designed for that purpose. The methods should be described in enough detail that an independent group could repeat the key experiment without contacting the authors. Peng (2011) argued that a result that cannot be reproduced is not yet a contribution to the field, and the description of the methods is the first condition of reproduction.

The materials, including strains, reagents and constructs, should be deposited with identifiers, so that any group can obtain them. A material described only in words may be impossible to obtain, and the loss of a strain can end the ability to repeat a result. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the sharing of materials is a basic condition of the reproducibility on which the field depends.

The raw data and the analysis code should be deposited with their version, so that the analysis can be repeated exactly. The conditions under which the result was obtained must be stated, since a failure to replicate may reflect a difference in conditions that the original report did not describe. Ioannidis (2005) described how unrecorded differences in conditions explain many failures to replicate, and the complete statement of conditions is the protection against misattribution.

The statistical details should be provided with the estimates and their intervals, so that the magnitude and the precision of the result are visible. Cumming (2014) argues that a result reported without its interval invites an overconfidence that replication will later correct, and the interval gives the replicating group a clear target.

The contribution should invite replication, and a failure to reproduce should be treated as informative rather than as an attack. The investigator who welcomes replication shows confidence in the contribution, and the field benefits from the independent test. The communication should make clear that the result is a claim to be tested, and that the investigator will take part in its testing.
