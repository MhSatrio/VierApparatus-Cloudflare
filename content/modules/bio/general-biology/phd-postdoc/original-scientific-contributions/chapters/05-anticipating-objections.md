A contribution will be examined by sceptical readers, and the investigator who anticipates their objections is better prepared than one who waits for them. The most likely objection is that the result is an artefact of the method, the instrument or the data, and the answer must be grounded in controls that exclude the artefact. Ioannidis (2005) described how many contested results were eventually shown to be artefacts, and the anticipation of this objection is the most important preparation.

A second objection is that another mechanism could produce the same observation. The investigator should identify the alternative mechanisms and the experiments that would distinguish them from the claimed one, and should state which have been excluded and which remain open. Cumming (2014) argues that the comparison of mechanisms should be made on the evidence and its uncertainty, rather than on the plausibility of each.

A third objection concerns the generality of the result beyond the conditions tested. The investigator should state the conditions under which the result was obtained and should be honest about the conditions that were not tested. Munafò et al. (2017) argued that the claim of generality must match the evidence, and the report should not extend the result beyond its tested scope.

Objections about the adequacy of the sample and the precision of the estimate are common, and they should be answered with the interval and the power of the study. An objection that the sample is too small is answered by showing the interval, and if the interval is wide, the objection is valid and should be acknowledged. Peng (2011) argued that the analysis that justifies the sample must be documented so that the objection can be evaluated.

Objections about statistical choices, and about prior results that the contribution appears to contradict, must be answered with the sensitivity analyses and the comparison of estimates. Where an objection is not answered by the evidence, the investigator should acknowledge it honestly and state the experiment that would address it. An honest acknowledgement of an unresolved objection strengthens the contribution, because it shows the investigator understands its limits.
