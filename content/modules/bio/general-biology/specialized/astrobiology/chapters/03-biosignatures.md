A biosignature is a feature that is produced by life and that can be detected from a distance or in a sample from another world. The best known are isotopic signatures, since biology favours the lighter isotope of carbon in its reactions, so that biological carbon is depleted in carbon-13 compared with the carbon of rock. Des Marais et al. (2008) described such signatures as a central tool of the search, although each has non-biological sources that can mimic it.

Organic molecules whose structure reflects biological synthesis may be a biosignature, for example if they show a pattern of chirality or of repeating units that is hard to produce without life. Such molecules can also be formed by non-biological chemistry in meteorites and in laboratory experiments, so the pattern must be shown to be distinctive before the molecule is taken as evidence.

Mineral textures and layered structures can preserve the traces of microbes, and some ancient structures on Earth have been interpreted as biological. The interpretation is contested, because abiotic processes can produce similar textures, and the evidence is strongest when several independent lines of evidence agree.

Atmospheric gases may be biosignatures when they coexist in an unstable combination, since life maintains gases that would otherwise react away. Oxygen and methane together, for example, would be hard to sustain without a biological source, but the detection of such gases from a distant planet is difficult and depends on the models of the atmosphere. Ioannidis (2005) described how a result can be accepted before its alternatives are excluded, and Cumming (2014) argues that the evidence should be stated with its uncertainty and the alternatives that remain.

A candidate biosignature must be tested against its abiotic alternatives, and it should be accepted only if the non-biological explanations are shown to be unlikely. The criteria for acceptance should be stated before the observation, and the evidence should be reported with the explanations that were considered and rejected.
