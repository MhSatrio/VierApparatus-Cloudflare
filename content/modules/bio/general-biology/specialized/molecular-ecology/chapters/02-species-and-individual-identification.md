A DNA barcode is a short standard gene sequence that differs among species, and comparing an unknown sequence with a reference library identifies its species. Taberlet et al. (2012) described how such barcodes allow organisms to be identified from fragments, eggs or traces, when morphology is unavailable. The method is only as good as the reference library, since a sequence that is not in the library can be assigned only to a relative, and the library must be curated with voucher specimens that have been identified by experts.

The barcode can reveal cryptic species, which are lineages that are genetically distinct but morphologically similar. A single morphological species may contain several barcode lineages, and the discovery of these lineages changes the count of species and the conservation status of each. The threshold of genetic difference used to separate species must be stated, since different thresholds give different counts.

Hybrid individuals carry genomes that mix two species, and a single barcode may assign them to one parent even though they are a mixture. Multilocus data reveal the mixture, and the identification of hybrids requires more markers than a barcode provides. The presence of hybrids should be reported, since they affect both the identification and the conservation of the species.

Individuals can be identified from multilocus genotypes, and this is used in population monitoring to count animals and to track individuals over time. The identification depends on the genotyping error rate. An error can make one individual appear as two, which inflates the count, or two individuals appear as one, which deflates it. The error rate must be measured with replicate genotyping of the same samples, and the matching criteria must be stated. Cumming (2014) argues that the number of individuals should be reported with its interval, and Ioannidis (2005) described how a count that ignores the error rate may be mistaken for a precise estimate. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended recording the reference library version and the matching criteria.
