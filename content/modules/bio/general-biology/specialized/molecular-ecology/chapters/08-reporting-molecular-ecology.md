A molecular ecology report makes inferences about nature from genetic data, and the reader must be able to judge whether those inferences are sound. The study organism, the site and the sampling design should be described first, with the number of samples and the way they were chosen. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a molecular ecology study those means include the samples, the markers and the analysis.

The sample type and its handling should be reported, along with the markers and the genotyping error rate. Taberlet et al. (2012) showed that the sample and the method strongly affect the species and individuals detected, and the report should state them. The reference libraries used for identification should be given with their versions, since a species name depends on the library.

The analysis and its assumptions must be stated, and the estimates should be given with their intervals and, where relevant, their detection probabilities. Cumming (2014) argues that the uncertainty of each estimate should be shown, and Ioannidis (2005) described how an inference that depends on untested assumptions may not describe the population studied.

The genotypes, the sequences and the analysis code should be deposited with identifiers. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs together, so that an inference about nature can be checked, reanalysed and combined with other studies.
