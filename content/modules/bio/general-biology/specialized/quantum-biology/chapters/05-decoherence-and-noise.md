Decoherence is the process by which a quantum system loses its coherence through interaction with its environment. The system becomes entangled with the surrounding degrees of freedom, and the phase relations that define the superposition are spread into the environment, where they cannot be observed. In a biological molecule the environment includes the protein scaffold, the surrounding water and ions, and the vibrations of the molecule itself, and each of these acts as a source of noise that drives the decay of coherence.

The rate at which coherence decays depends on the spectral density of the noise, which describes how the fluctuations of the environment are distributed across frequencies. Noise at frequencies that match the energy spacings of the quantum states is most effective at destroying coherence, while noise at other frequencies has less effect. Lambert et al. (2012) emphasised that the spectral density must be known to estimate the lifetime of coherence, and in biological systems it is poorly characterised.

Some proposals suggest that the biological environment could protect coherence. A structured environment, in which the noise is concentrated at frequencies that do not affect the transition, could preserve coherence longer than a random bath. Narrowing the energy distribution of the states, so that they are less disturbed by fluctuations, is another proposed protection. Each proposal must be tested with a model that reproduces the observed lifetime, since an unmodelled claim of protection is easy to make and hard to check.

The noise and the lifetime must be measured with their uncertainties, and the model that relates them should be stated. Cumming (2014) argues that the lifetime should be reported with its interval, since it is estimated from fits to noisy spectra. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended recording the spectral analysis and the model used, so that the lifetime can be reproduced and the protection mechanism tested. Ioannidis (2005) described how a model can be adjusted to fit a desired lifetime, and the model should be fixed before the data are analysed.
