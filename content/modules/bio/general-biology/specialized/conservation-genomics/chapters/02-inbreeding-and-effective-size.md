Inbreeding can be estimated from genomic data by finding the stretches of the genome in which an individual is homozygous, called runs of homozygosity. A parent who is related to the other parent passes on identical segments from both sides of the family, and these segments form long runs of homozygosity. The total length of such runs as a proportion of the genome gives the inbreeding coefficient, and their length reveals the age of the inbreeding, since long runs arise from recent relatedness and short runs from distant relatedness.

The effective population size is the size of an idealised population that would lose diversity at the same rate as the real one. It is usually smaller than the census size, because the sex ratio may be unequal, because the number of breeding individuals varies between years, and because some individuals contribute many offspring while most contribute few. Allendorf et al. (2010) emphasised that the effective size is the quantity that determines the loss of diversity, and it is therefore the key quantity for management.

Linkage disequilibrium, the non-random association of alleles at nearby loci, reveals the effective size over recent generations. In a small population, drift creates linkage that decays slowly with recombination, and the strength of the linkage among loci at a given distance gives an estimate of the effective size. The estimate is sensitive to the sample size and to the recombination map, and both must be reported.

Management thresholds for the effective population size are often set at values such as fifty or five hundred, but the thresholds are rules of thumb that depend on the species and the goal. The estimates are uncertain, especially in small samples, and the uncertainty must be reported before a threshold is applied. Cumming (2014) argues that the effective size should be reported with its interval, and Ioannidis (2005) described how a threshold applied to an uncertain estimate may lead to the wrong decision. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended recording the sample, the markers and the estimation method.
