A plant physiology result depends on the conditions in which the plant grew and was measured, and a report must state them. The species, the genotype and the growth stage should be given, since a physiological response can change with each. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a physiology study those means include the plants, the growth conditions and the measurement procedure.

The growth conditions must be reported in full, including the light intensity and its spectrum, the temperature, the humidity, the carbon dioxide concentration and the nutrient supply. Ainsworth and Long (2004) showed that the responses to carbon dioxide and temperature depend on these conditions, and a result from one set of conditions may not hold in another. The treatment and its duration should be stated.

The measurement methods must be described with their calibration, and the time of day and the conditions at measurement should be reported, since many physiological processes vary through the day. The sample sizes and the number of replicates should be given, and the statistical analysis should be stated with the intervals of the responses. Cumming (2014) argues that each response should be shown with its precision, and Ioannidis (2005) described how a response measured at a favourable time may mislead about the typical behaviour.

The data and the analysis code should be deposited with identifiers. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs of an analysis together, which allows a physiological result to be reproduced and compared with others.
