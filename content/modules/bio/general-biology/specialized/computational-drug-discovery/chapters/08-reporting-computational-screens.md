A computational screen can be tested and reproduced only if its target, library, method and criteria are fully described. The target and the structure used should be given with their source and their preparation, since the choice of structure can change which compounds are found. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a screen those means include the library, the method and its settings.

The library should be described with its origin and the preparation applied to it, and the docking or ligand-based method should be stated with its settings and its scoring function. Shoichet (2004) showed that the outcome of a screen depends on these choices, and they must be reported. The selection criteria and the number of compounds tested should be given, along with the reasons for the selection.

The validation must be reported, including the enrichment, the decoys used and the prospective tests. The experimental confirmation should describe the assays and the orthogonal checks used to rule out artefacts. Ioannidis (2005) described how a screen reported without its selection criteria and its confirmation may be misleading, and Cumming (2014) argues that the hit rate should be reported with its interval and the random comparison.

The library, the scores, the settings and the code should be deposited with identifiers. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs of an analysis together, so that the screen can be repeated and its hits checked by others.
