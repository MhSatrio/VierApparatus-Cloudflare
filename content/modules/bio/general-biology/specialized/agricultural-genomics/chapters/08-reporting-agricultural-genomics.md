An agricultural genomics report must allow another group to reproduce its discoveries and to apply its predictions in their own breeding or research. The crop, the cultivar or population and its origin should be described, along with the structure of the panel or the training population, since the structure affects every result. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for an agricultural study those means include the genotypes, the phenotypes and the environment.

The genotyping platform and the quality of the markers should be reported, along with the phenotypes and the method of their measurement. The environments of each trial and their management should be described, since a prediction or a discovery depends on the conditions in which the crop grew. Heffner et al. (2009) showed that the accuracy of genomic prediction depends on the training population and its environments, and the report should state them.

The statistical model and its validation must be given, along with the accuracy of prediction and the effect sizes of the discoveries, each with its interval. The candidate genes and whether they have been confirmed by a functional test should be stated, since a candidate that has not been tested is a hypothesis. Cumming (2014) argues that each estimate should be shown with its precision, and Ioannidis (2005) described how a discovery reported without its validation may not hold in other populations.

The genotypes, the phenotypes, the environmental data and the analysis code should be deposited with identifiers. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs together, so that a breeding or research claim can be checked and repeated.
