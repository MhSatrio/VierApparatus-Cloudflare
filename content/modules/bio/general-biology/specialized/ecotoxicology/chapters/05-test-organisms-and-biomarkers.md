Toxicity tests use organisms that can be cultured, that respond reliably to chemicals and that are representative of the groups at risk. Water fleas, algae, small fish and earthworms are among the standard test species, each chosen for its sensitivity and its ease of handling. The tests measure endpoints such as survival, growth and reproduction, which are directly relevant to the fitness of a population. The choice of species and endpoint determines what the test can detect, and each choice must be justified.

Biomarkers are measurable changes in the cells or the molecules of an organism that indicate exposure or an early response to it. Examples include the induction of detoxifying enzymes, the formation of oxidative damage and the change in the expression of particular genes. Biomarkers can show exposure before any harm is visible, which makes them useful for early warning, but their relevance depends on whether they are linked to an effect that matters for the organism or the population.

The link between a biomarker and an ecological effect must be demonstrated before the biomarker is used as a measure of risk. A change in an enzyme may indicate a response that is adaptive and harmless, or one that presages damage, and the interpretation requires evidence from exposures that also produce measurable harm. Ioannidis (2005) described how a marker that is useful in one setting may mislead in another, and the validation must cover the range of conditions in which the marker will be used.

Laboratory strains may differ from wild populations in their sensitivity, because they have been selected for convenience and have lost the variation that natural populations carry. The results of a test on a laboratory strain may therefore underestimate or overestimate the risk to wild organisms. The strain, its origin and its culture conditions should be reported, and the endpoints should be measured with their intervals. Cumming (2014) argues that the variation between replicates should be shown, and Sandve et al. (2013) recommended recording the species, the strain and the conditions for every test.
