Some toxicants are stored in the fat and other tissues of organisms faster than they are removed, and their concentrations in organisms can exceed those in the surrounding water or food. Bioconcentration is the uptake of a compound from water alone, measured as the ratio of its concentration in the organism to that in the water. Bioaccumulation adds the uptake from food, and it is the more important process for compounds that are poorly soluble in water and that concentrate in lipids.

Biomagnification is the increase in concentration at each step of a food chain. A predator that eats many prey can accumulate the compound from all of them, and the concentration in the predator may be many times that in its prey. The effect is most pronounced for compounds that are persistent and that the organism cannot break down or excrete readily, and it can make top predators the most exposed organisms in a system.

The measures of these processes include the bioaccumulation factor and the trophic magnification factor, which summarises the change in concentration with trophic level. The estimate depends on the species, the tissue and the time at which the organisms were sampled, and each must be reported. A measure from one species may not describe another, and the trophic position of each organism should be established before the factor is estimated.

Body size, lipid content, metabolism and the capacity to excrete a compound all influence its accumulation. Larger and fattier organisms may hold more of a lipophilic compound, while organisms that metabolise the compound may clear it. These differences mean that the accumulation of a given compound varies among species, and the variation must be measured rather than assumed. Cumming (2014) argues that the factors should be reported with their intervals across individuals, and Ioannidis (2005) described how a factor estimated from a few individuals may not describe the population. The exposure of humans who eat top predators depends on the same processes, and the reporting should note this link.
