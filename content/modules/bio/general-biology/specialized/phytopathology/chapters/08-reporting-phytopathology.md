A phytopathology report must allow another group to repeat the disease test and to apply its results. The pathogen should be identified with its species, its isolate and, where relevant, its race, since the resistance of a host depends on the race it meets. Peng (2011) argued that a result is reproducible only with the means of producing it, and for a disease test those means include the isolate, the host and the procedure. Dangl and Jones (2001) showed that the outcome of an infection depends on the pair of host and pathogen, so both must be described.

The host should be given with its genotype and its growth stage, and the inoculation method should be stated with the inoculum density and the timing. The environmental conditions of the test, including temperature, humidity and light, should be reported, since they determine the success of infection. Without these the result cannot be applied to another environment.

The disease assessment must be described with its scale, the number of plants and plots assessed, and the statistical design. The disease measures should be reported with their intervals, and the variation across replicates should be shown. Cumming (2014) argues that the precision of each measure must be visible, and Ioannidis (2005) described how a subjective assessment may bias the result unless it is made blind to treatment.

Where diagnostics were used, their validation should be reported, and the isolate data and the analysis should be deposited with identifiers. Sandve et al. (2013) recommended keeping the inputs, steps and outputs together, so that a disease result can be reproduced and its recommendations checked.
