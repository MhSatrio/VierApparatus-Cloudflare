When X-rays strike an ordered crystal, the waves scattered by the atoms reinforce one another only in certain directions, and the condition for reinforcement is the Bragg law, which relates the wavelength, the angle of incidence and the spacing of the planes of atoms in the crystal. Each set of planes gives a spot at a characteristic angle, and the pattern of spots is a map of the lattice. Watson and Crick (1953) read the spacings of DNA from the fibre diffraction pattern in the same way, and the principle is the basis of all crystallographic data.

To record the three-dimensional pattern, the crystal is rotated in the beam, and the diffraction is collected as a series of images, each recording the spots that satisfy the Bragg condition at that orientation. The complete set of images must cover enough of the reciprocal space to define the structure. The completeness of the data, the proportion of the possible spots that are measured, is one of the measures of quality.

Redundancy is the number of times each unique spot is measured. A spot measured several times in different images gives a more reliable intensity, because the errors average, and the consistency of repeated measurements is an estimate of the error. The signal-to-noise ratio of the intensities and the resolution to which they extend determine how much information the data contain.

Radiation damage limits the data. The X-ray beam breaks the chemical bonds of the molecules in the crystal, and the diffraction fades as the crystal is irradiated. Cooling the crystal to low temperature reduces the damage, and the data are often collected from a single frozen crystal. The damage must be monitored, and the data from a damaged crystal are not reliable.

Ioannidis (2005) described how the statistics of a data set can be made to look better than they are by the choice of which data to include, and Platt (1964) recommended reporting all the measurements, including those rejected, so that the selection is visible.

The diffraction pattern is the raw record of the crystal, and its quality limits every structure derived from it.
