A crystal structure is a claim that the atoms are arranged in a particular way, and the claim can be judged only from the report of the work that produced it. The report must state the crystallisation conditions, including the protein, the precipitant, the pH and the temperature, since these determine the crystal form and may influence the structure. Kendrew et al. (1958) described their crystals and conditions in enough detail for the work to be followed, and the same standard applies now.

The data collection statistics must be reported: the resolution, the completeness, the redundancy, the signal-to-noise ratio and the merging statistics. The phasing method and its statistics are given, since the phases determine the model and their quality limits its accuracy. The refinement statistics are reported with the free residual, which Brünger (1992) established as the honest measure of the model's agreement with the data, together with the geometry statistics and the number of atoms and water molecules placed.

The validation report accompanies the structure, and the regions of the model that are poorly determined, such as flexible loops, are identified. The report also states any alternative conformations, the occupancy of the atoms and the temperature factors, since these describe the uncertainty of the model. A structure reported without its uncertainty invites an over-reading of its detail.

The coordinates and the diffraction data are deposited in a public archive, which allows others to check the model and to recompute the statistics. The deposition of the data, as well as the coordinates, is what makes the structure verifiable, and a structure that is described but whose data are not available cannot be independently assessed.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a result reported with selected statistics and without its data can be accepted on the authority of the publication, and Platt (1964) recommended a report that gives the means of its own refutation, namely the data from which another group could reach a different conclusion.

A crystal structure report is a complete account of the experiment and the model, and its value lies in allowing any reader to test the claims against the data.
