An NMR structure is a set of restraints and an ensemble calculated from them, and it can be reproduced only if the restraints and the calculation are reported. The sample must be described, including the protein construct, the isotope labelling, the buffer, the pH, the temperature and the concentration, since the conditions determine the structure and the exchange. Ioannidis (2005) described how the failure to report the conditions of a measurement makes its results impossible to test, and the NMR report must give the conditions in full.

The experiments performed are listed with their parameters, such as the mixing times of the nuclear Overhauser experiments, since these determine the spin diffusion and the reliability of the distances. The completeness of the assignment is stated, with the residues and atoms that could not be assigned, and the number and types of restraints are given, including distance, dihedral and any residual dipolar coupling restraints, along with the number per residue.

The structure calculation protocol is described, including the force field, the annealing schedule, the weights of the restraint and force field terms and the number of structures calculated and retained. The ensemble statistics are reported: the violations of the restraints, the deviations from ideal geometry and the precision of the ensemble. Anfinsen (1973) showed that a protein's fold follows from its chain, and the report should make clear which features of the ensemble are determined by the restraints and which are the product of the force field.

Accuracy is assessed separately from precision. The report should state the evidence that the structure is correct, such as the agreement with the mutational effects on function, the comparison with a crystal or cryo-EM structure or the validation of the restraints by independent measurements. Platt (1964) recommended stating the claims in a form that could be refuted, and an ensemble with its evidence allows that test.

The deposition of the restraints, the chemical shift assignments and the coordinates in public archives is part of the report. The restraints and assignments allow the calculation to be repeated, and their availability is what makes an NMR structure checkable.

An NMR report is an account of the data, the assignments, the restraints and the calculation, and it is complete only when another group could reach the same ensemble from them.
