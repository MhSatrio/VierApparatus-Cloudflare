The most informative single-molecule measurements would be made in the living cell, where the molecule acts in its natural environment. Tracking individual molecules inside cells is now possible for proteins labelled with bright fluorophores, and the trajectories reveal how molecules diffuse, bind and are confined. Singer and Nicolson (1972) proposed the fluid membrane through which many such molecules move, and single-molecule tracking has confirmed that much of the membrane motion is restricted, as the earlier topic on membrane biophysics described.

The cell is a difficult environment for measurement. The background fluorescence of the cytoplasm is high, the molecules are crowded and the label may not be as bright as in vitro. The molecule may be present in very low numbers, and the tracking must follow it through a volume that contains many similar molecules. Moerner and Orrit (1999) described the detection of single molecules in a controlled setting, and the extension to cells requires further methods to suppress the background and to identify the molecule of interest.

The behaviour observed in the cell may differ from that observed in vitro. The partners, the crowding, the membrane and the cytoskeleton all differ, and a molecule that was measured as a free particle in solution may be restricted or engaged with others in the cell. The question of how far an in vitro result represents the cell is therefore a central one, and it must be answered by comparing the two environments.

The comparison is made by perturbing the cell. The partners are removed or added, the cytoskeleton is disrupted, and the change in the behaviour of the molecule is measured. A molecule whose behaviour changes as predicted by the environment has shown that the environment is responsible, and the prediction is a test of the understanding.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a result from a single cell or a single molecule may be generalised to the population without the replication that the generalisation requires, and Platt (1964) recommended the perturbation test that distinguishes the cell's influence from the molecule's own behaviour.

Single molecules in the cell reveal the behaviour of the molecule in its natural setting, and the value of the result depends on showing how the setting shapes what is seen.
