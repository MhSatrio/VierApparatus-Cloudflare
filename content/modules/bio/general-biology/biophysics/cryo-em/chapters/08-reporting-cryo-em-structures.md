A cryo-EM result depends on many processing choices, and it can be reproduced only if those choices are reported. The sample and its preparation must be described, including the buffer, the concentration, the grid type, the blotting conditions and the time between preparation and freezing. The imaging conditions, including the voltage, the dose, the defocus range and the detector, determine the quality of the images, and they must be stated. Ioannidis (2005) described how the processing of noisy data offers many choices that change the result, and the report is the place where those choices are set out.

The processing must be described in full. The number of particles picked and retained, the criteria for their selection, the classification procedure with its number of classes and the reference used for alignment are all required, and the software and its version are named. The alignment from an independent reference, or a statement that none was used, is needed to show that the reference has not biased the result. Platt (1964) recommended that the report state the conditions under which the claim could fail, and the reference bias is one such condition.

The resolution is reported by the half-map Fourier shell correlation, with the threshold used and the curve shown. The model is reported with its validation, including the fit to the map, the geometry and the comparison with the half-maps, following the principle Brünger (1992) set out for crystal structures. The regions of the model that are poorly determined are identified.

The deposition of the data is essential. The maps, the half-maps and the models are deposited in public archives, together with the raw images or the particle stacks where possible, so that the processing can be repeated and the classification can be examined by others. A structure whose processing cannot be checked cannot be fully assessed.

Anfinsen (1973) showed that the structure of a protein is a defined arrangement, and a cryo-EM report must make clear which of the features it describes are determined by the data and which are inferred from the model.

A cryo-EM structure is a reconstruction from many choices, and the report is complete only when those choices and their consequences can be seen by the reader.
