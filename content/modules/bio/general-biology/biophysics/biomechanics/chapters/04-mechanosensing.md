A cell responds to mechanical force by changing its behaviour, and the conversion of force into a chemical signal, mechanosensing, depends on proteins whose conformation changes when they are loaded. A mechanosensitive channel opens when the membrane tension stretches it, in a manner parallel to the voltage-gated channels that Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) described, with the force replacing the voltage as the gating stimulus. The channel's gating is a conformational change driven by the work that the force does on it, and the open probability depends on the tension.

Force-bearing proteins in the cell's adhesions and cytoskeleton also change conformation under load. A protein that unfolds or extends when pulled may expose a binding site or a phosphorylation site, and the signal is generated by the exposure. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the focal adhesions that link the actin cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix, and the proteins in these adhesions are the candidates for force sensing.

The cell's response to mechanics includes changes in growth, division and differentiation. Cells on stiff substrates often spread and proliferate, while those on soft substrates may remain rounded, and the stem cell fate can be biased by the stiffness of the matrix. The responses are mediated by signalling pathways that the mechanical cues activate, and they link the mechanics of the cell to its gene expression.

The mechanism must be tested. A proposed mechanosensor is shown to respond to force by applying a controlled load and measuring the change in its activity or conformation, and the response is shown to depend on the sensor by removing or altering it. Ioannidis (2005) described how a sensor that is proposed from its location may be assumed to respond to force without the direct test, and Platt (1964) recommended the control that removes the proposed sensor and tests whether the response disappears.

Mechanosensing converts force into a signal through conformational change, and its demonstration requires applying a controlled load and removing the proposed sensor.
