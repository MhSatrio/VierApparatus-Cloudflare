Equilibrium constants and free energies are measured by observing how the populations of states change with a controlled variable, such as the concentration of a ligand, the temperature or the concentration of a denaturant. Titration follows the fraction of bound molecules as the ligand is added, and spectroscopy follows a signal that differs between the states. Calorimetry measures the heat absorbed or released as the populations shift, which gives the enthalpy directly. Each method turns a population into a measured quantity, and each depends on assumptions about what the signal reports.

The principal assumption is that the system is at equilibrium when the measurement is made. A slow reaction that has not reached equilibrium gives a population that depends on the time and the route taken, and the apparent constant is then a kinetic quantity. Anfinsen (1973) measured the refolding of a protein after denaturation, and the time course of the refolding was checked to confirm that the final state was reached. Equilibrium must be shown by approaching it from both directions, since a system that is at a steady state away from equilibrium will not show the same value from both sides.

A second assumption is about the states present. A two-state model assumes that the folded and unfolded populations are the only ones, and a signal that changes in one transition is interpreted as the whole of the change. An intermediate state that is populated in part of the transition is missed by the two-state fit, and the free energy is then wrongly estimated. Ioannidis (2005) described how a simple model applied to a complex process produces a result that looks clean but misrepresents the system.

Platt (1964) recommended a test that could separate the models, and an independent measure that reports the enthalpy, such as calorimetry, can show whether the van't Hoff enthalpy from the titration agrees with the direct value. A disagreement shows that the two-state assumption fails.

The equilibrium measurement reads the populations, and the reading is trustworthy only when the system has reached equilibrium and the states it occupies have been identified.
