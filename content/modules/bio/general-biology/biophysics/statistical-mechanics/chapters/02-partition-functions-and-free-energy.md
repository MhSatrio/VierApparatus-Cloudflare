The partition function is the sum over all the states of a system of the Boltzmann weight of each state. It is a single number that contains the full thermodynamic information about the system, and the free energy follows from it by a logarithm. Zwanzig (1954) showed how the partition function of an interacting gas could be approximated by perturbation, and the same technique applies to the interactions of molecules in solution, where the exact sum over states is intractable.

The free energy is the quantity that the earlier topic used to predict the direction of reactions. Its relation to the partition function is the link between the microscopic states of molecules and the macroscopic free energy change. A system with a large partition function has many accessible states and a low free energy, which is why entropy and energy both enter the free energy of a reaction.

The equilibrium constant of a binding reaction is the ratio of the populations of the bound and unbound states, and it is determined by the difference in their free energies. A tight binding reaction has a large equilibrium constant because the bound state is much more populated, and the dissociation constant is the concentration at which half the molecules are bound. These are the quantities measured in the binding assays of the biochemistry modules.

The folding of a protein is an equilibrium between the folded and unfolded states, and the free energy of unfolding is calculated from the ratio of their populations. Anfinsen (1973) showed that the folded state is the one with the lower free energy under physiological conditions, and the partition function sums over the many unfolded conformations that contribute to the unfolded population.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a free energy calculated from a model of the states may depend on which states the model includes, and the choice of states must be justified. Platt (1964) recommended a comparison of the calculated free energy with the value measured by calorimetry or by equilibrium titration, which tests whether the model captures the relevant states.

The partition function is the sum of the possibilities, and the free energy is the logarithm of that sum, weighted by the temperature.
