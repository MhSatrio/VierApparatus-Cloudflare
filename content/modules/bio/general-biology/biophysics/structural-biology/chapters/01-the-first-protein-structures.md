The structure of myoglobin, the oxygen-storing protein of muscle, was the first protein structure determined at atomic detail. Kendrew et al. (1958) built a model of the chain that fitted the electron density obtained from crystals, and the model showed that the polypeptide folds into a compact arrangement of helices with a heme group held in a pocket. The work took the better part of two decades, and it established that proteins have definite three-dimensional architectures that can be determined from their diffraction.

The model revealed how the protein's function is accommodated by its fold. The heme sits in a cleft lined by the side chains of the helices, which protect the iron from the solvent and position the oxygen-binding site. The arrangement explains how a single chain can bind a small ligand with high affinity while excluding water, and it linked the physical structure to the function of storing oxygen.

The work built on the method that Watson and Crick (1953) used for DNA, in which the structure is inferred from the diffraction pattern and the chemistry of the molecule. The myoglobin structure extended this by solving the phase problem for a protein of much greater complexity, and the methods developed for it became the standard of the field.

The early structure also raised the question that Anfinsen (1973) answered. If the fold is defined by the chain, the sequence must determine the structure, and the structural determination of many proteins became the means of testing the principle. The structure was thus both a result and a motivation for the study of folding.

Ioannidis (2005) described how the historical accounts of early structures may present a clean path from data to model, while the real work involved many revisions. Platt (1964) recommended a test of a model against independent measurements, and the early structural work proceeded through exactly such revisions as the data improved.

The first protein structures established that folded chains have definite architectures, and the methods that determined them remain the basis of the field.
