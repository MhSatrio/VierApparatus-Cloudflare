A structure describes a molecule as a single arrangement of atoms, and many molecules are better described as a population of arrangements. Anfinsen (1973) established that a sequence folds into a defined structure, and for many well-folded proteins the single structure is an excellent approximation. Many others contain flexible loops, disordered regions and domains that move relative to one another, and for these the single structure describes only the state that happened to crystallise or be imaged, and it may omit the other states that matter for function.

The environment of a structure matters. A crystal lattice holds the molecules in contacts that may fix a conformation the molecule does not adopt in solution, and a frozen film of a membrane protein may hold it in a detergent or a lipid arrangement that differs from the membrane. The structure is a faithful image of the molecule in its experimental condition, and its extension to the cell requires that the difference be assessed.

The conformational ensemble describes the molecule as a set of structures weighted by their populations. Ensemble descriptions are constructed by combining structures in multiple states with measurements of dynamics, such as NMR relaxation and hydrogen exchange, that report on the distribution. The ensemble is more faithful than a single structure, but it is also less determined, and its populations depend on the models used to interpret the dynamic data.

Function often depends on the minor states. A rare conformation, populated only a small fraction of the time, may be the active or the binding state, and it may be invisible to a structure determined from the dominant state. The discovery of such states requires methods that detect minor populations, and the static structure may be silent about them.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a single structure can be treated as the molecule, when it is one state among several, and Platt (1964) recommended the combination of structural and dynamic measurements that could show which states are present and which are functional.

A static structure is a valuable description of a molecule in one state, and the molecule's function may depend on states that the structure does not show.
