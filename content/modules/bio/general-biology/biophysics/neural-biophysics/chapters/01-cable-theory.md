A dendrite or an axon is a cable: a long cylinder of cytoplasm surrounded by a membrane that is both resistive and capacitive. When a current is injected at one point, the voltage spreads along the cable and decays with distance, and the decay is described by the cable equation, which combines the axial resistance of the cytoplasm with the membrane resistance and capacitance. Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) worked on the squid axon, which is a large cable whose passive properties were among the quantities they measured, and the cable equation describes the spread that their active model builds upon.

The length constant is the distance over which the voltage falls to a fraction of its value at the point of injection, and it is set by the ratio of the membrane resistance to the axial resistance. A thicker cable has a lower axial resistance and a longer length constant, which is why large axons carry signals passively over greater distances. The time constant is the time for the membrane to charge, and it is set by the membrane capacitance and resistance. A synaptic input is therefore smoothed in time and attenuated in space as it travels through the dendrite.

Dendritic trees branch, and the branching changes the attenuation. An input at a distal branch is attenuated as it travels to the cell body, and the attenuation depends on the geometry of the branches between. The arrival of inputs at the soma is therefore a function of their location, and the same synapse at different positions contributes differently to the output. The principle is important for understanding how a neuron weighs its inputs.

The passive model is valid only where the membrane is passive. Active channels amplify some signals and attenuate others, and the presence of voltage-gated channels in dendrites makes the spread of a signal depend on its amplitude and history. The passive cable is therefore a first approximation, and its limits must be assessed for each neuron.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a cable model fitted to a neuron's response may be applied where the passive assumption does not hold, and Platt (1964) recommended measurements that could test the passive model, such as the linearity of summation at distal sites, which the active channels would violate.

The dendrite is a cable whose geometry and membrane properties determine how inputs arrive at the cell body, and the passive model describes this where the active channels are absent.
