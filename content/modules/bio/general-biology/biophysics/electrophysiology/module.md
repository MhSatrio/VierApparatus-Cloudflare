# Electrophysiology

Cells that are excitable, including neurons, muscle and the cells of the heart, communicate and act through electrical signals. Electrophysiology measures these signals, and it is the means by which the ionic mechanisms of excitability are discovered and tested. The Membrane Biophysics and Channels topics described the physics of ion channels and membranes, and this topic describes how their activity is recorded in living cells and what the recordings show.

The chapters begin with the voltage clamp that Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) used to separate the ionic currents of the nerve membrane, and the patch clamp that Neher and Sakmann (1976) introduced to record the currents of individual channels. The recording of the membrane potential, the analysis of the currents and the identification of the channels responsible follow. The extracellular recording of populations of cells and the electrophysiology of the heart and the nerve are then described, and the final chapters address the reporting of recordings and the interpretation of abnormal activity.

Electrophysiological recordings are sensitive to the conditions of the preparation, the quality of the seal and the settings of the amplifier, and the interpretation of a current depends on the identification of the channels that carry it. Ioannidis (2005) described how a current that is attributed to one channel by its pharmacology may be carried by another, and Platt (1964) recommended the specific tests that separate the candidates.

Alberts et al. (2022) describe the channels and transporters that produce the electrical activity of cells, and the recordings are a direct readout of their behaviour.
