Neher and Sakmann (1976) recorded the currents through individual channels by pressing a fire-polished glass pipette against the membrane and applying suction. A seal of very high resistance, of the order of a gigaohm, forms between the glass and the membrane, and it electrically isolates the small patch of membrane under the pipette. Any current that flows through the patch must pass through the channels within it, and the recording reveals the discrete openings of individual channels as steps of current.

The seal is the key to the technique. Its resistance reduces the noise of the recording to a level at which the small single-channel current, of a few picoamperes, can be detected. The formation of the seal depends on the cleanliness of the membrane and the glass, and the seal must be stable throughout the recording. A leaky seal adds a current that is not from the channels and obscures the events.

Several configurations are used. In the cell-attached configuration the pipette records from the patch of an intact cell, leaving its interior undisturbed. In the whole-cell configuration the membrane under the pipette is broken to give access to the cell's interior, and the current from the whole cell is recorded. In the excised configurations the patch is removed from the cell, and the inside or outside of the membrane can be exposed to different solutions. Each configuration answers a different question about the channel.

The single-channel openings are analysed to give the conductance, from the size of the current step, and the gating, from the durations of the open and closed periods. The statistics of the openings reveal how the channel moves between its states, and the Markov models described in the stochastic processes topic are fitted to these records. The analysis requires a sufficient number of events, and the detection threshold must be set in advance.

Ioannidis (2005) described how the identification of a channel from its single-channel behaviour may depend on the analysis choices, and Platt (1964) recommended the consistency of the measured conductance and kinetics across patches and conditions as a test of the identification.

The patch clamp isolates a single channel in a small seal and reads its openings, and the conclusions depend on the seal, the configuration and the analysis of the events.
