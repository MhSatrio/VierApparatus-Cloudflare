In current clamp the membrane is left free to change its potential, and a current is injected to depolarise it. When the depolarisation reaches threshold, the cell fires an action potential, a rapid, stereotyped change in the potential that returns to rest after a brief interval. The recording shows the threshold, the amplitude of the spike and the duration, and the refractory period during which a second spike is difficult to evoke. Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) described the spike in terms of the sodium and potassium currents that compose it, and current-clamp recordings show the spike that those currents produce.

The shape of the action potential differs among cell types, and the differences reflect the channels each cell expresses. A neuron has a brief spike driven by fast sodium channels and rapid potassium repolarisation. A cardiac myocyte has a long plateau, sustained by a slow calcium current, which prolongs the refractory period and prevents the sustained tetanic contraction that would stop the heart. A muscle fibre has a spike of intermediate duration, adapted to its contraction. The shape is therefore a readout of the complement of channels, and changes in the shape indicate changes in that complement.

The threshold depends on the balance of the inward and outward currents at the start of the spike. A small change in the sodium channel's activation or in the potassium current can shift the threshold, and the change in threshold is a sensitive indicator of altered excitability. The measurement of threshold must be made with the same protocol in each cell, since the stimulus duration and the resting potential affect the result.

The refractory period has two phases, the absolute period during which no spike can be elicited, and the relative period during which a stronger stimulus is required. These reflect the inactivation of the sodium channels and the slow recovery of the channels that regulate the spike, and their measurement reveals the state of the channels.

Ioannidis (2005) described how the shape of a spike recorded in one cell type may be attributed to a channel composition without measuring the composition, and Platt (1964) recommended a test that could separate the attribution, such as a specific blocker of the proposed channel and an observation of the change in the spike that the blocker produces.

The action potential is the output of a cell's channels, and its shape and threshold indicate the composition and state of those channels.
