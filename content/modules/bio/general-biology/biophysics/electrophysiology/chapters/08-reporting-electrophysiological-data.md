An electrophysiological result depends on many details of the recording, and a current or a potential can be altered by any of them. The composition of the intracellular and extracellular solutions determines the ionic gradients and the currents, and the solutions must be reported in full, including the concentrations of the ions, the buffers and the drugs. The temperature is essential, since the kinetics of the channels depend strongly on it. Ioannidis (2005) described how the failure to report the conditions makes an electrophysiological result impossible to repeat, and the report must give the conditions in full.

The recording configuration and its quality must be described. For patch clamp, the seal resistance, the access resistance and the configuration are reported, along with the stability of the seal over the recording. For voltage clamp, the series resistance and the compensation are stated. These determine the accuracy of the recorded current, and a recording with poor series resistance compensation distorts the kinetics of fast currents.

The voltage protocols are described with their steps, durations and the holding potentials, since the currents depend on the protocol. The subtraction of leak and capacitive currents, and the criteria by which recordings were included or rejected, are stated. Platt (1964) recommended that the controls be reported so that the claim could be tested, and the subtraction method and the blockers used for identification are among the controls.

The analysis must state the criteria for the detection of events, the fitting procedures and the statistics. The number of cells and the number of independent recordings are reported, since the cell is the unit of replication and recordings from one cell are not independent. The sample size and the variation between cells determine the reliability of the conclusions.

The raw traces are deposited, where possible, with the protocols and the analysis code. The traces allow others to repeat the analysis and to test the identification of the currents, and their availability is part of a transparent report.

The electrophysiological report is a record of the recording conditions and the analysis, and its completeness determines whether the currents it describes can be reproduced.
