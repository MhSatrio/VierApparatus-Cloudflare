Anfinsen (1973) established that the sequence of a protein contains the information needed for it to fold into its native structure, and that the native structure is stable under physiological conditions. He showed that a denatured protein could refold spontaneously when the denaturant was removed, which indicated that the native fold is the state of lowest free energy in the environment of the cell. The result made the fold a thermodynamic state, determined by the energies of the interactions among the residues and between the protein and the solvent.

The stability of a fold is the small difference between two large opposing terms. The folded chain has fewer possible conformations than the unfolded chain, which is an unfavourable entropy change, and the folding is driven by the favourable formation of hydrogen bonds, van der Waals contacts and the burial of hydrophobic residues. The balance between enthalpy and entropy is delicate, and a protein is stable by only a few kilocalories per mole, which is why small changes in temperature or the solvent can unfold it.

The hydrophobic effect is the dominant driving force for most folds. Nonpolar side chains disrupt the structure of the surrounding water, and when they are buried in the interior of the protein, the water is released, and its entropy increases. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the arrangement of hydrophobic and polar residues that results, and the hydrophobic effect explains why the interior of a folded protein is packed with nonpolar side chains.

Folding can be reversed by heat, denaturants and changes in pH, and the reversal reveals the thermodynamics. Some proteins do not fold reversibly, and some fail to fold without the help of chaperones, which prevent the aggregation of intermediates. The failure of folding is the basis of many diseases in which misfolded proteins accumulate.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a model of folding that fits the equilibrium data may not describe the pathway, and Platt (1964) recommended experiments that could discriminate between the alternative pathways, such as measurements of the intermediates that the models predict.

The fold of a protein is the state that the free energy of its environment selects, and understanding that selection explains both its stability and its fragility.
