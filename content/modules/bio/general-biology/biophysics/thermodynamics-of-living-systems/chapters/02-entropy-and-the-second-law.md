The second law of thermodynamics states that the entropy of an isolated system does not decrease, and it increases in any spontaneous change. Entropy is a measure of the number of arrangements of a system's energy and matter that are consistent with its observed state, and the law expresses the tendency of systems to move towards the more probable arrangements. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the cell as a highly ordered system, and the order is the fact that the second law appears to challenge.

The resolution is that a living system is open. It maintains its internal order by exchanging matter and energy with its surroundings, and it exports entropy to them. A cell that builds a complex protein from simple amino acids decreases its local entropy, but the energy it uses to do so is released as heat and as waste products, whose entropy increases in the surroundings by a larger amount. The total entropy of the cell and its environment increases, as the law requires, while the cell itself becomes more ordered.

Anfinsen (1973) showed that a protein folds into a defined structure, which is a state of lower entropy than the unfolded chain. The folding is spontaneous only when the entropy of the surrounding water increases enough to compensate, which happens because the hydrophobic side chains are released from the water and the water gains freedom. The organisation of the protein is therefore paid for by the disorder of the solvent, and the second law is satisfied for the system as a whole.

The second law also limits the efficiency of conversion between forms of energy. Some energy is always dispersed as heat, and no biological process is perfectly efficient. The efficiency sets the amount of food that an organism must consume to perform a given amount of work.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a claim that living systems violate a physical law can be accepted without examining the accounting that resolves it, and Platt (1964) recommended a test that could separate the alternatives, such as measuring the entropy exported in the heat and the waste products. The resolution is a claim that can be checked against measurement.

Life is not an exception to the second law, but an example of how a system can maintain order while increasing the disorder of its surroundings.
