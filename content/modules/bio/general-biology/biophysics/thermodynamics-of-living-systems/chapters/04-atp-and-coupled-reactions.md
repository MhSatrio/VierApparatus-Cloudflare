Adenosine triphosphate is the principal carrier of energy in the cell. Its hydrolysis to adenosine diphosphate and inorganic phosphate releases a large negative free energy under cellular conditions, because the products are more stable than the reactants, and the phosphate groups are strongly charged and close together. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the high-energy phosphate bonds of ATP and the reasons for their large free energy, and the hydrolysis is used to drive many reactions that would not proceed on their own.

Coupling makes this possible. An unfavourable reaction with a positive free energy change is linked to the hydrolysis of ATP so that the sum of the two reactions is negative. The most common mechanism is the formation of a covalent intermediate: the ATP phosphorylates a substrate, creating a high-energy intermediate that then reacts with the second substrate, and the phosphate is transferred in the process. The overall free energy is the sum of the two steps, and the intermediate carries the energy from the ATP to the reaction that needs it.

The coupling of reactions is therefore a matter of the sum of free energy changes, and the cell can drive a reaction of any positive value by providing enough ATP hydrolysis. The ratio of ATP to ADP and phosphate in the cell determines the free energy available, and the cell maintains this ratio at a high value, which makes the hydrolysis strongly favourable.

ATP is the common currency of energy transfer in the cell, and its use in many reactions allows energy to be shared among processes. Mitchell (1961) showed that the proton gradient can also be used to synthesise ATP, and the two forms of energy are interconverted through the ATP synthase. The currency of ATP and the gradient together link the energy-capturing processes to the energy-spending ones.

The free energy available from ATP in a living cell is larger than the value calculated under standard conditions, because the concentrations of its components are far from standard. Ioannidis (2005) described how a calculated value that depends on concentrations that are not measured may be presented as precise, and Platt (1964) recommended measuring the concentrations directly to test the value.

Coupling is the principle by which the cell does work against the thermodynamic grain, and ATP is the means by which the coupling is paid.
