An ion channel is a protein that forms a pore through the membrane, through which ions flow down their electrochemical gradients at a rate many orders of magnitude faster than any transporter. Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) inferred the existence of voltage-dependent conductances from the membrane currents of the squid axon, and the channels that carry these currents are now known as individual proteins. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the channel families and their roles in excitable cells, and each channel is a structure whose pore is lined with residues that interact with the ion.

Selectivity arises from the arrangement of the pore. A potassium channel admits potassium and excludes sodium, though the two are similar in size, because the carbonyl oxygens lining the selectivity filter mimic the water shell that surrounds potassium and make the passage of sodium energetically unfavourable. The physics of the selectivity filter is the subject of much study, and the ions in the pore are not free but are arranged in a single file with a few binding sites.

Gating opens and closes the pore. The gate is a conformational change that is driven by the voltage across the membrane, in the case of voltage-gated channels, or by the binding of a ligand, in the case of ligand-gated channels. The gating charge that moves across the membrane during the transition is measured as a small current, and the magnitude of this current reveals how many charges move in the gating step. Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) modelled this gating with variables whose voltage dependence they measured, and the later structural studies have identified the moving parts.

Single-channel recording reveals the behaviour of individual molecules. The current through a single open channel is a discrete step, and the sequence of openings and closings reveals the gating kinetics. The macroscopic current of the membrane is the sum of many such channels, and the dynamics of the population follow from the single-channel behaviour.

Ioannidis (2005) described how a model of gating that fits macroscopic currents may not describe the single-channel behaviour, and Platt (1964) recommended single-channel measurements that could discriminate between models of the same macroscopic current.

Channels are selective pores whose gates open in response to a signal, and the electrical behaviour of the membrane is the sum of their individual openings.
