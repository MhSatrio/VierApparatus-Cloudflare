The physics of folding and dynamics is useful when it predicts something that can be tested. The effect of a mutation on stability can be estimated from the change in the forces it introduces, and the effect on function can be estimated from the change in the dynamics it causes. Anfinsen (1973) showed that the fold is determined by the sequence, so a change in the sequence changes the fold, and the physics makes the change predictable in principle. Dill and MacCallum (2012) noted that the prediction of the effects of mutations is one of the practical tests of the understanding of folding.

Engineering a protein requires the same understanding. A new protein is designed by choosing a sequence that folds into a target structure, and computational design searches for sequences whose energy is lowest in that structure. The designed proteins have been made and shown to fold, which tests the physics, but the functions designed into them are often less active than natural ones, and the gap shows the limits of the model.

Shaw et al. (2010) showed that molecular dynamics simulations could reproduce the motions of a protein on timescales that can be compared with experiment, and the simulations are used to explore the states that a drug might bind. Structure-based design uses the shape and the dynamics of a binding site to find ligands that fit, and the approach has produced candidate drugs, but the predicted affinity is often not realised, because the simulated states and the water and the flexibility of the protein are imperfectly represented.

The limits must be stated. Predictions of mutation effects are best for small changes in well-characterised folds, and they are poorer for large changes, for disordered regions and for the effects on dynamics that depend on the environment. Ioannidis (2005) described how a method that succeeds on the cases it was developed for may fail on new ones, and the validation must include independent cases.

Platt (1964) recommended a test that could show a design to be wrong, and the experimental measurement of the stability and the function of each designed or predicted variant is the test, since it shows whether the physics has captured the effect.

The understanding of folding and dynamics is most valuable when it predicts the behaviour of a protein that has not yet been measured, and each prediction is a test of the understanding.
