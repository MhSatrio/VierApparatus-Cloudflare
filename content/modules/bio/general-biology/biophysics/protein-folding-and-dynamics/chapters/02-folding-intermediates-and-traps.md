Some proteins fold through partly structured intermediates, in which parts of the chain have formed their native packing while others remain disordered. An intermediate is a local minimum on the landscape, and the chain may spend time in it before completing the fold. The intermediate can be productive, lying on the pathway to the native state, or unproductive, lying in a side branch from which the chain must unfold before it can continue. Dill and MacCallum (2012) described the difficulty of identifying which intermediates are on the pathway, since both kinds may be populated.

Kinetic traps are misfolded states that are stable enough to hold the chain for a long time. The barrier that separates a trap from the native state is large, and the escape from it is slow. A trap is not necessarily a deep energy minimum; it may be a shallow one that is hard to leave because the local structure must be rearranged. Anfinsen (1973) observed that some proteins refold slowly after denaturation, and the slow refolding is consistent with the presence of traps.

Intermediates and traps are detected by following the kinetics of folding. Rapid mixing methods, which initiate folding in milliseconds, reveal the early events, and spectroscopic signals report the changes in structure at each stage. The signals must be interpreted with a model, and the number of distinct phases in the kinetics is a clue to the number of intermediates. Ioannidis (2005) described how a model with more intermediates can fit more data, and the number must be justified by a test of the model against independent measurements.

The cell helps proteins escape traps. Chaperones bind partly folded chains and prevent them from aggregating, and some provide a sheltered environment in which the chain can fold without interference from other molecules. Alberts et al. (2022) describe the chaperone systems of the cell, and their action shortens the time that chains spend in traps.

Platt (1964) recommended experiments that could distinguish between the pathways, such as a mutation that removes an intermediate and a prediction of the consequence for the folding kinetics.

The path of folding is a sequence of intermediates and traps, and the cell provides means of guiding the chain past the traps that would otherwise delay it.
