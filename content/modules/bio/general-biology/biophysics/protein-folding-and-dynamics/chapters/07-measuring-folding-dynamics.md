Folding and conformational motions span many orders of magnitude in time, and no single method covers them all. Rapid mixing initiates folding by diluting a denatured protein into native conditions and follows the change within milliseconds, while relaxation methods perturb an equilibrium by a sudden jump in temperature, pressure or concentration and measure its return. Anfinsen (1973) measured the refolding of ribonuclease after denaturation, and the methods since have extended the time window in both directions. Each method has a dead time, the shortest interval it can resolve, and motions faster than this are invisible.

Hydrogen exchange reports which parts of a protein are protected from the solvent. Amide hydrogens exchange with deuterium in the solvent at rates that depend on whether they are in a folded, hydrogen-bonded structure, and the pattern of exchange reveals the structure and its stability. Fluorescence reports the environment of a probe, and it can be followed in real time. Single-molecule force spectroscopy pulls a single protein apart and records the forces at which its structure breaks, which reveals the energies of the individual steps. Moerner and Orrit (1999) described the detection of single molecules by optical methods, which underlies many such measurements.

The data from different methods are combined to test a model. A model of folding predicts the rates at each stage, the protected regions and the forces needed to unfold each step, and the predictions can be compared with the measurements from each method. A model that agrees with one method but not another is refuted in part, and the agreement across methods is the stronger evidence.

Each method has its own assumptions, and the interpretation of each signal depends on them. Ioannidis (2005) described how the combination of many methods can be presented as convergent evidence when the methods share a common assumption that is false, and the independence of the methods must be examined.

Platt (1964) recommended a set of tests that could discriminate between the models, and a combination of methods that separate the models where each alone cannot is the means of making progress.

The dynamics of folding are measured across timescales, and the reliability of the picture depends on combining methods whose assumptions are independent.
