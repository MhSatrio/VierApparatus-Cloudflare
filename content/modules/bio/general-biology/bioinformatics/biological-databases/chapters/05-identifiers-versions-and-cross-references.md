Biological databases give their records identifiers, and an analysis depends on those identifiers to link its data to the databases. The identifiers are not all of the same kind. An accession number identifies a sequence record, a versioned identifier adds the revision of that record, and a gene identifier names a gene in a particular annotation. The different kinds are related but not interchangeable, and treating them as the same leads to errors that are difficult to detect.

A gene symbol, such as a short abbreviation of a gene name, is the identifier that most people recognise, and it is the least reliable. Symbols are not unique across species, they are sometimes renamed as knowledge changes, and some are the same as the names of other things. A list of genes given by symbol may therefore contain errors, and the mapping must be checked. Benson et al. (2012) described the archive that holds the sequences from which the gene identities are derived, and the accession is the more stable reference.

Stable identifiers are assigned to gene models by the annotation projects. Cunningham et al. (2022) describe how Ensembl gives each gene and transcript a stable identifier that is retained across releases where the model is unchanged, which allows an analysis to refer to a gene unambiguously. The identifier must still be recorded with its release, since a model that has changed may keep its identifier while its structure differs.

The mapping between identifiers from different databases is itself a dataset. It changes as the databases are revised, and it may map one identifier to several or none. The mapping should be generated by a documented procedure, recorded with the date and the version of each database, and the number of identifiers that failed to map should be reported.

Wilkinson et al. (2016) recommended that data be identified with persistent identifiers that are globally unique, and a mapping that records its sources supports that recommendation in practice.
