The Protein Data Bank is the archive of experimentally determined structures of proteins, nucleic acids and their complexes. Berman (2000) describes the archive as a single global resource for these structures, and each entry holds the coordinates of the atoms together with the information about how they were determined. This topic shows how the archive is used in practice: how a structure is chosen, how its file is read, how its quality is judged and how it is recorded.

The chapters follow the work of a structural analysis. They start with what an entry contains and how to search for one. They then cover the reading of a coordinate file, the quality indicators that tell a reader how far the model can be trusted, the handling of ligands and waters, and the use of predicted models next to experimental ones. The last chapters address the checks before use and the citation of the structure.

The topic draws on the structural methods of the General Biology level, which describe how the structures are determined. Here the emphasis is on the choices an analyst makes with a structure already in hand, and on the errors that follow when those choices are not checked.
