Metagenomics is applied wherever a community of microorganisms matters. In environmental science it describes soils, oceans and the sediments that carry the carbon and nitrogen cycles. In medicine it describes the microbiome of the gut, the skin and the airway, and it detects the pathogens and the resistance genes in a clinical sample without the need to culture them. In industry it guides the design of fermentation and waste-treatment processes, where the community determines the outcome.

Each application asks a different question, and the reliability of the answer depends on the question. A profile of which organisms are present answers one question, while the function of a community in a process answers another, and the evidence needed for the second is greater. Alberts et al. (2022) describe how the activities of cells depend on the genes they express, and a metagenome that shows a gene is present does not show that the gene is active.

Clinical applications carry the highest stakes. Detecting a pathogen or a resistance gene in a sample is a claim that may guide treatment, and it must meet a standard of accuracy that includes the controls and the thresholds described earlier. A positive detection from a sample with contamination or from a low-depth run may be false, and a negative detection from a sample with a high host load may be missed. Ioannidis (2005) described how results that are selected or that depend on flexible analysis can be less reliable than they appear, and clinical use requires validation against an independent standard.

Environmental applications are often comparative, tracking how a community changes over time or across a gradient. The comparison is valid only if the methods are constant, as the previous chapter set out, and a change in the profile may reflect a change in the method rather than in the environment.

Quince et al. (2017) describe the analysis from sampling to result, and the application determines which steps must be controlled most strictly. The question should be stated first, and the analysis designed to answer it.
