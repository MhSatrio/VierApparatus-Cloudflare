The National Center for Biotechnology Information is part of the United States National Library of Medicine. Its resources are a set of linked databases rather than a single archive, and Sayers et al. (2022) describe the collection as a whole, with its nucleotide, protein, genome, gene, structure, taxonomy and literature resources. Most of these share a search system and a common set of identifiers, so a query in one database can lead to records in another.

GenBank is the nucleotide sequence part of the collection. Benson et al. (2012) describe its content as the sequences deposited by researchers and exchanged with the other members of the international sequence collaboration. The records are the primary data behind most sequence work, but they carry the annotation of the submitter, and the other NCBI resources add computed and curated information on top of them.

A researcher chooses the database according to the question. A question about the sequence of a gene in one species points to the nucleotide database. A question about a protein's domains or variants points to the protein database. A question about which species have been sequenced points to the genome resources, and a question about the name or relationships of an organism points to the taxonomy database.

The choice matters because the same organism may appear in several resources with different identifiers and different levels of annotation. An analysis should state which database was searched, why it was chosen and which version of its records was used.
