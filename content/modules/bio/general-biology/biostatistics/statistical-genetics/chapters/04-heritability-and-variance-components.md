The phenotypic variance of a trait in a population is the sum of its genetic and environmental components, and the genetic component may be further divided into additive effects, dominance and interactions between genes. Heritability is the proportion of the phenotypic variance that is attributable to genetic differences. Broad-sense heritability includes all genetic components, and narrow-sense heritability includes only the additive component, which is the part that resembles a parent to offspring and determines the response to selection.

The components are estimated from the resemblance among relatives or from the genotypes of unrelated individuals. Twin and family designs compare the similarity of relatives with different degrees of shared genes, and the difference in their similarity indicates the genetic contribution. Mixed models estimate the variance components directly, using the relationship among individuals derived from genotypes, and this approach allows heritability to be estimated from unrelated people. Visscher et al. (2017) described how these estimates have been used to show that many traits are highly polygenic.

Heritability describes a population at a given time. It is a property of the variance in that population, and it changes when the environmental variation changes or when the allele frequencies alter. A heritability of 0.8 does not mean that 80 per cent of an individual's trait is caused by genes, because the genetic and environmental effects interact and the share of an individual's trait is not defined by the variance. Ioannidis (2005) warned that a summary statistic can be misread when its scope is forgotten, and the scope of heritability should be stated with the estimate.

Cumming (2014) argues that the estimate of heritability should be reported with its interval, since variance components are often estimated with wide intervals in modest samples. The population, the environments represented and the model used should be recorded, so that an estimate from one population is not carried to another without comment.
