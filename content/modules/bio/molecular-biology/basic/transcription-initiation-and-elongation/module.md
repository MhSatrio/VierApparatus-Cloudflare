## Overview

Every gene is first copied into RNA before it can be used. This copying step, called transcription, is carried out by a large enzyme called RNA polymerase. In this module you will follow one transcription event from start to finish. You will see how the enzyme finds the right place on the DNA, how it opens the double helix, how it makes the first bonds of a new RNA chain, and how it keeps adding nucleotides as it moves along the gene.

The module is written for readers who know the basic structure of DNA and the idea of a gene, and who are ready to meet the names of the main players. Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, followed by a plain explanation. If a term feels unfamiliar, the explanation right after it is enough for the rest of the module.

The eight chapters go in order. The first explains what transcription is and why cells need it. The second covers promoters, the DNA signals where transcription starts. The third introduces the bacterial enzyme and its sigma factor, and the fourth describes how the first steps are taken. The fifth turns to the eukaryotic machinery and the role of chromatin. The sixth and seventh cover elongation and pausing, and the eighth covers the end of transcription and brings the picture together.

Readers who want to go further will find the reference list useful. The papers listed there are review articles and classic studies, chosen because they explain the mechanisms in depth and are easy to find.
