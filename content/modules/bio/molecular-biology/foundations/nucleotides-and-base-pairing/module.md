## Overview

The two strands of DNA are held together by base pairs, and the pairing rules are the reason DNA can store information and be copied accurately. This module looks closely at the bases themselves, at how they pair and at the forces that hold the pairs in place. It also shows how these rules are used in the cell and in the laboratory.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module assumes that you have read the DNA Structure module, or that you already know the three parts of a nucleotide.

The eight chapters move from the bases to their use. The first introduces the four bases. The second separates purines from pyrimidines. The third states the pairing rules, and the fourth explains hydrogen bonds. The fifth describes base stacking, and the sixth looks at mismatches. The seventh and eighth show the pairing rules at work in copying, reading and laboratory methods.
