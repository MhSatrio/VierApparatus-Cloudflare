## Overview

A newly made protein is a floppy chain. To work, it must fold into a precise three-dimensional shape, and it must do so quickly and reliably inside a crowded cell. Folding is one of the central problems of molecular biology, and when it goes wrong, the results can be serious. This module explains what folding is, how it is guided and what happens when it fails.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module builds on the Protein Shape Basics module.

Readers should finish this module able to explain the main ideas in their own words. The chapters move from the basic problem, to the forces that act on a chain, the energy landscape, intermediate states and chaperones, and then to misfolding, disease and the laboratory methods used to study folding. Each chapter ends with a summary, and the references at the front of the module point to the original studies.

The eight chapters trace the story of folding. The first defines the problem. The second describes the forces at work. The third and fourth cover the energy landscape and the idea of intermediate states. The fifth introduces chaperones, the sixth covers misfolding and the seventh describes disease. The eighth reviews how folding is studied.
