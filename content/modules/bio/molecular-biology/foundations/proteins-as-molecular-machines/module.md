## Overview

Some proteins are more than catalysts or structural parts. They work as machines, moving through a series of shapes that carry out a task. A motor protein walks along a track, a ribosome assembles a chain, a chaperone folds other proteins and a pump moves molecules across a membrane. These machines run on chemical energy, usually from the breakdown of the energy carrier ATP, and their movements are controlled with great precision.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module assumes that you have read the module on enzymes.

The eight chapters explore protein machines in order. The first defines what a molecular machine is. The second covers how shape changes are powered. The third to fifth describe motors, the ribosome and chaperones. The sixth introduces pumps and transporters, the seventh looks at machines that copy and assemble, and the eighth explains how such machines are studied.
