## Overview

A protein is a chain of amino acids, but it does not work as a straight line. It folds into a precise three-dimensional shape, and that shape is what allows it to catalyze reactions, bind partners and carry signals. Biologists describe protein shape at four levels, from the simple sequence to the complete folded form. This module introduces those levels and explains why shape is so closely tied to function.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module assumes that you have read the modules on amino acids and peptide bonds.

The eight chapters move through the levels of structure. The first describes the primary structure, the sequence. The second and third cover the regular local folds, helices and sheets. The fourth introduces tertiary structure, the complete fold of one chain. The fifth covers quaternary structure, where several chains come together. The sixth and seventh show how shape relates to function and how shape is determined, and the eighth describes how shapes are studied.
