## Overview

Almost every chemical reaction in a living cell is sped up by an enzyme. Enzymes are mostly proteins, and each one is specialized for a particular reaction or a small group of related reactions. Without them, most reactions would be far too slow to sustain life. This module explains what enzymes do, how their shape allows them to work and how cells control their activity.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module assumes that you know the basic structure of proteins.

The eight chapters follow the story of an enzyme from its basic role to its control. The first describes what a catalyst is. The second and third cover the active site and how substrates bind to it. The fourth explains how enzymes lower the energy needed for reactions, and the fifth describes specificity. The sixth covers rates and saturation, the seventh introduces inhibition and the eighth describes control of enzymes in the cell.
