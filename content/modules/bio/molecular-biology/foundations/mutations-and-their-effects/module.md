## Overview

DNA is copied with remarkable accuracy, but no copy is perfect. Occasionally a base is replaced, a segment is lost or a stretch is duplicated. These changes are called mutations, and they are the raw material of genetic variation. Most mutations have little or no effect, some are harmful and a few have helped populations adapt. This module explains what mutations are, how often they arise, how they change genes and proteins, and what they mean for disease and evolution.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module assumes that you know the structure of DNA and the basic idea of a gene.

The eight chapters move from definitions to consequences. The first chapter defines a mutation, and the second describes the main types. The third discusses mutation rates, and the fourth separates spontaneous from induced mutations. The fifth looks at effects on protein function, the sixth at mutations in disease and the seventh at mutations in cancer. The eighth describes the role of mutation in evolution.
