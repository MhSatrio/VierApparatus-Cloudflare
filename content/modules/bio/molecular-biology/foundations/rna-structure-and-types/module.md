## Overview

RNA is often described as the "cousin" of DNA. The two molecules are built from similar parts, and they share many features, but RNA is far more versatile. Some RNA molecules carry messages from genes to the protein factories of the cell. Others help build those factories, act as adaptors during protein assembly, or switch genes on and off. In recent decades, scientists have also discovered many RNAs with roles that were not expected. This module introduces the chemistry of RNA, the shapes it can take, and the main types that cells make.

The module assumes that you have read the DNA Structure module, or that you already know the basic parts of a nucleotide: a sugar, a phosphate and a base. Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, followed by a plain explanation. Where a term has both a common and a scientific meaning, the scientific meaning is used here.

The eight chapters follow a clear path. The first chapter compares RNA and DNA directly. The second looks at the sugar and the base uracil. The third and fourth describe messenger RNA and transfer RNA, the two most familiar kinds. The fifth describes ribosomal RNA and the sixth covers small regulatory RNAs. The seventh introduces long noncoding RNAs, and the eighth explains how RNA folds into its three-dimensional shape.
