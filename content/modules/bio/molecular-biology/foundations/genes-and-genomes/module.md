## Overview

Every living thing carries a set of instructions written in DNA, and the complete set is called its genome. Within the genome, the instructions are divided into genes, each of which holds the information needed for a particular product, usually a protein or an RNA. This module explains the words gene and genome, how the genome is organized, how it differs across life and what the sequencing of the human genome taught us.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module assumes that you know the structure of DNA and the basic idea of chromosomes, which are covered in earlier modules.

The eight chapters move from definitions to applications. The first chapter asks what a gene is, and the second describes the genome as a whole. The third compares genome sizes across organisms, and the fourth distinguishes coding and noncoding DNA. The fifth looks at regulatory sequences, and the sixth at repetitive DNA and transposons. The seventh describes the sequencing of the human genome, and the eighth explains how genomes are used in research and medicine.
