## Overview

DNA is the molecule that carries the instructions for building and running a living cell. Its shape is simple enough to draw on a sheet of paper, yet it explains how genetic information is stored, copied and passed to the next generation. This module introduces the structure of DNA from the ground up. You will meet its building blocks, the way they link into strands, and the double helix that the two strands form together.

Nothing here requires advanced chemistry. Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, followed by a short explanation. When a term has a common everyday meaning as well as a scientific one, the scientific meaning is the one used in this module.

The eight chapters follow a sensible order. The first describes the discovery of the double helix. The second and third look at the parts of a nucleotide and how the bases pair. The fourth explains why the strands run in opposite directions. The fifth describes the grooves and twist of the helix, and the sixth explains what holds the helix stable and how the strands separate. The seventh introduces DNA packaging, and the eighth describes how scientists measure and image DNA in the laboratory.
