## Overview

The central dogma is one of the most famous ideas in biology. It describes, in a single sentence, how the instructions stored in DNA are turned into the proteins that do most of the work in a cell. Its name sounds dramatic, but the idea is simply a map of the main route of information. This module explains that map, what each step involves, and why the idea has been refined as research has advanced.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, followed by a plain explanation. The module assumes that you know the basic structure of DNA and RNA, which are covered in the two earlier modules. You do not need any advanced chemistry.

The eight chapters move from history to practice. The first chapter explains where the idea came from. The second describes the flow of information as a whole. The third and fourth cover the two central steps, transcription and translation. The fifth introduces reverse transcription, an important exception. The sixth describes the many roles of RNA beyond messages, and the seventh shows how the dogma is used in modern research. The eighth warns against common misunderstandings.
