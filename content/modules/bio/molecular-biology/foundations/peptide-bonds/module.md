## Overview

Amino acids are linked into chains by a single kind of bond, the peptide bond. The peptide bond is the link that makes a protein a chain rather than a loose heap of molecules, and its chemistry shapes the way every protein folds. This module explains how the bond is formed, why it behaves as a rigid unit and how cells build millions of these bonds during protein synthesis.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. The module assumes you have read the Amino Acids module.

The eight chapters move from the chemistry of the bond to its biology. The first chapter describes how the bond forms, and the second explains why it is rigid and flat. The third and fourth look at the backbone it creates and the way the bond is broken. The fifth describes how the ribosome makes bonds, and the sixth covers the reading of the genetic code. The seventh and eighth look at errors and at the control of peptide bond formation.
