## Overview

Proteins are built from amino acids, small molecules that link together in long chains. There are twenty amino acids that cells use to make proteins, and together they give proteins their enormous range of shapes and functions. This module introduces the amino acids: their shared structure, their main groups and the way their side chains determine how a protein behaves.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, with a plain explanation. No advanced chemistry is needed, although a little comfort with the idea of charges and water helps.

The eight chapters move from the basic structure to the families of side chains and their use. The first chapter introduces the shared structure. The second and third describe the side chains that are water-loving and water-avoiding. The fourth looks at charged amino acids, the fifth at special cases and the sixth at how properties are measured. The seventh and eighth show how the properties shape proteins and how cells control them.
