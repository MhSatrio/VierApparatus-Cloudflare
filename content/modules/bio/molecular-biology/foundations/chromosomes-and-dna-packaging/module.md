## Overview

A human cell contains about two meters of DNA, yet the nucleus that holds it is only a few micrometers across. The DNA must therefore be folded, wrapped and organized so that it fits, and so that the right parts can still be reached when a cell needs them. This module explains how that packaging works, from the first unit of wrapping to the compact chromosomes that appear when a cell divides.

Each important term appears in quotation marks the first time it is used, followed by a plain explanation. The module builds on the DNA Structure module, especially on the idea that DNA carries negative charges along its backbone.

The eight chapters move from the question of why packaging is needed, through the nucleosome and its histone proteins, to the higher levels of folding, the chromosome ends and the ways scientists study chromosomes today.
